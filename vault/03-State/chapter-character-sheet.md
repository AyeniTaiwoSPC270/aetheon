**CHAPTER CHARACTER SHEET**

*Per-Chapter Writing Reference Template*

Generate one of these during /plan before every chapter is written.

> *PURPOSE: This document tells Claude exactly who is in the chapter,
> what state they are in, what they want, and what function they serve —
> before a single word of prose is written. It prevents character
> hallucinations, inconsistencies, and drift across sessions.*

**PART I — HOW TO USE THIS TEMPLATE**

**The /plan Phase Workflow**

Before writing any chapter, the author runs /plan. During /plan, Claude
must:

1.  Read the chapter brief provided by the author.

2.  Identify every character who appears in the chapter — including
    cameos and background figures with names.

3.  Cross-reference the Character Bible to confirm current status,
    personality, and known information for each character.

4.  Fill out this Chapter Character Sheet for the chapter.

5.  Present the completed sheet to the author for approval before
    writing.

6.  Only proceed to /write after the author confirms the sheet is
    correct.

**The Four Character Tiers**

Every character in a chapter falls into one of four tiers. Identify the
tier before filling out their block.

**TIER 1 — POV CHARACTER:** The character whose perspective the chapter
is written from. Full block required. Maximum depth.

**TIER 2 — ACTIVE SUPPORTING:** Characters with dialogue, decisions, or
emotional beats in the chapter. Full block required.

**TIER 3 — PRESENT BUT PASSIVE:** Characters physically present but
without significant dialogue or decisions. Short block — state and
function only.

**TIER 4 — CAMEO / MENTIONED:** Characters referenced by name but not
physically present. One line only — what is said about them and by whom.

**Hard Rules**

**⚠ Never invent a character's emotional state. It must follow from the
Character Bible and the chapter's position in the story.**

**⚠ Never give a character information they do not have yet. Track what
each character knows carefully.**

**⚠ Never add a new named character to a chapter without flagging it to
the author first.**

**⚠ If a character's behaviour in the chapter would contradict their
Bible entry, flag it before writing — do not override it silently.**

**⚠ Do not skip the sheet for 'simple' chapters. Every chapter has
characters. Every character needs a sheet.**

**✓** The sheet is also a continuity check — if filling it out surfaces
a contradiction with the Character Bible, that is the system working
correctly.

**✓** Short chapters need shorter sheets. The depth of each block should
match the character's role in that specific chapter.

**✓** A character's exit from the chapter is as important as their
entrance. Note it explicitly.

**PART III — WORKED EXAMPLE**

> *This is a sample filled sheet for Chapter 3 of Arc 3, Saga 1 — the
> academy entrance exam day. Use this as a reference for how to fill the
> template correctly.*

**CHAPTER HEADER — EXAMPLE**

**Saga:** Saga 1 — Origin & Awakening

**Arc:** Arc 3 — The Academy Application

**Chapter Number:** Chapter 3

**Chapter Working Title:** The Weight of a Number

**Primary Setting:** The Academy entrance grounds — exam hall and
outdoor combat arena

**Time of Day / Season / Conditions:** Early morning to late afternoon.
Late summer. Bright, hot, uncomfortable.

**POV Character(s):** Aldric Vane (primary). Brief Daran POV at chapter
close.

**Target Word Count:** 2,500–3,000 words

**Chapter's Primary Purpose:** The academy exam. Aldric passes. Daran
fails. The promise breaks.

**Emotional Tone:** Nervous energy → triumph → gut-punch. The chapter
ends heavy.

**How This Chapter Ends:** Emotional gut-punch — Daran's face as
Aldric's name is called and his is not.

**CHAPTER BRIEF SUMMARY — EXAMPLE**

Aldric and Daran take the three-stage academy entrance exam together.
Written stage — both pass. Practical magic stage — Aldric's mana output
surprises the examiners; Daran's is below threshold. Combat stage —
Aldric performs well. Results are posted. Aldric's name appears. Daran's
does not. The chapter closes on Daran's face — not crying, not shouting.
Just still. The chapter does not show what Daran decides. It only shows
the moment before.

**TIER 1 — POV CHARACTER — EXAMPLE**

**▸ ALDRIC VANE** *— POV — Primary*

> **Emotional state:** Surface: nervous energy masked with humor.
> Underneath: terrified he will fail and leave Daran behind.
>
> **Physical state:** Rested but fidgety. He slept badly. He is wearing
> his best clothes which are still clearly commoner clothes.
>
> **What he WANTS:** To pass. To pass alongside Daran. Both of them,
> together.
>
> **What he FEARS:** That he passes and Daran doesn't. He has not let
> himself think this thought directly.
>
> **What he knows / doesn't know:** Does not know his mana output is
> unusual. Does not know Daran's mana channel is below academy
> threshold.
>
> **His role this chapter:** POV anchor. His triumph is hollow because
> of what it costs. His humor appears early and disappears by the end.
>
> **Consistency checks:** White hair, red eyes. Adaptable and quietly
> silly in tense moments. Commoner mannerisms — does not bow naturally
> to authority figures.
>
> **How he exits:** Name is called. He looks for Daran before he
> celebrates. Daran is already gone.

**TIER 2 — ACTIVE SUPPORTING — EXAMPLE**

**▸ DARAN** *— Active Supporting — Chapter's emotional core*

> **Emotional state:** Controlled. He has told himself he will pass. He
> has prepared more than anyone. He is not considering failure.
>
> **Physical state:** Sharp, composed. He looks more prepared than
> Aldric. This is deliberate.
>
> **What he WANTS:** To pass. To prove that effort beats talent.
>
> **What he FEARS:** That effort is not enough. He will not name this
> fear.
>
> **What he knows / doesn't know:** Does not know his mana channel
> assessment will disqualify him. Does not know this is coming.
>
> **His role this chapter:** The chapter's tragedy engine. His arc
> begins here. His final moment — still, silent, gone — is the chapter's
> last image.
>
> **Consistency checks:** Dark-haired. Always the harder worker. Pride
> is his armour. He does not cry in front of people.
>
> **How he exits:** His name is not called. He is present for one beat —
> Aldric looks for him. He is already leaving. No words.

**TIER 3 — PRESENT BUT PASSIVE — EXAMPLE**

**▸ EXAM PROCTOR:** Present and functional. Delivers exam instructions.
Reads results. Neutral — do not give personality.

**▸ OTHER APPLICANTS:** Background presence. Two noble applicants who
sneer at Aldric's clothes. Do not name them yet.

**TIER 4 — CAMEOS & MENTIONS — EXAMPLE**

**▸ Maren & Josse Vane:** Mentioned by Aldric internally — he thinks of
them when his name is called. Do not show them physically.

**PART IV — PHASE COMMAND REFERENCE**

These are the commands used in the writing workflow. Paste this into
your Claude project instructions.

**/plan — Planning Phase**

> *Author provides: chapter number, arc, saga, brief description of what
> happens, and any specific instructions. Claude must: recap the
> previous chapter (3–5 sentences), generate the Chapter Character
> Sheet, outline the chapter beat by beat, and present both to the
> author for approval before writing anything.*

**/write — Writing Phase**

> *Triggered only after the author approves the /plan output. Claude
> must: write the full chapter in prose, stay within the approved
> outline and character sheet, maintain the approved POV, hit the target
> word count, and end the chapter exactly as specified in the sheet.*

**/review — Review Phase**

> *Claude audits the completed chapter against: — The Chapter Character
> Sheet (did every character behave correctly?) — The Character Bible
> (any contradictions with established traits?) — The Master Plan (does
> this chapter serve its arc and saga correctly?) — The Power System
> Bible (any magic use that violates established rules?) Claude flags
> issues clearly. It does not silently fix them.*

**/rewrite \[note\] — Rewrite Phase**

> *Author provides specific rewrite instructions after /review. Claude
> rewrites only the flagged sections — not the entire chapter unless
> instructed. It explains what it changed and why.*

**/update-bible — Bible Update Phase**

> *Used after a chapter introduces new named characters, reveals new
> information, or changes a character's status. Claude flags what needs
> updating in the Character Bible and presents the proposed additions to
> the author for approval before anything is treated as canon.*
