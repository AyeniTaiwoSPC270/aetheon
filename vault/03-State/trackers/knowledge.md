## Aldric
- **Knows (through Ch. 41, unchanged):** The pressure sensation and the Ashford burst. Daran saw it. Solen read his weight shift. Maret's notebook pause. His first bolt was the tightest in the yard. Doven's warmth. The Ch. 18 burst and its shape matching Ashford. The night-yard failure and the warden's "not the only name." The window-watcher (Varen, named Ch. 31). Lirien's frost leak (Ch. 22) and her Ch. 28 diagnosis and eleven-second threshold theory. Crest's redirected attention to Rynn (Ch. 23-25) and what it cost to fix it. The third burst (Ch. 26) with no trigger. Torval's stand against Crest (Ch. 29) and his circuit-density resolution (Ch. 39). Davan's scorch mystery (Ch. 30, unsolved). Sable's warning about Varen (Ch. 32) and Dross's echoing line (Ch. 33). The tournament bracket (Ch. 37, seeded 4th) and the dismissive noble. Corwin Bale (Ch. 38) and the mid-fight stillness Solen flagged. Rynn's loss to Torval and the low-grade dread it left (Ch. 39). Crest's silent win and wordless look (Ch. 40). Solen's unprompted scouting of Tavish Ardell and the Final prediction (Ch. 41).
- **Ch. 42:** Faces Torval in the semifinal — the tournament's strongest, most controlled fighter, who tells him plainly "I don't do that anymore [go easy]. You'll understand why by the end of this," meaning Rynn. Under real sustained pressure, his control slips worse than at any point since Ch. 26; he fights it back down at real, visible cost (shaking hands, a failed shell) and wins on an unconventional low-angle Mana Bolt aimed at the ground rather than at Torval directly. Learns Crest lost his own semifinal to Tavish Ardell, off-page, an hour earlier — and finds unexpected sympathy rather than the old rivalry-coldness. Notices an unrecognized, unhurried man in the Noble Council tier watching him directly with open interest, who does not look away first and inclines his head slightly before returning to the match. Does not know who he is.
- **Ch. 43:** Seeks quiet before the Final and finds Essa already in the library cut-through. Learns, for the first time, what her Ch. 17 line meant: her own "first mistake" was a wrongly-answered classroom question in her first week that the year has never let her live down, and — the part that lands hardest — that his own "first mistake," as far as Greyveil is concerned, was simply arriving looking the way he does (white hair, red eyes, no story), before he'd said or done anything at all. She delivers this as recognition, not encouragement, and leaves before he can ask his own question, cutting him off with "Nobody forgot yours. Neither did I."
- **Ch. 44:** Faces Tavish Ardell in the Final in front of the King. Learns Ardell's water ward (unnamed to Aldric on the page, though the reader/registry knows it as Tideguard) banks every ordinary attack he spends against it and can be released on Ardell's own schedule. Wins by refusing to feed it — then, for the first time in two years, consciously chooses not to suppress the sensation in his chest rather than fighting it down; it arrives as a full, unaimed, chosen discharge that overwhelms the ward. Learns from Ardell's own reaction ("That wasn't a spell... I don't know what it was") that the win came from something outside anything Ardell had prepared for. Wins the tournament. Afterward, notices the unrecognized noble's seat in the Council tier sitting empty — he left sometime during the match, before it ended — and feels a small, specific unease about it that he doesn't examine further.
- **Ch. 45:** At the closing ceremony, learns Torval placed third and is **of House Ashwynne**, an old House assumed extinct for two generations — the entire noble section reacts audibly. Learns Tavish Ardell placed second (House Ardell). Is announced first place, "Aldric Vane" — with no House name read after it, a public gap the entire arena notices, landing with its own silence before the applause. Shakes the King's hand personally; notices the royal heir's open, unguarded curiosity; notices Solm's public composure not slipping, but catches two seconds of something private and warm in his eyes. Hears the herald name "Lord Cassian Veyra" among roughly thirty Council dignitaries — the name means nothing to him consciously, though the text notes it "snagged" at an unplaced memory (Aldric himself does not consciously register this snag as significant). Learns from Solen that Varen exchanged a few words with Sable in the second-year section that neither of them can hear or explain. Notices small, deliberately economical movement at the grounds' edge near the end of the celebration that doesn't belong to the ordinary bustle of a tournament ending — and dismisses it as tiredness, without mentioning it to Rynn or Solen (though he notices Solen glance at the same spot a moment later).
- **Ch. 46:** During the post-tournament gathering, recognizes disguised attackers (tournament staff uniforms, wrong coordination) moving toward Solm specifically, not the crowd. Places himself between the second attacker and Solm's exposed flank on pure instinct, throws one plain Mana Bolt at an attacker's foot to buy time, and holds the gap until Bavel and other faculty arrive and repel the attack in under a minute. Confirms Solm is unhurt via a direct, held look. Is told by Solm, gently, "You shouldn't have been here" and "Later, Vane. Not tonight" — and is sent to find Rynn and Solen rather than given any explanation. Does **not** learn who sent the attackers, why Solm was the target, or anything about Solm's private suspicion — none of Solm's Ch. 46 POV content is known to Aldric.
- **Ch. 47 (new):** Tries actively to get a real explanation from Bavel, an unnamed administrator, and Nessa Croft (whose own theory — that Solm specifically, not the Council generally, was the target — is the closest thing to real information anyone gives him, and it's speculation, not confirmation). Gets nothing definitive from anyone. Watches the search line work the treeline for the three attackers who fled, and sees one of them stop, turn, and hold a look directly on him — not at Solm, not at the chaos — before vanishing back into the dark. Understands, with the same instinct that once told him a Greyback was already inside the walls, that this was a deliberate message addressed specifically to him, though he doesn't have the rest of the sentence yet. Also registers, prompted by Solen naming it aloud, that his own tournament win has gone almost entirely unfelt, eclipsed by tonight's violence.
- **Ch. 48 (new — Solm's POV, not Aldric's):** Aldric is not on-page in this chapter and learns nothing new here. His knowledge state is unchanged from the close of Ch. 47. He does **not** know that Solm spent that night failing to write an incident report, drafting and then locking away a personal letter about him, or that Solm privately believes the choice of when Aldric learns "the truth" may no longer be entirely his own to make. None of Ch. 48's content is known to Aldric.

- **Ch. 52 (new):** From a hillside, sees an unnamed man taken into custody and an unnamed figure in white armour with iron-grey trim. Does not know who either is, nor the operation's purpose. Writes home to Maren and Josse. Now certain the story "will come looking for him."
