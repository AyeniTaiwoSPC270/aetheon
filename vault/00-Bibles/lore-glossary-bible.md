# **SECTION I — THE HISTORY OF AETHON**

Aethon's history is divided into five named eras. Most ordinary humans
in Valdenmere know the last two eras in rough outline. Elven scholars
know all five in detail. Demi-human oral traditions describe the same
events from perspectives that differ significantly from the written
human record.

> *Writing rule: Characters reference history naturally — through
> idioms, offhand comparisons, and assumptions. A soldier might say
> 'this feels like the Ember War all over again' without explaining what
> the Ember War was. Let historical references breathe. Explain only
> when a character would genuinely explain.*

**THE FIRST AGE — The Age of Roots** *(Unknown duration — before
recorded history)*

> The period before organised kingdoms. Dragonites moved freely across
> all three continents. The other races — elves, demi-humans, goblins —
> existed in small tribal communities. Humans were the youngest race and
> the least established. Magic was not classified or ranked — it was
> simply part of life, used instinctively and passed through direct
> teaching. No written records exist from this period. Elven oral
> histories and Dragonite-adjacent ruins are the only evidence it
> existed as described.

**THE SECOND AGE — The Age of Ordering** *(Approximately 800–1200 years
before the story)*

> Humans began building the first organised kingdoms on Valdris. The
> mana channel and circuit system was formally documented for the first
> time by a human scholar named Drevos — establishing the theoretical
> framework that the modern rank system is built on. The first
> inter-racial treaties were signed between humans and elves. Demi-human
> territories on eastern Valdris were formally recognised. Goblins had a
> functioning nation — the Grath Confederacy — occupying southern
> Valdris. The period ended with the first major human-goblin conflict,
> the causes of which are disputed by every race involved.

**THE THIRD AGE — The Age of Conflict** *(Approximately 400–800 years
before the story)*

> The great wars. Human kingdoms rose and consumed each other. The Grath
> Confederacy was destroyed in what human records call the Southern
> Pacification and goblin oral tradition calls the Burning. The original
> peace treaty after the Burning gave goblins protected status — a fact
> suppressed in subsequent human historical rewrites. The elves withdrew
> significantly from human politics during this period, a decision their
> isolationist faction still defends as correct. The Obsidian Compact —
> the first formal agreement between sword users and mage institutions —
> was signed at the end of this age, establishing that both traditions
> would coexist without one subordinating the other.

**THE FOURTH AGE — The Age of Institutions** *(Approximately 100–400
years before the story)*

> The building period. The Greyveil Academy was founded 340 years before
> the story begins. The modern F-to-S mage ranking system was formally
> adopted by Valdenmere and gradually spread to other kingdoms. The
> goblin indentured servitude system was legally codified — the first
> step toward formal slavery. Demi-human territories on eastern Valdris
> were gradually encroached upon by expanding human settlements,
> creating the border tensions that will erupt in Saga 2. Varek Noss was
> born at the end of this age and began his career as a Phasite
> researcher during the transition to the Fifth Age.

**THE FIFTH AGE — The Age of the Story** *(The present — the 8 sagas)*

> The age in which the story takes place. It begins without a name —
> ages are named in retrospect. Elven historians, watching from a
> distance, have begun calling it the Age of Correction in their private
> records, referencing the Phasite prophecy. Whether that name proves
> accurate depends on what Aldric Vane and the world around him do with
> what they have been given.

## **Key Historical Events Referenced in the Story**

**The Burning**

> The destruction of the Grath Confederacy — the goblin nation — by
> human kingdoms approximately 300 years before the story. Human records
> call it the Southern Pacification. The original peace treaty gave
> goblins protected status. That treaty was rewritten twice. Sera's
> liberation movement invokes the Burning as their foundational
> historical injustice.

**The Obsidian Compact**

> The formal agreement between sword user institutions and mage
> academies, signed approximately 350 years before the story. It
> established that both traditions are legitimate paths of combat and
> that neither may legally suppress the other. Ironmark sword school and
> Greyveil Academy both trace their institutional legitimacy to this
> compact. Varek Noss's faction considers it an incomplete solution — it
> addressed the sword-magic divide but left racial inequality entirely
> untouched.

**The Greyveil Founding**

> 340 years before the story. The academy was founded by seven A-rank
> mages who believed magical education should be standardised. Their
> original charter included a clause guaranteeing admission based on
> ability regardless of birth — a clause that has been quietly ignored
> for most of the institution's history until Principal Solm began
> enforcing it.

**The Phasite Silence**

> The period of approximately 200 years — beginning roughly 250 years
> before the story — during which no new Phasite appeared in the world.
> Elven scholars noted this absence. Varek Noss's research identified it
> as significant — the world was not ready. The appearance of the
> current 11 Phasites in a compressed timeframe is, in his reading, the
> world's correction activating. New canon: with the Calamities defined
> (below), the Silence now reads as a period with no rising Calamity
> threat — the current 11 appearing together suggests one is stirring
> again.

**The Sealing of Vherith, the Hollow King** *— new canon, NEW_CANON.docx
Section D*

> A great demon-type Calamity (see "Calamity," Section II), SEALED — not
> killed — by the 7 Great Powers (see World Bible) in the deep past,
> proposed placement First Age, before recorded history, pending
> confirmation. Each of the 7 Great Powers wielded one of the 7 legendary
> weapons (see Power System Bible, Section XI) to accomplish the
> sealing — Mournblade denied him regeneration, the Composite Sword
> began unmaking his nature, Hollowfang stripped his reserves,
> Duskmere carved the prison itself, the Ember-Bound Chain physically
> bound him, and the Unnamed One anchored the whole working shut. The 7
> Great Powers no longer exist today; their nature and disappearance are
> open threads. The current state and stability of the seal is
> deliberately left open as a long-game hook.
>
> *NAMING NOTE: an earlier working name for this figure was explicitly
> rejected by the author as breaking world-internal naming conventions.
> "Vherith, the Hollow King" is the confirmed replacement — do not
> revert.*
>
> *OPEN: what the 7 Great Powers actually were and why none exist
> today. Do not resolve without author instruction.*

# **SECTION II — MAGICAL TERMINOLOGY & NAMED SPELLS**

Magical terminology in Aethon operates on two levels: formal spell names
used for common, teachable techniques, and technical descriptive
language used for advanced or unique abilities. Common spells have names
that mages call out or reference in academic contexts. Advanced
techniques — especially Phasite abilities — are described technically
because they have no established names in the literature.

> *Writing rule: Named spells can be called out in combat naturally.
> Technical descriptions should feel precise and grounded — not poetic.
> A mage describing advanced circuit manipulation speaks like a surgeon
> describing a procedure, not a wizard casting a fantasy spell.*

## **Core Magical Terminology**

**Calamity** *— new canon, NEW_CANON.docx Section D*

> A classification parallel in structure to the 11 Phasites but
> distinct: an entity or being that, if it goes rogue or uncontrolled,
> is capable of potentially destroying the world. There are exactly 3,
> fixed the same way the Phasite count of 11 is fixed — Vampirica, one
> specific (unnamed) Dragonite, and Vherith, the Hollow King (see "The
> Sealing of Vherith," Section I). The 11 Phasites were created
> specifically to combat the Calamities should any go rogue. Full
> mechanics: Power System Bible, Section X. Do not invent a fourth
> Calamity without author instruction.

**Mana Weave**

> The process of shaping raw mana output into a structured spell. All
> casting is technically mana weaving — the term is used academically
> and by trained mages. Commoners just say 'casting'.

**Circuit Burn**

> Overuse injury to the mana circuits. Manifests as pain, heat, and in
> severe cases visible redness along circuit pathways under the skin.
> Aldric experiences this regularly when using dual sword-and-magic
> simultaneously.

**Channel Depth**

> The measured capacity of a mage's mana channel. Assessed during
> ranking. Referenced when mages discuss each other's raw power — 'he
> has remarkable channel depth' means he can sustain high output longer
> than average.

**Mana Signature**

> The unique pattern of a mage's mana output — detectable by Mana Sense.
> Like a fingerprint. S-class mages can identify individuals by
> signature at significant distance. Phasites have signatures that are
> structurally unlike any regular mage's.

**Circuit Threading**

> The advanced technique of directing mana through specific circuit
> pathways to achieve precision effects. Solen's primary method.
> Requires exceptional circuit density and fine motor control of mana
> flow.

**Void Casting**

> Casting while in mana Stage 3 or beyond — drawing on reserves the body
> cannot safely provide. Dangerous. Occasionally the only option. The
> term is used by military mages as both a technique reference and a
> grim acknowledgment of what it costs.

**Ki Bloom**

> The visible manifestation of concentrated Ki output — a faint shimmer
> or distortion around a sword user at high exertion. Daran's Ki Bloom
> by Saga 5 is described as dark-edged — like a heat shimmer that
> absorbs light rather than refracts it.

**Resonance Break**

> When a mage's mana output accidentally synchronises with an object or
> structure's natural frequency, causing it to vibrate and potentially
> shatter. A known hazard of fire and sound-adjacent magic near glass or
> stone.

**Force Lattice**

> Aldric's term — coined by academy theory faculty to describe the
> invisible structural web of force manipulation he maintains around
> himself at moderate exertion. Not yet in formal literature at Saga 1.
> By Saga 6 it appears in Varek's research notes.

## **Named Spells — Common Academy Curriculum**

**Mana Bolt** *\[Universal Offensive\]*

> **Effect:** Raw mana compressed and projected as a high-velocity
> impact. The first offensive technique taught at every academy. Low
> precision, high accessibility. Effective up to C rank. Above C rank,
> specialists move to more refined techniques.
>
> **Cost / Limit:** Low mana cost. Unlimited uses within channel
> capacity. Accuracy degrades at range beyond 40 metres.

**Mana Shell** *\[Universal Defensive\]*

> **Effect:** A thin barrier of hardened mana around the caster's body
> or a small area. The standard defence technique. Quality depends
> entirely on the caster's circuit density — a sparse circuit mage
> produces a brittle shell, a dense circuit mage produces something that
> can stop a sword strike.
>
> **Cost / Limit:** Continuous mana draw while maintained. A shell under
> sustained attack drains the channel rapidly.

**Ignis Lance** *\[Fire — Offensive\]*

> **Effect:** A concentrated beam of fire magic. Higher precision than a
> Mana Bolt. Standard mid-range offensive technique for fire-affinity
> mages. Rynn learns this in his first academy year and immediately
> starts trying to make it bigger than recommended.
>
> **Cost / Limit:** Moderate mana cost per use. Charging time of 2–3
> seconds for full power. Can be fired faster at reduced output.

**Frost Lattice** *\[Ice/Water — Area Control\]*

> **Effect:** A spreading layer of ice or frost across a surface or
> through the air. Used for area denial, footing disruption, and as a
> setup for follow-up attacks. Lirien's preferred control technique in
> early sagas.
>
> **Cost / Limit:** Low-to-moderate cost. Effect persists without
> continuous mana draw once established. Cannot be maintained in
> high-heat environments.

**Sever Strike** *\[Ki — Blade Technique\]*

> **Effect:** A Ki-enhanced sword strike specifically directed at a
> mage's circuit pathway. The intent is to temporarily disrupt or
> permanently damage the circuit at the point of contact. Requires
> Silver-level Ki control minimum. Obsidian-level practitioners can
> target specific circuits with surgical precision.
>
> **Cost / Limit:** Requires physical contact with the target. High Ki
> expenditure per strike. Cannot be used in rapid succession.

**Pressure Field** *\[Force — Aldric's Technique\]*

> **Effect:** Aldric's most controlled force application — a sustained
> zone of increased gravitational pressure around a target or area. At
> low output it is slowing and disorienting. At high output it pins
> targets to the ground. At maximum output it is lethal.
>
> **Cost / Limit:** Continuous mana draw scaled to intensity. Line of
> sight required. Multiple simultaneous targets multiply the cost
> exponentially.

**Kinetic Reflect** *\[Force — Aldric's Technique\]*

> **Effect:** Aldric redirects the kinetic energy of an incoming attack
> — a spell, a projectile, a physical strike — back toward its source or
> in a chosen direction. Not a block. A redirection. The force of the
> original attack determines the mana cost of the reflect.
>
> **Cost / Limit:** Reactive — must be triggered in response to an
> attack. The larger the incoming force the greater the mana cost.
> Cannot reflect attacks he cannot see.

**Gravity Spike** *\[Force — Aldric's Technique, Stage 2+\]*

> **Effect:** A concentrated point of massively increased gravity
> applied to a single target or small area. Crushes rather than pushes.
> At Stage 2 mastery it immobilises. At Stage 3 mastery it can collapse
> structures.
>
> **Cost / Limit:** High mana cost. Cannot be sustained for more than a
> few seconds at full intensity without Stage 3 burnout risk. Line of
> sight required.

# **SECTION III — THE RELIGIOUS FRAMEWORK OF AETHON**

Aethon does not have one dominant religion. It has three major spiritual
traditions that coexist, overlap, and occasionally conflict. The story's
sword-vs-magic theme is echoed in its religious landscape — two of the
three traditions are built around that divide. Religion is present in
the background of the world: in oaths characters swear, in ceremonies,
in the language people reach for when they face something beyond
explanation. It is not a plot engine. It is texture.

## **The Dual Path — Human Dominant Faith**

The most widespread human spiritual tradition. It holds that the world
was shaped by two equal and opposing forces — the Forge and the Current.
The Forge represents physical mastery, endurance, craft, and the sword.
The Current represents magical flow, knowledge, transformation, and the
arcane. Neither is superior. Both are sacred. The highest human ideal is
the person who walks both paths — which is why Aldric's existence
carries religious weight for some characters long before it carries
political weight.

- Practitioners swear oaths 'by Forge and Current' — the most serious
  oath a human character can make.

- Temples to the Dual Path have two wings: a sparring hall and a study
  chamber. Services involve both physical training and scholarly
  discussion.

- The religion's clergy are called Walkers. A Walker who can both fight
  and cast — even minimally — is considered blessed. Most cannot.

- The Dual Path does not have gods in the conventional sense. The Forge
  and the Current are forces, not persons. Prayers are directed toward
  the force you are invoking, not a deity who might answer.

- Noble corruption of the Dual Path is a background detail — in wealthy
  districts, Dual Path temples have become social institutions where
  actual practice is secondary to status display. Aldric's village
  temple is the unglamorous, genuine version.

## **The Ember — Goblin Spiritual Tradition**

Documented in the World Bible. Expanded here for writing reference.

The Ember is not a god. It is the self — the irreducible core of a
person that persists through suffering if it refuses to be extinguished.
Goblin spiritual practice is centred on tending the Ember: keeping it
lit through community, memory, story, and refusal to accept that what is
done to you defines what you are.

- The central ritual is called the Telling — a regular gathering where
  community members speak aloud what they have endured and what they
  have refused to become because of it.

- To 'lose the Ember' is the deepest goblin spiritual failure — becoming
  what was done to you, or giving up entirely.

- Sera's strength as a liberation leader comes partly from her Ember
  practice — she has kept hers burning through conditions that
  extinguished others.

- The liberation movement's battle cry — used for the first time in Saga
  7 — is a single word in the goblin language: 'Gratha.' It means 'the
  Ember holds.'

## **The Long Memory — Elven Spiritual Tradition**

Elves do not worship gods. They practice a form of ancestral reverence
combined with what they call the Long Memory — the belief that every
significant event in Aethon's history leaves a permanent impression on
the world's fabric, and that sensitive individuals can learn to read
these impressions. Elven scholars who develop this sensitivity are
called Readers.

- The Phasite prophecy is technically a Reading — an ancient elven
  Reader perceived the pattern of the Phasites' eventual appearance in
  the world's fabric and transcribed it. This is why the elven council
  takes it seriously when Elyn brings evidence that the 11 are
  gathering.

- Elves mark significant life events by adding them to a personal Memory
  Record — a document maintained across their entire lifespan that
  becomes a historical artifact after death.

- The Long Memory tradition holds that the Dragonites are not simply a
  powerful race — they are the world's oldest Readers, perceiving its
  fabric at a depth no other race can approach.

- Elves who convert to the Dual Path exist and are not ostracised — but
  they are quietly considered to have given up something irreplaceable.

# **SECTION IV — LEGENDARY TITLES OF AETHON**

Beyond the formal F-to-S mage rankings and the Blade Titles for sword
users, Aethon has a small set of legendary titles — names given to
individuals who have achieved something historically unprecedented.
These titles are not awarded by institutions. They emerge from
collective acknowledgment — scholars, soldiers, and ordinary people
begin using them and they become real. A title only exists if the world
agrees it is earned.

> *Writing rule: Legendary titles should be introduced slowly and feel
> earned. Characters do not refer to themselves by their legendary
> title. Others use it — sometimes in awe, sometimes as a weapon,
> sometimes simply as identification. Aldric never calls himself the
> First Blade.*

## **Existing Legendary Titles — Historical**

**The Unbroken**

> A title given once, approximately 200 years before the story, to a
> sword user named Edras Koll who survived seventeen consecutive duels
> against ranked mages without a single circuit-severing strike landing
> on him. He died at 94, still holding the title. The Obsidian Compact
> was partly inspired by his career. Referenced in sword school
> tradition — Grevan Tusk has a painting of him.

**The Still Voice**

> A title given to an elven Reader approximately 400 years before the
> story who correctly predicted three consecutive inter-kingdom wars
> before they occurred. Her Memory Record is considered the most
> significant historical document in elven scholarly tradition. Elyn
> Dawnveil has read it seventeen times.

**The Ember King**

> A title from goblin oral tradition — not recognised by human kingdoms
> — given to the last leader of the Grath Confederacy before the
> Burning. He refused to sign the revised treaty that would have made
> his people indentured. He was executed. The liberation movement
> considers him their founding martyr.

## **Titles Earned During the Story**

**The First Blade —** Holder: Aldric Vane \| Earned: Saga 8 — after the
final battle \| *The first person in recorded history to wield sword and
magic at Phasite level simultaneously. The title emerges organically —
soldiers who witnessed the final battle begin using it. By the time
Aldric is crowned king it is already established.*

**The Edgeless —** Holder: Daran \| Earned: Saga 7 — after reaching
Obsidian rank at record speed \| *A reference to the paradox of his
ability: his blade is the sharpest in the world, yet he himself cannot
be categorised, predicted, or contained. He hates the title. He uses it
anyway when it serves him.*

**The Warden —** Holder: Principal Edrath Solm — posthumous \| Earned:
Awarded after Saga 4 by the academy's remaining faculty \| *Given to a
principal who died protecting his students. A formal posthumous
recognition. His portrait in the academy is labelled with it. Aldric
sees it in Saga 7 when he returns.*

**The Transformed —** Holder: Varek Noss \| Earned: Saga 7 — after the
half-Dragonite transformation \| *Not a compliment. A descriptor that
becomes his name in military and political contexts. He accepts it
because it is accurate.*

**Gratha-Borne —** Holder: Sera \| Earned: Saga 8 — given by the goblin
liberation movement \| *In goblin tradition, Gratha-Borne means 'one
whose Ember lit others.' The highest recognition a goblin community can
give. She is the first in three hundred years to receive it.*
