# **SECTION I — PROTAGONISTS**

**ALDRIC VANE** *— The Protagonist — The First of His Kind*

## **Core Identity**

**Full Name:** Aldric Vane

**Age at Saga 1:** 15–16

**Appearance:** White hair, red eyes. Noble features that betray his
bloodline to anyone who knows what to look for. Carries himself like
someone who grew up working — not stiff, not ceremonial.

**Blood:** Noble — lineage unknown to him. Abandoned as an infant and
raised by commoners.

**Classification:** Phasite — one of only 11 in existence. His specific
ability is documented in the Power System Bible.

**Distinction:** The first person in recorded history to wield both
magic and the sword simultaneously.

## **Personality**

Aldric is fundamentally adaptable — he reads rooms, reads people, and
adjusts instinctively. He survived being an outsider at the academy not
by fighting every battle but by knowing which ones mattered. He is
observant in ways people don't expect, which means he often surprises
them. He also has a streak of genuine silliness — dry humor, unexpected
jokes at tense moments, the ability to make someone laugh when they
desperately need it. This is not a mask. It is as real as his intensity.

> *Writing note: Aldric's humor should never undercut serious moments —
> it appears in the margins of tension, not at its centre. It makes him
> feel human rather than legendary. Even kings can be ridiculous
> sometimes.*

## **Internal Wound**

Aldric's deepest fear is losing the people he has chosen as family. He
has no blood family he knows. His adoptive parents, his two friends, his
love interest — these people are everything. The idea of losing them the
way the principal was lost in Saga 4 is the thing that genuinely breaks
him. Every major sacrifice in the story cuts deeper because of this.

His secondary fear — which he does not consciously name — is that his
power makes him something other people cannot fully reach. That the
Phasite in him is a wall between him and the ordinary life he sometimes
aches for.

## **Motivation Arc — Across 8 Sagas**

**Sagas 1–2:** Proves himself. Fights to belong. The academy is a world
that rejects him by default and he refuses to be rejected.

**Sagas 3–4:** Protects. The people around him start dying and being
betrayed. His motivation sharpens from ambition into something fiercer
and more personal.

**Sagas 5–6:** Reckons. The world is bigger and uglier than the academy
ever prepared him for. He starts asking what he actually believes — not
just what he wants.

**Sagas 7–8:** Accepts. Kingship was never the dream. It becomes the
answer to a question the world asked him. He accepts it not for glory
but because he is the only one every faction will follow.

## **Relationships**

**Daran:** The brother he lost. The wound that never fully closes. Their
final handshake in Saga 8 is the emotional resolution of the entire
series.

**The Principal:** The first authority figure who saw him without
prejudice. His death in Saga 4 is the moment Aldric stops being a
student in his soul.

**His Two Friends:** His chosen family at the academy. He is protective
of them to a fault.

**The Love Interest:** She challenges him in ways no one else does. He
falls for her precisely because she does not treat him as special.

**The Faction Boss:** A dark mirror — someone who also saw the world's
rot clearly, and chose destruction instead of endurance.

## **Arc Summary by Saga**

**Saga 1:** Village → academy entrance → tournament winner → Phasite
ability first awakens.

**Saga 2:** Second year at academy. World broadens. Begins sensing
something is wrong.

**Saga 3:** Phasite ability grows uncontrollably. Realises he is being
watched. Trust in institutions begins eroding.

**Saga 4:** Principal dies. Betrayal revealed. Leaves the academy. Steps
into the world changed.

**Saga 5:** Pulled into war. Becomes a symbol. Fights Daran to a draw.
Witnesses goblin conscription.

**Saga 6:** Noble blood exposed. Meets other Phasites. Celebrated and
targeted simultaneously.

**Saga 7:** Kingdom falls. Begins gathering the coalition. Carries the
weight of what the world needs him to be.

**Saga 8:** Final battle. Defeats the boss alongside Daran and the
Phasites. Becomes king.

ALDRIC VANE — Chapter 6 state update

First full Phasite awakening complete. Physical cost profile confirmed
in the field — nosebleed, disorientation, circuit resonance. He filed
the event. He did not look at his hand at chapter's end. His response to
the awakening is consistent with his established pattern: notice,
suppress, file, continue. The drawer is getting full.

**DARAN** *— The Rival — The Greatest Sword in the World*

## **Core Identity**

**Full Name:** Daran (family name deliberately withheld — he stopped
using it)

**Age at Saga 1:** 15–16 — same as Aldric

**Appearance:** Dark-haired, sharp-featured. He grew into someone who
looks like he was carved for combat — lean, efficient, nothing wasted.
His eyes carry the specific coldness of someone who decided warmth was a
liability.

**Blood:** Commoner — same background as Aldric, which makes the
divergence between them even more pointed.

**Classification:** Non-mage. Pure swordsman. The finest in the world by
Saga 5.

## **Personality**

Proud and intensely competitive — for Daran, everything is personal and
everything is a measure. He does not do anything halfway. The warmth he
had as a child was not destroyed; it was methodically buried under years
of grinding, suffering, and choosing not to feel what hurt. He is not
cruel by nature. He is someone who decided cruelty was more useful than
grief.

> *Writing note: Daran should never feel like a simple villain. His
> pride is earned — he built himself from nothing through sheer will.
> The tragedy is that Aldric did the same thing and they ended up on
> opposite sides of it.*

## **The Core Wound**

As children, Aldric had natural gifts. Daran had only will. He was
always the harder worker, always the one who had to try twice as much to
keep up. He did not resent this — until the academy exam. When the mana
assessment showed he lacked the channel capacity to be a mage, it was
not just a rejection. It was the world confirming his worst belief about
himself: that his effort was never going to be enough.

The broken promise — 'we will both become mages' — is the wound
underneath all the hatred. He did not hate Aldric for getting in. He
hated Aldric for making a promise he could not keep.

## **The Convincing**

By the time Daran reappears as an antagonist he has genuinely convinced
himself he feels nothing for Aldric. Not hatred — nothing. He has
reclassified their friendship as a childhood error. This is the lie he
lives in until Saga 6, when he witnesses the faction's true nature and
something in him — something he thought he had buried — hesitates. The
crack in Saga 6 surprises him more than anyone.

## **Relationships**

**Aldric:** The person he measured himself against for his entire life.
He cannot stop, even now. Their draw in Saga 5 is the moment he admits —
only to himself — that Aldric became real.

**Sword School Mentor:** The only person who shaped Daran after the
split. Documented in Section III.

**The Faction:** He refuses to serve them. This is important — his
antagonism is not aligned with the story's primary evil. He is his own
thing.

Daran's mana channel confirmed sub-threshold at the Greyveil entrance
exam. He leaves the assessment hall before his name is not called. His
POV close in Chapter 9 confirms the decision forming: he will not follow
the path that rejected him. He will forge his own. The promise — we'll
both get in, made in Chapter 1 and believed completely by both — is
broken. He does not revise it retroactively. He acknowledges it as real.
This is the engine of his entire subsequent arc. Next confirmed
appearance: Ironmark sword school. His trajectory from this point is the
Ki title progression — Steel by end of Saga 2.

## **Arc Summary by Saga**

> *Full detail per NEW_CANON_DOC_2.docx Section C — Forgehall specifics
> below supersede the short version previously here.*

**Saga 1:** Fails the Greyveil exam, watches Aldric leave, arrives at
Forgehall (the sword school institution at Ironmark — see World Bible).
Clears both the physical trial and the Ki aptitude assessment — the
first time in his life something measures him as good at something.

**Saga 2:** Enters Instructor Kaelin Vosk's brutal fundamentals track.
Matched against his Saga 3 rival Corvin Aske during this phase and loses
badly — keeps getting back up past the point of tactical sense. Grevan
Tusk stops the match personally and, without conversation or ceremony,
begins correcting his form the next day. Reaches Steel rank by end of
saga.

**Saga 3 (Iron rank):** Forgehall reputation builds around Corvin Aske —
genuinely his equal at this stage, so his eventual dominance is earned.
Grevan's mentorship deepens into truth-telling: the only person willing
to tell Daran his hatred of Aldric is making him better and destroying
him at the same time. Dessa Rho ("The Sword Widow," see Section on The
Unbound Core) seeks him out, tests him in a real bout, and offers him a
shortcut to power. His refusal — out of pride, not nobility — is the
saga's central beat and the first on-page proof of his "refuses to serve
the faction" trait. Iron rank achieved, closing the saga.

**Saga 4 (Silver rank):** News of Aldric reaches him indirectly — the
academy assault, Principal Solm's death, Aldric's growing name. This is
where his established "unreadable reaction" to the principal's death
actually lives. The Vampirica encounter — her true first appearance in
the series — is deliberately ordinary and unremarkable in setting, which
is what makes it unsettling; she says or does something that lodges in
him without explanation, then is simply gone. Neither Daran nor the
reader know what she is at this point. Silver rank achieved, closing the
saga.

**Saga 5 (Gold rank):** Reaches Gold rank. The draw with Aldric happens
via open battlefield chaos during the inter-racial/inter-kingdom war —
neither seeks the other out deliberately. Private admission (Aldric
became real to him). His line to Aldric, LOCKED: "Don't die before I
beat you."

**Saga 6:** Core sequence locked — see Grevan Tusk entry above for full
detail. Desperate for power to close the gap with Aldric, seeks out
Grevan, who refuses to hand over Duskmere; Daran kills him and takes the
weapon, which chooses him anyway. **NEW — fugitive status, LOCKED:**
Daran becomes formally wanted by Valdenmere after Grevan's death and
Duskmere's theft — a real fugitive, not just an ambiguous wild card,
shaping his Saga 6–7 isolation. This act is the direct engine of his
established Saga 6 hesitation upon seeing what the Unbound truly is.

**Saga 7:** Reaches Obsidian rank. Reappears at Aldric's gathering, sits
down, says "I'm not doing this for you." Stays. Underneath the line sits
Grevan's resurfacing, unspoken words. **NEW — the Corvin Aske
confrontation, LOCKED:** Corvin Aske, now a Sword Unit officer, is
present with standing orders to arrest Daran on sight. He stands down —
not out of friendship, but recognition that years since their Saga 3
rivalry have made him the kind of officer who follows real judgment over
a technicality. This makes Daran's return both emotionally loaded
(Grevan) and legally loaded (a fugitive walking back into the one
institution that would have him hanged, and staying anyway).

**Saga 8:** Fights as the coalition's greatest swordsman. Saves Aldric's
life. Takes his hand as an equal. Per author instruction, Grevan's death
remains a permanent, unabsolved scar through all of this, including the
series' ending. The last line of the novel is his.

DARAN — Chapter 6 state update

Witnessed Aldric's first full awakening from inches away. Saw the
shimmer, the contact flash on the Greyback, and the eye shift — red to
silver and back. Has no framework for any of it. Said nothing. Did not
look at Aldric again for the remainder of the scene. The image is filed
in a place he will not examine, not immediately, but that will not stop
working. This is the first crack. Not jealousy. Something closer to
realisation. His warmth in this chapter was real and complete — it is
the last time it will be uncomplicated.

# **SECTION II — ALDRIC'S ACADEMY CIRCLE**

**RYNN** *— Friend One — The Heart of the Group*

**Role:** Aldric's first and most loyal friend at the academy

**Background:** Commoner-born like Aldric — their shared outsider status
is what initially bonds them

**Personality:** Loud, hotheaded, fights first and asks questions after.
He is the one who throws the first punch when someone insults Aldric and
the one who apologises too loudly afterward. Underneath the bravado is
someone who is deeply, almost embarrassingly loyal. He would die for
Aldric without thinking about it — and the story may test that.

**Magic Type:** Fire-adjacent — aggressive, instinctive, hard to
control. Mirrors his personality exactly.

**Arc:** Rynn grows from a reactive hothead into someone who learns that
loyalty requires wisdom, not just courage. By the war sagas he is a
genuinely formidable fighter. His Saga 8 moment is earned.

> *Writing note: Rynn provides comic energy in early sagas — his
> interactions with Aldric's humor create the story's lightest moments.
> Do not sacrifice this in later sagas. Even in war, Rynn is Rynn.*

**SOLEN** *— Friend Two — The Mind of the Group*

**Role:** Aldric's second close friend — the thinker to Rynn's fighter

**Background:** Minor noble family — not wealthy or powerful enough to
be arrogant, but enough to understand how noble society works from the
inside

**Personality:** Calm, analytical, quietly perceptive. He is the one who
notices things three scenes before they matter. He rarely raises his
voice. When he does, everyone stops. He is also, secretly, the most
emotionally intelligent person in the group — he reads Aldric better
than Aldric reads himself.

**Magic Type:** Precise and technical — he is not the most powerful mage
but the most controlled one. His spells do exactly what he intends,
nothing more.

**Arc:** Solen becomes the group's strategist in the war sagas. His
noble background gives him access to political knowledge that proves
crucial. His Saga 8 moment involves something he calculates and chooses
— not something that happens to him.

> *Writing note: Solen and Rynn should feel like genuine opposites who
> need each other. Their bickering is affectionate. Do not play Solen as
> emotionless — his calm is chosen, not natural.*

**LIRIEN** *— The Love Interest — Cold Light*

**Role:** Aldric's love interest — feelings concealed until later sagas

**Background:** High noble family. Has been at the academy longer than
Aldric. Already established, already respected — she earned it.

**Personality:** Cold and sharp. She is genuinely brilliant and does not
perform modesty about it. She finds most people beneath her attention —
not out of cruelty but out of a genuine impatience with mediocrity.
Aldric is the first person in a long time who does not bore her. She
does not show this. She shows irritation instead, which he finds
amusing, which irritates her further.

**Magic Type:** Ice and precision — her magic reflects her personality
with almost uncomfortable accuracy.

**Arc:** Lirien's arc is about the slow dismantling of a wall she built
for very good reasons. Her feelings for Aldric emerge not in a grand
confession but in a series of small choices she makes that she cannot
explain away. By the war sagas she is one of the most capable fighters
in the coalition and she fights with a fury that surprises people who
only knew the cold surface.

**Romance Pacing:** Saga 1–3: Mutual antagonism with subtext. Saga 4:
The principal's death cracks her composure — she shows Aldric something
real for the first time. Saga 5–6: Acknowledged but unclaimed. Saga 7–8:
No longer hidden.

> *Writing note: Never make Lirien soft for Aldric's sake. Her sharpness
> is who she is — it does not disappear when she loves someone. The
> romance works because he does not ask her to be warmer. He likes the
> cold.*

**CREST HALVON** *— The Academy Bully — Entitlement With a Wound*

**Role:** Noble student antagonist in Saga 1 — evolves across the series

**Background:** Old noble family with significant academy history. His
father was a celebrated alumnus. He carries that weight like armour.

**Personality:** Classic entitled — he looks down on commoners because
the world he grew up in told him to. But underneath the contempt is a
student who is terrified of not being good enough for the name he was
born into. He bullies Aldric because Aldric's existence — a commoner who
is clearly better than him — is an existential threat to everything he
was told the world was.

**Arc:** Crest does not get a redemption arc quickly or cheaply. He
earns it slowly and ungracefully — making wrong choices, living with
them, and gradually becoming someone who understands what he was. By
Saga 5 he fights in the war on the right side. Not because he became
good. Because he finally chose something bigger than his pride.

> *Writing note: Crest's growth should never be sudden or comfortable.
> He remains prickly, occasionally awful, and genuinely trying. Real
> change is ugly and slow.*

# **SECTION III — MENTORS & AUTHORITY FIGURES**

**PRINCIPAL EDRATH SOLM** *— The Principal — The First Believer*

**Status:** DECEASED — Killed in Saga 4 defending the academy

**Personality:** Warm in private, political in public — genuinely
complex. He navigated academy politics with the precision of someone who
learned early that idealism without strategy accomplishes nothing. In
his office, with the door closed, he was the warmest person Aldric ever
encountered in a position of power. In council meetings he was
unreadable. Both versions were real.

**His Role in Aldric's Life:** Edrath Solm is the first authority figure
who saw Aldric without the filter of his commoner status. He admitted
him to the academy against institutional resistance. He never made a
show of this — he simply acted as if it was obvious. That quiet
confidence in Aldric was more powerful than any speech.

**His Death:** He dies in Saga 4 defending the academy during the
faction's assault. He does not die heroically in a cinematic last stand.
He dies doing his job — protecting his students — and the MC arrives too
late. The wrongness of it is the point.

**Legacy:** Edrath Solm's belief in Aldric is the invisible thread that
runs through every saga after his death. Whenever Aldric doubts himself,
the principal's quiet confidence is what he remembers.

> *Writing note: Reference him sparingly but meaningfully after his
> death. He should feel present in his absence.*

**GREVAN TUSK** *— Daran's Mentor — The Sharpening Stone*

**Status:** DECEASED — killed by Daran, Saga 6, early. Per author
instruction (NEW_CANON.docx Section F / NEW_CANON_DOC_2.docx Section
C) — a major, deliberate darkening of Daran's arc. This supersedes the
prior "Active, brief Saga 7 moment" status below.

**Role:** Forgehall instructor — the person who shaped Daran after the
split. Keeper of the Duskmere shrine/vault at Forgehall (see World
Bible) — his death is a violation of something institutional and sacred
at the school, not just personal.

**Background:** A former soldier who survived three wars by being the
most dangerous person in any room without magic — explained, per new
canon, by his original holdership of the legendary weapon Duskmere (see
Power System Bible, Section XI). He retired to teach because killing had
stopped meaning anything and teaching, he discovered, still did.

**Personality:** Brutal in training. Completely honest. He does not
comfort students — he tells them the truth and lets them decide what to
do with it. He saw in Daran a fury that most teachers would try to
control. He taught Daran to aim it instead.

**His Role in Daran's Arc:** Grevan is the only person who ever told
Daran the truth about himself — that his hatred of Aldric was making him
better and destroying him at the same time. Daran did not listen. But he
remembered.

**Saga 2 — the intervention:** Daran, matched against his Saga 3 rival
Corvin Aske during Instructor Kaelin Vosk's fundamentals track, loses
badly — badly enough a normal instructor would stop the match, but keeps
getting back up past the point of tactical sense. Grevan stops the match
personally, not to end it but to intervene, recognizing the exact
quality that built his own reputation decades ago. No conversation, no
ceremony — he simply begins correcting Daran's form the next day, as if
already decided. Daran does not ask or beg for this.

**Saga 6 — the killing, LOCKED:** Desperate for power to close the gap
with Aldric after their Saga 5 draw, Daran seeks out Grevan, who holds
Duskmere. Grevan refuses to hand it over — not from possessiveness, but
because he sees exactly what Daran is becoming and will not arm it.
Daran kills him — the only person who ever told him the truth about
himself, and the keeper of Forgehall's sacred vault — to take the
weapon. Duskmere chooses Daran anyway afterward: not despite the
killing, but because of the totality of will it demonstrated. This act
is the direct engine of Daran's established Saga 6 hesitation upon
seeing what the Unbound truly is — having just paid this price for
power, he recognizes and is repulsed by the same hunger in Varek's
faction. Consequence: Daran becomes formally wanted by Valdenmere after
Grevan's death and Duskmere's theft — a real fugitive, shaping his Saga
6–7 isolation.

**Saga 7 — now a memory, not a live scene:** The prior note that Grevan
has "a brief but significant moment in Saga 7" is superseded — he does
not appear on-page. When Daran reappears at Aldric's gathering and says
"I'm not doing this for you," underneath the line sits Grevan's
resurfacing, unspoken words — the truth Daran killed a man to avoid
hearing. It is Grevan's own words, resurfacing uninvited, that tip Daran
toward the coalition.

**Saga 8 and beyond:** Per author instruction, Grevan's death is a
PERMANENT, UNABSOLVED scar — never cleanly forgiven or resolved,
including at the series' end, even as Daran's established beats
(coalition's greatest swordsman, saving Aldric's life, taking his hand
as an equal) remain intact.

**Arc:** Saga 2 (mentorship, on-page). Saga 6 (killed, on-page — early
saga). Saga 7 (memory only, not a live scene).

> *Writing note: Grevan should feel like the anti-principal — same
> function, completely different method. Both believed in their student.
> One with warmth, one with iron. His death must carry full weight — see
> Power System Bible's writing note on circuit severing for the
> equivalent standard: do not use it casually.*

# **SECTION IV — ALDRIC'S ADOPTIVE FAMILY**

**MAREN & JOSSE VANE** *— Aldric's Adoptive Parents — The Foundation*

**Maren Vane:** Aldric's adoptive mother. A herbalist and informal
healer in the village — the person everyone comes to when they cannot
afford anything better. Practical, quietly fierce, and the parent Aldric
inherited his humor from. She noticed he was unusual from his first
months — not with fear but with a calm certainty that he would need more
than she could give him, and a determination to give him everything she
had anyway.

**Josse Vane:** Aldric's adoptive father. A carpenter and occasional
village guard. Steady, patient, not a man of many words but every word
chosen carefully. He was the one who first put a wooden sword in
Aldric's hand — not because he knew what Aldric would become, but
because the boy clearly needed somewhere to put his energy.

JOSSE VANE — Chapter 6 state update

Injury confirmed: two cracked ribs, side dressed, conscious. Held the
Greyback long enough to draw it from a cornered villager. Went down from
a single foreleg sweep — dismissal rather than targeted attack. One look
at Aldric from the cot. No words. Correct.

**The Discovery:** They found Aldric as an infant abandoned in a storage
shed on the edge of the village during winter. The decision to keep him
was made between them in about thirty seconds. Neither of them ever
referred to him as anything other than their son.

**The Village Incident:** They are both present during the monster
attack in Saga 1. Whether either of them is hurt is a story decision —
but the attack is the moment Maren and Josse understand that the boy
they raised is something the world is going to come for. Their response
to this — not fear, but quiet ferocious protectiveness — is one of the
series' most important early emotional beats.

**Arc:** They remain in the village through most of the series. They are
not warriors or mages. Their importance is emotional — they are what
Aldric is fighting for in the abstract, made specific and human. A scene
with them in Saga 7 or 8, after the world has changed, is recommended.

> *Writing note: Maren and Josse should feel completely real — not
> idealized parents, but people who made a choice and lived by it every
> day. Their love for Aldric is ordinary and therefore extraordinary.*

# **SECTION V — THE VILLAIN**

**MASTER VAREK NOSS** *— The Faction Boss — The Man Who Looked Too Deep*

**Role:** Primary antagonist — leader of the faction, architect of the
series' central conflict

**Former Identity:** A Phasite researcher — one of the most respected
scholars of magical theory in the world. He spent decades studying the
nature of Phasites: what they are, why they exist, what their presence
means for the world's magical future.

**The Discovery:** Something he found changed him. The specifics are
revealed gradually across Sagas 6 and 7 — but the core of it is this:
his research revealed that the 11 Phasites are not accidents of nature.
They are the world's response to something. A correction. A preparation.
And the current magical and political power structure — built on
hierarchy, on racial inequality, on the suppression of certain kinds of
power — is actively preventing that correction from occurring. The world
is sick. The Phasites are the cure. And nobody in power wants the cure
administered.

**His Goal:** On the surface: overthrow the corrupt power structure of
the kingdoms. Beneath that: force the world into a condition where the
Phasites can fulfil what he believes is their purpose. And beneath even
that: his secret personal goal — to use the Dragonite transformation to
place himself at the centre of that new world, beyond the reach of any
power, magical or political.

**The Half-Dragonite Transformation:** He achieves this in Saga 7. It is
not a simple power upgrade — it is the culmination of decades of
forbidden research into Dragonite physiology and magic. The
transformation is horrifying. It is also, in its own terrible way, a
kind of proof that he was right — that the walls between what the races
are supposed to be are permeable.

**Personality:** Measured, intellectually brilliant, almost courteous in
conversation. He does not rant. He explains. He is the most dangerous
kind of villain because his diagnosis of the world's problems is largely
correct. His prescription — mass death, political destruction, forced
transformation — is monstrous. He sees no contradiction between these
things.

**His View of Aldric:** Varek has known about Aldric since before
Saga 1. A Phasite who is also the first dual sword-and-magic wielder in
history is not incidental to his research — he is its most significant
data point. He does not want Aldric dead. He wants Aldric to understand.
This makes him far more dangerous than an enemy who simply wants to win.

> *Writing note: Varek should never monologue. He converses. He asks
> questions. He listens. His scariest moments are when he agrees with
> someone. Write him as someone who, in a different story, might have
> been the hero.*

**Ability, new canon — Latticework:** Perceives and maps the underlying
structure of mana itself — how circuits, spells, and entire magical
systems are architected. The same gift that let him first notice
anomalous patterns in Phasite mana architecture decades ago. He is the
strongest of the Unbound Core not through raw output but mastery and
knowledge — Latticework lets him read how an opponent's power works in
real time. **Vs. a Paladin:** wins by identifying and dismantling the
exact shape of the opponent's ability before the fight is decided by
force. **View of Ki users:** genuine intellectual dismissiveness, not
fear or hatred — regards Ki as pure accumulated discipline with no
theoretical ceiling to study. Dessa Rho is his one deliberate exception
(see The Unbound Core, below). Mechanics: Power System Bible, Section
IX.

**Arc:** Revealed gradually from Saga 3 (faction hinted), fully present
from Saga 6, transformed in Saga 7, defeated in Saga 8.

# **SECTION VI — WORLD CHARACTERS**

**SERA** *— The Goblin Survivor — The Thread That Runs Through
Everything*

**First Appearance:** Saga 5 — the war. She is one of thousands of
enslaved goblins conscripted as frontline soldiers.

**Background:** Born into slavery. Has never known freedom as a concept
she could apply to herself. Intelligent, watchful, and possessed of a
very dark sense of humour that she uses the way Aldric uses his — to
make unbearable things survivable.

**Personality:** Quietly furious. She has spent her entire life
performing smallness so that people with power over her would not feel
threatened. This performance is so practised that most people read it as
her actual personality. It is not.

**Arc:** She survives the battle where she was meant to die as fodder.
Aldric witnesses it and does nothing in the moment — but he remembers
her face. She reappears in Saga 6 as part of the nascent goblin
liberation movement. By Saga 7 she is one of its leaders. By Saga 8 she
fights in the final coalition — not for the kingdoms, but for what comes
after. Her Saga 8 moment is the abolition scene. She is in the room when
it happens.

> *Writing note: Sera is the story's moral compass on the racial justice
> thread. Her presence reminds readers — and Aldric — that some things
> cannot wait for the right political moment.*

**ELYN DAWNVEIL** *— The Elf — The Prophet's Daughter*

**First Appearance:** Saga 5 — the war, elf POV arc

**Background:** Daughter of one of the elven high council members. She
did not want to be involved in the human conflict. She became involved

**SECTION VIII — THE THREE PALADINS**

Appointed by the Noble Council. Answer to the king. In peacetime — the
king's personal guard. In crisis — the kingdom's final answer. There
have never been more than three Paladins at any time in recorded
history. All three current Paladins are late-20s prodigies, each
appointed young because their ability demanded it. Uniform: fitted white
armour with gold trim as the base. Each Paladin's individual accent
colour distinguishes them.

CALDER VOSS — PALADIN I

Age: 29. Magic type: Light — the rarest affinity in the kingdom,
possibly in the world. Light magic in this context is not gentle — it is
blinding velocity, searing heat, and movement faster than most eyes can
track by riding his own output. He is the strongest and fastest of the
three and he knows it with a completeness that has never required
external confirmation. Proud in the way only people who have never
genuinely lost can be proud — not performative arrogance, but a
bone-deep certainty that the hierarchy of ability places him near its
apex and everyone beneath him should accept this as fact. He looks down
on commoners not out of cruelty but out of genuine indifference — they
exist at a distance he has never felt the need to close. Uniform accent:
gold. His armour catches light and holds it slightly longer than it
should — a passive effect of his affinity he has never bothered to
suppress.

Story role: The first truly elite mage Aldric encounters who is not on
his side. Their eventual confrontation — not necessarily combat — is a
moment where Aldric's dual nature meets the pure magical apex the world
formally recognises. Calder will not know what to do with Aldric. That
loss of certainty will quietly matter.

YSEN ARRAL — PALADIN II

Age: 27. Magic type: Wind and spatial — she creates corridors of
compressed air that can redirect projectiles, collapse structures, or
move her across a battlefield in ways that resemble teleportation but
are not. The youngest of the three and the one who fought hardest to be
taken seriously before her appointment. Precise, controlled, and
privately furious underneath a surface of complete professionalism. She
does not dislike commoners — she dislikes incompetence, which she finds
equally distributed across every class. She and Calder have a working
relationship built entirely on mutual respect for ability and mutual
disinterest in each other as people. Uniform accent: silver-blue. Her
armour is the most minimal of the three — she removed every decorative
element that added weight.

Story role: The Paladin most likely to deal with Aldric fairly. Her read
of him will be the most accurate of the three. She becomes important in
the political sagas.

ORIN VAEL — PALADIN III

Age: 34 *(corrected 2026-09-18 — was 28, which made him 16 at the time
of the assassination attempt below; author confirmed the age shifts,
the "twelve years ago" timing stays)*. Classification: Non-mage.
Sub-threshold mana channel — same assessment result as Daran at academy
age. Ki rank: Gold, approaching Obsidian. He earned the king's personal
respect during an assassination attempt twelve years ago — a palace
guard, off-duty, in the wrong corridor at the right moment. He killed four mages with a sword and no
magic and walked the king to safety with an arrow in his shoulder. The
Noble Council appointed him under sustained pressure from the king. The
mage units of the military have never fully accepted him. He has never
asked them to. Uniform accent: dark iron-grey — the only Paladin whose
trim is not gold or silver. This was not assigned. He chose it. No one
argued.

Story role: The most important of the three for Daran's arc. A swordsman
at the kingdom's highest position is the first proof the world offers
that Daran's path was not a consolation. Their eventual meeting is one
of the series' quieter but more significant moments. He oversees the Arc
7 Tamer capture operation.

**Predecessor, new canon:** Dessa Rho — "The Sword Widow" — held Paladin
III before him. Known capital-wide, though her current whereabouts are
unknown to anyone in power. Her prior hold on the seat gives Orin's own
status as a rare Ki-user Paladin real precedent — Paladin III quietly
becomes "the seat a sword can earn." Full profile: The Unbound Core
section below.

DREL VOSS — THE MONSTER TAMER

Age: Mid-forties. Magic type: Beast Resonance — see Power System Bible.
Formal rank: C-class.

Appearance: Lean and weathered. Medium height. Dark skin, heavily
scarred forearms from years of working with creatures that did not
always cooperate. Pale amber eyes that animals respond to before he has
done anything — they sense the Beast Resonance passively. Dresses in
practical layers — nothing that identifies him as a mage. He looks like
a hunter or livestock trader until he does not.

Status: Captured by Valdenmere military in Saga 1 Arc 7. Coerced by The
Unbound through unknown means — the method of control is not revealed in
Saga 1. He is not comfortable with what he was made to do. He does not
run particularly hard when the military closes in.

Story role: The instrument of The Unbound's monster deployment operation
in Saga 1. His capture ends the visible thread — but the coercion method
being unknown leaves a question the kingdom files away as resolved. It
is not.

**DESSA RHO — The Exception / "The Sword Widow"**

**Age:** Mid-30s. **Classification:** Ki user, Obsidian rank.

**Public title:** The Sword Widow. Known capital-wide, even to the
Paladins themselves — though her current whereabouts are unknown to
anyone in power. Former Paladin III — the seat Orin Vael currently
holds.

**What happened:** She commanded a Sword Unit alongside her closest
friend and fellow Ki practitioner, Corran Vess. On a mission, mage
command (Noble Council-sanctioned, not battlefield judgment) ordered the
unit to hold a strategic position rather than break formation to
retrieve Corran from a collapsing engagement. Corran died following that
order. Everyone above her called it the correct call.

**Her core wound:** "What's the point of power if it can't protect
someone you love." She didn't leave because she lost a fight — she left
because she won, and the cost was ruled acceptable by people who never
had to bury anyone themselves. Timeline: roughly 15–18 years before the
story begins.

**Why she approaches Daran:** Old Sword Unit contacts bring word of an
Iron-rank commoner student outperforming an established rival at an
institution that claims equality but doesn't practice it. She sees a
version of her own former self in him. Her offer (Saga 3) is genuine on
her own terms, even though it also serves the Unbound's recruitment
purposes. She half-wants him to prove her right about the world's
unfairness, and half-wants him to prove it can still be fair without her
interference.

**Backstory:** Ex-military. Watched Ki practitioners denied command and
treated as the military's second-class force despite being the most
dangerous people in the room — ties directly to the Sword Units
resentment thread (see World Bible).

**Personal goal:** Wants the mage-first hierarchy gone outright;
privately wants a real fight against a Paladin on equal terms, for her
own sake. **Knows Varek's full plan:** No.
