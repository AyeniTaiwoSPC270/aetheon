# **SECTION I — THE WORLD**

## **World Name — AETHON**

The world is called Aethon — from the ancient elven word meaning 'the place that endures.' Humans rarely use the name in daily speech. It appears in formal documents, historical texts, and elven scholarship. Most ordinary people simply call it 'the world' or refer to their kingdom or continent. The name Aethon carries weight — when a character uses it deliberately, it signals they are thinking in terms larger than politics.

*»» NEW (2026-09-26): Aethon is one world among a large, sprawling multiverse of realms, most unexplored and unnamed, that Aethon's own creator, Ordessa, did not make and does not govern. The only two other realms named so far — the Hollow (the demon realm) and Aurelia (the angel realm) — lie entirely beyond Aethon and appear on no map of it. This is Act 3 ("Man vs. the Divine") territory — do not develop further without author instruction.*

# **SECTION II — THE KINGDOM OF VALDENMERE**

The dominant human kingdom of Valdris is called Valdenmere. It is the political, cultural, and magical centre of the known world — at least from a human perspective. Elves dispute this framing politely but consistently.

## **Character ****&**** Feel**

Valdenmere feels like a city-state that grew too large for its own philosophy. Its capital is grand — broad stone avenues, towers that catch the morning light, monuments to battles won and kings remembered. But walk two streets off the main thoroughfare and the grandeur gives way to something more honest: cramped housing, market stalls that smell of yesterday's fish, children playing in streets that haven't been repaired since the last king's coronation.

Valdenmere is not medieval European — it is something older and more layered. Its architecture draws on centuries of conquest and absorption — you can see the bones of older kingdoms in the stonework if you know what to look for. Its culture values magical achievement above almost everything. A powerful mage from a commoner family has more social mobility here than a wealthy merchant's son with no mana. This is both its greatest virtue and the engine of its inequality.

## **Key Locations in Valdenmere**

**The Capital — Valdris Prime: **The seat of the king and the kingdom's political heart. A walled city of roughly 400,000 people. Three tiers: the Crown District (nobility and government), the Mage Quarter (ranked mages, academy alumni, magical commerce), and the Common Ring (everyone else). The gap between tiers is architectural — the Crown District's streets are paved with pale stone imported from the north. The Common Ring's streets are packed earth. Every major road in Central Valdris radiates outward from here.

**The Academy — The Greyveil Academy of the Arcane Arts: **Located at the northern edge of Valdris Prime, built into the base of a mountain that was partially excavated to create its lower levels. State-funded and state-influenced, but the principal has traditionally held significant autonomy. It is the most prestigious institution in Valdenmere and one of the most prestigious in Aethon. Students come from across Valdris and occasionally from elven communities. Commoner students are accepted — in theory.

## **The Class System**

**Nobility: **Old families with land, titles, and hereditary seats. Social power comes from bloodline first, magical rank second. A non-mage noble outranks a B-class commoner mage in most social contexts — though not in military ones.

**Ranked Mages: **The meritocratic tier. Social mobility exists here — a D-rank commoner who rises to A rank will be treated as a peer by minor nobility. B rank and above receive formal recognition from the crown.

**Commoners: **Farmers, craftspeople, merchants, labourers. The vast majority of the population. Mana-sensitive commoners are identified young and offered academy placement if their output is sufficient. Most are not.

**Goblins: **Not a class. A caste. Documented separately in Section IV.

Section: Kingdom Infrastructure / Monster Incident Protocol

Monster Incident Reporting

Following a confirmed monster incursion in a settlement, the local district post files an incident report with the kingdom's administrative record. The report places the settlement's name in kingdom records. Acknowledgment from the kingdom typically takes two to three weeks, sometimes longer. The filing does not guarantee a response or remediation — it establishes a paper record only. Ashford's incident report was filed the morning after the Greyback attack in Saga 1, Arc 2. This is the first time Ashford appears in kingdom records.

# **SECTION III — THE GREYVEIL ACADEMY**

## **Overview**

The Greyveil Academy of the Arcane Arts is the dominant institution of magical education in Valdenmere and, by extension, in most of the known world. It is state-funded, politically influential, and theoretically meritocratic — practical reality is more complicated. Founded 340 years before the story begins by a coalition of A-rank mages who believed magical education should be centralised and standardised rather than passed through private family instruction.

## **Structure**

**Years of Study: **Six years standard. Students enter between 14–18. Graduation requires achieving a minimum D rank by final assessment.

**Entry: **Three-stage exam: written theory, practical mana assessment, and combat application. Assessed by a rotating panel of academy faculty. The principal has historically had override authority on borderline cases — which is how Aldric was admitted.

Preliminary Assessment: In advance of the formal three-stage academy entrance exam, families may request a preliminary mana review from the kingdom's travelling assessor. This review is informal — it carries no official weight and does not substitute for the full exam — but it establishes a baseline and allows applicants to understand where they stand before the formal process begins. The assessor visits Ashford and surrounding villages once a year; preliminary reviews are scheduled through the same visit.

**Years 1–2: **Foundation years. Core theory, elemental basics, circuit conditioning, physical training. All students together regardless of rank. Social hierarchies form fast.

**Years 3–4: **Specialisation begins. Students track into their affinity type. Elective courses include advanced combat magic, healing applications, enchanting, and theoretical research.

**Years 5–6: **Advanced study. Students begin participating in real-world assessments — kingdom-contracted work, supervised combat deployment, research projects. »» NEW: this is the mechanism behind Saga 5's capital deployment cohort (Aldric, Rynn, Solen — see SAGA_5_BIBLE.md, Arc 1). The Grand Tournament occurs at the end of Year 1 and Year 3.

Assessment Hall interior — confirmed layout:

Four distinct spaces used across the entrance exam process. Waiting hall: long, high-ceilinged, stone, lamp-oil smell, bench seating. Applicants self-sort by social background without instruction. Written stage room: rows of desks, single proctor, exam distributed face-down and turned on instruction. Practical assessment room: low-ceilinged, off the main hall, single measurement instrument on a stand, one faculty examiner. Combat yard: walled, open-sky, flagstoned, training construct at centre, marked rope observation line, two faculty examiners at a scoring table. Results delivered by lead examiner in the waiting hall, read alphabetically by family name.

## **The Grand Tournament**

The end-of-year Grand Tournament is the academy's most public event. It serves three functions: it assesses student progress in a high-pressure environment, it provides the kingdom with a showcase of magical talent for recruitment purposes, and it is spectacle — nobles attend, bets are placed, and the winner gains significant social capital regardless of their background. Commoner winners make nobles uncomfortable. This is not incidental.

## **The Faculty**

- The Principal: Edrath Solm. His role, character, and »» Saga 5 death (amended, previously Saga 4) are documented in the Character Bible.

- Deputy Principal: A politically cautious B-rank mage who has spent twenty years not making enemies. After Solm's death he becomes acting principal — and immediately begins making compromises that Solm never would have.

- Combat Faculty: Three instructors, all former military A-rank mages. One of them (Instructor Doven) is the compromised mentor of Saga 3 — a trusted figure whose faction allegiance is revealed mid-story, and who earns a genuine redemption by Saga 5 (see Character Bible).

- Theory Faculty: Academics rather than combat mages. Less politically involved, more likely to notice anomalies in students' mana patterns. One theory professor (Instructor Maret) quietly documents Aldric's unusual mana signature in Saga 2 — and files it somewhere it will matter later.

## **The Academy's Political Position**

The academy is state-funded, which means the Noble Council has budget oversight. In practice this means noble families have historically influenced admissions, faculty appointments, and curriculum. The principal's traditional autonomy has been the only check on this. With Solm gone (»» Saga 5), that check disappears. By »» Act 2 (amended, previously "Saga 5" — pushed back one saga since Solm's death moved) the academy has become politically captured — a fact that several former students find personally enraging.

# **SECTION IV — THE RACES OF AETHON**

*Every race in this story is important. Their cultures, politics, and grievances are real and specific. Do not flatten any race into a single stereotype. Each has internal diversity, factions, and contradictions — just like humans.*

**HUMANS***  —  Dominant but fracturing*

**Distribution: **Primarily Valdris, with significant populations on Serath's western coast. The most politically organised race — and the most internally divided.

**Magic Relationship: **The only race that formally ranks and classifies its mages. The F-to-S system was invented by human kingdoms. Elves find it reductive. Demi-humans have their own classifications.

**Internal Divisions: **Noble vs commoner. Mage vs non-mage. Valdenmere vs the smaller kingdoms (»» NEW: Corvane, Kessari, Thalyss — see Section I). Humans are their own worst enemies politically — which the faction exploits deliberately.

**Cultural Feel: **Ambitious, hierarchical, and capable of extraordinary generosity and extraordinary cruelty within the same social structure. Their relationship with other races is defined by a discomfort they rarely examine: they are not the oldest, not the most magically gifted, and not the strongest — but they built the most powerful political structures. They hold power through organisation, not superiority.

**Story Role: **The world's political centre of gravity for »» Act 1 (Sagas 1-5). Their fracturing in »» Saga 5 (amended, previously "Saga 4-5") creates the power vacuum the faction exploits.

**ELVES***  —  Ancient, integrated, and quietly furious*

**Distribution: **Integrated throughout Valdris — primarily in the north (»» Thaelwood, Sil-Aera), in major cities, and in the academy. Their ancestral homeland on Serath's eastern coast (»» Aelith's Reach and surroundings) is considered culturally sacred but no longer politically controlled.

**Social Position: **Elves are treated as socially superior to humans in most formal contexts — their longer lifespan, greater average magical ability, and older cultural history give them a status that is acknowledged even by nobles who resent it. In practice this superiority is performed rather than enforced. An elf cannot simply override a noble's decision. But a noble who publicly disrespects an elf will face social consequences.

**Lifespan: **400–600 years. This creates a specific kind of patience that humans find unnerving and elves find difficult to explain. An elf who is 200 years old has watched three or four human generations rise and fall. Their perspective on 'current events' is genuinely different.

**Magic: **Elves do not use the human rank system but their mages are generally considered equivalent to B or A class. Their magic tends toward precision and complexity rather than raw output. Elven healing magic is the finest in Aethon.

**The Phasite Prophecy: **In elven historical records — not widely shared with humans — there is a passage describing eleven individuals who will appear when the world requires correction. Elyn Dawnveil is the elven scholar who connects this prophecy to the living Phasites in Saga 6. The elven council knew. They chose not to share this information. Their reasons are political and will be a source of conflict.

**Internal Factions: **Isolationists who believe elves should withdraw from human politics entirely. Integrationists who believe elven influence within human society is their best protection — »» NEW: Ambassador Ilvane Thess leads this camp, her position dealt a personal blow by the Saga 2 Wrenhollow summit's collapse. Hawks who believe the elven response to centuries of encroachment should be active resistance — »» NEW: Consul Vaeth Ironroot, a Hawk-faction elder, publicly breaks with Thess after Wrenhollow. Elyn belongs to none of these factions, which is why the council sent her — she is unaligned and therefore deniable.

**Elven leadership: **»» NEW: Queen Aelira Duskwane rules the elven homeland; her daughter Princess Sae Duskwane (Phasite 3 — Genesis) is a figure established in Saga 2 whose future role is deliberately undefined beyond that, her Phasite status known only to elven leadership.

**Story Role: **A third power that has been observing. Their entry into the war in Saga 7 changes the balance — but their motives are never simple.

**DEMI-HUMANS***  —  Independent but economically trapped*

**What They Are: **Demi-humans are a broad category covering multiple sub-races that share partial animal characteristics — lupine, ursine, avian, and others. They are not a unified race in the biological sense; the term is a human political convenience that groups very different peoples under one label. Demi-humans themselves use their specific sub-race names and find the blanket term reductive.

**Distribution: **Their own territories on eastern Valdris (»» Kelmoor as the integration model) and central Serath (»» Kethrayn). These territories are formally independent — no human kingdom claims sovereignty over them. In practice, demi-human territories are economically dependent on human trade networks, human-controlled ports, and human banking systems. The independence is real but the leverage is not.

**Magic Relationship: **Demi-humans have their own magical traditions that do not map cleanly onto the human rank system. Many demi-human mages are assessed as C or B class by human instruments — but their actual capabilities are different in kind, not just degree. Avian demi-humans have wind and spatial magic traditions. Lupine demi-humans tend toward enhancement magic and tracking.

**Commander Tavo's People: **The lupine demi-humans of central Serath (Kethrayn). A warrior culture with a strong oral tradition and a specific, long-standing grievance: for 200 years, lupine demi-human soldiers have served in Valdenmere's military and been systematically denied promotion above a certain rank. Tavo is the first lupine officer to reach a senior command position — and he got there by being so obviously the best option that denying him became politically costly.

**Eastern Valdris demi-human leadership: **»» NEW: Kael Rowan, avian demi-human elder-envoy (community connected to students Pell and Cael), represents the settlement whose land is reassigned at the Saga 2 Wrenhollow summit. Warden Brask Emberclaw is the informal "strongest leads the pack" authority of the broader region — no formal government, but the one Kael answers to informally.

**Story Role: **The third power that formally declares its independence in Saga 6. Their entry into the coalition in Saga 7 is on their terms — they are not joining the human cause, they are fighting for what comes after.

**GOBLINS***  —  The wound at the centre of the world*

**Physical Description: **Small — roughly 1.2 to 1.4 metres on average. Green-grey skin ranging from pale olive to deep forest. Large eyes adapted for low-light environments. Physically stronger per kilogram than humans but shorter and lighter overall. Longer fingers, excellent fine motor control. Historically this made them sought after for precision craftwork — which became the justification for the institution of goblin labour exploitation.

**Legal Status in Valdenmere: **Formally enslaved goblins: owned by noble houses, registered as property in kingdom records, subject to purchase and sale. Legally they have no rights, cannot own property, cannot testify in court, and cannot leave their owner's land without written permission. Indentured goblins: technically free, legally recognised as persons, but bound by debt contracts that are structured to be unpayable. They can move, own minor property, and theoretically seek legal recourse — but the courts that serve them are staffed by people who benefit from the system. In practice, indentured goblins have the appearance of freedom and the substance of slavery. »» NEW: the heaviest concentration is at Ember Hollow in Southern Valdris.

**How They Got Here: **Three hundred years ago, Valdenmere fought a war against a goblin nation — »» NEW named: the Grath Confederacy — that no longer exists, destroyed in what human records call the Southern Pacification and goblins call the Burning. The war ended with a treaty that was rewritten twice by human historians. The original treaty gave the defeated goblin peoples protected status as a subject people. The first rewrite reclassified them as indentured labourers. The second rewrite, fifty years later, legalised formal ownership. By the time the current story takes place, most humans in Valdenmere have no knowledge of the original treaty. Elven scholars know. They have not shared this information publicly. This will become relevant.

**Goblin Culture: **Oral tradition — goblins have maintained their history in spoken form because written records were confiscated or destroyed. They have a complex spiritual tradition centred on the concept of the Ember — the idea that the self persists through suffering if it refuses to be extinguished ("the ember never dies, even when the fire is stamped out"). Practically, this manifests as keeping a small hearthfire or coal perpetually alight within goblin households and communities, even in slavery, even in secret. »» NEW: the tradition traces to Skarn Emberkin, the goblin member of the 7 Great Powers who sealed Vherith in the First Age — his sacrifice is the origin of both the Ember tradition and the name Ember Hollow. The liberation movement's battle cry, first used Saga 7, is "Gratha" ("the Ember holds").

**The Ember-Bound Chain: **»» NEW — one of the 7 Legendary Weapons (see Character Bible Section V-C), tied directly to this tradition. Presumed destroyed in the Burning, actually smuggled out and hidden near Aelith's Reach in Eastern Serath, protected by the elf Yssa Thornwyn as an act of personal atonement. Goblins remain its spiritual keepers by tradition but, per the Scattered & Multi-Race Standing Rule, never its wielders. Living prophecy: it will one day "reignite" and play a role in real goblin liberation — a proposed Saga 6-7 recovery arc.

**The Liberation Movement: **Small and underground in Sagas 2–5. Growing and organised by Saga 6. Led openly by Saga 7. Sera becomes one of its most significant voices — not because she is the most radical but because she is the most strategic. She understands that the movement needs allies outside goblin communities and she is willing to build those alliances even when it costs her.

**Story Role: **The moral centre of the world-building thread. The goblin situation is the clearest expression of the story's theme that power structures justify their own perpetuation. The abolition later in the series is not a clean ending — it is a beginning that will take generations to fully realise. The story is honest about this.

**DRAGONITES***  —  The apex. The myth. The warning.*

**What Is Known by Most Characters: **Almost nothing reliable. Historical records describe encounters that may be exaggerated. The academic consensus in Valdenmere is that Dragonites are extinct or mythological. A minority of scholars — Varek Noss among them — believe they exist and are simply choosing not to be found.

**What Is Actually True: **They exist. They inhabit the interior of Dravenmoor (»» the Silent Peaks, beyond the Frost Line). They are aware of everything that happens in Aethon at a level that most races cannot comprehend. They have not intervened in world events in over a thousand years — not because they cannot, but because they have been waiting for something. Varek's transformation using stolen Dragonite biological material is the first thing in a thousand years that has motivated them to move.

**Physical Description: **Do not describe in detail until their appearance in Act 4 (»» amended, previously "Saga 7-8"). Keep them vast, ancient, and incomprehensible in scale. A Dragonite in flight should make the sky feel small. Their magic should feel like weather, not like casting.

**Magic: **Documented in the Power System Bible. Systemic. Total. Beyond measurement.

**Their View of the Other Races: **Patient. They have watched human kingdoms rise and fall. Elven centuries feel short to them. They do not hate or love any race — they observe with the specific quality of attention that very old things give to very young ones. Varek's transformation represents something different: a violation. Something was taken from their nature without permission. Their response to this is not anger as humans understand it. It is correction. »» NEW: one specific, unnamed Dragonite is among the 3 Calamities (see Character Bible Section V-B) — a single individual, not the race as a whole; identity deliberately held back.

**Story Role: **Mentioned as myth through »» Act 1. Hinted as real from Act 2. One speaks a single line in the series' endgame. The thread they leave open is deliberate — their story continues beyond the novel's ending.

**OTHER RACES OF AETHON**

Aethon contains other sentient species beyond those documented above — sea peoples on Serath's coasts, the stone-kin of northern Valdris, and others referenced in passing. These races play minor roles in the main story but exist in the world's background. Do not invent new major races without author approval. When existing minor races are referenced, treat them as real peoples with their own cultures — not flavour text.

# **SECTION IV-A — VALDENMERE'S MILITARY STRUCTURE (EXPANDED, 2026-09-26)**

**The Chain of Command: **King → Noble Council (15 seats, advisory) → Paladins (3, council-appointed, answer directly to the king) → Military Commander → Ranked Mage Units (A-D class, the kingdom's PRIMARY military force) → Sword Units (Ki practitioners — SECONDARY status, a slow-burn resentment thread) → City Guards → Village Guards.

- Mage Units, not Sword Units, are the kingdom's primary military force.
- Paladins sit above the Military Commander in the chain, but their real function is peacetime king's-guard duty and "the kingdom's final answer" in crisis — not day-to-day command of Mage/Sword Units.
- Paladins are specifically the response to Sovereign-tier (Tier 5) threats and above (Power System Bible). This is why there are only three — apex-threat response is a narrow, rare skillset the kingdom can't spread thin. »» NOTE: Saga 5's invasion leader, "Beast," is Tier 6 — beyond even this, which is why its emergence catches the kingdom's response structure off guard.

**The Military Commander: **Marshal Kaelen Rourke — an A-class mage, career military, the professional backbone between the Paladins and the actual fighting force. Not cruel or biased by intent — he's perpetuated the Mage-over-Sword status quo through decades of institutional inertia rather than malice. Leads from the front personally rather than directing from safety (confirmed in Saga 5's war). Fits the saga's theme: nobody in this structure is actively villainous, the system just isn't looking hard enough at its own imbalances.

**Named Sword Units:**
- **The Ashenguard** — Dessa Rho and Corran Vess's former unit. Its reputation still carries the weight of what happened to them; serving here means inheriting that history whether you want to or not. Fully committed to the field in Saga 5's war.
- **The Ironline** — Frontier/border-specialized, stationed near Eastern Valdris. Executes Saga 3's child-rescue extraction. Takes real losses defending frontier positions in Saga 5's war.

**Named Mage Units:**
- **The Stormwake** — Rapid-response, elemental specialists, the kingdom's go-to for fast escalation. Fights a Branded to a standstill in Saga 5's war at real cost.
- **The Duskwatch** — Investigation and covert operations. Provides the investigative groundwork in Saga 3 that locates where Doven's child is being held, ahead of the Ironline's extraction. Also fields the first reports of Saga 5's transformed creatures.

**Saga 3 application: **The rescue climax uses both tiers of the military correctly — the Duskwatch (Mage Unit, primary force) does the investigative groundwork; the Ironline (Sword Unit, secondary force) executes the physical extraction.

# **SECTION V — THE FACTION**

## **Name — The Unbound**

Varek Noss's faction calls itself The Unbound — referencing their core ideology: that the current world is defined by chains, and they intend to remove them. The name is known only internally until »» Act 2 (amended 2026-09-26, previously "Saga 4"). Before that they operate under various covers — research collectives, trade guilds, philosophical societies.

»» **LOCKED (2026-09-26, author decision):** the sigil, founding date, physical base, and funding model below — drafted as proposals in an earlier session, then set aside pending confirmation — are now confirmed as canon exactly as drafted: their sigil is a single broken manacle/shackle link, small and easily concealed, worn as a tattoo or brand — recognizable only to those who already know to look for it. Founded approximately 16 years before Saga 1, shortly after Dessa Rho's departure from the Paladins. Their physical base is hidden in unclaimed wild land between Corvane, Kessari, and Thalyss — old ruins from Southern Valdris's fallen minor kingdoms, in a no-man's-land none of the three current kingdoms patrol, kept invisible through Halden Corr's Ward Weaving. Funding is three-layered: Juno Vasker's noble house wealth diverted from the top; smuggling and intelligence funneled through Oren Vask's network in the middle; the outer Unaware tier unwittingly providing labor and cover.

## **Ideology**

The Unbound's stated ideology is liberation — from corrupt noble power, from the suppression of non-human races, from the artificial limitations placed on magical development. In broad strokes, they are not wrong about the world's problems. Their methods — assassination, infiltration, mass violence, and Varek's personal goal of forced transformation — are where ideology becomes atrocity. »» NEW: their true underlying purpose, per Varek's research, is that the 11 Phasites were created to combat the 3 Calamities (Character Bible Section V-B) should any go rogue — the current power structure is, in his view, actively preventing the Phasites from fulfilling that purpose.

A significant number of Unbound members genuinely believe in the cause and would be horrified by what Varek intends in its totality. This internal tension matters — some of them are reachable. Kordas's shift in Act 2 is the most visible example.

## **Structure**

**The Core — 7 members: **»» AMENDED (2026-09-26, previously "The Five" — Varek and four lieutenants).
