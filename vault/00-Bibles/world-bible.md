# **SECTION I — THE WORLD**

## **World Name — AETHON**

The world is called Aethon — from the ancient elven word meaning 'the
place that endures.' Humans rarely use the name in daily speech. It
appears in formal documents, historical texts, and elven scholarship.
Most ordinary people simply call it 'the world' or refer to their
kingdom or continent. The name Aethon carries weight — when a character
uses it deliberately, it signals they are thinking in terms larger than
politics.

## **Geography — The Three Continents**

Aethon has three known landmasses, each with its own dominant cultures,
races, and political structures. The story begins on the first continent
and expands outward as the sagas progress.

**CONTINENT I — VALDRIS:** The primary setting for Sagas 1–4. The
largest and most politically complex continent. Home to the dominant
human kingdom, elven integrated communities, and significant goblin
populations. Named by humans after a legendary first king whose
existence is disputed by elven historians.

- Northern Valdris: Dense forests and mountain ranges. Cooler climate.
  Historically elven territory — now shared uneasily with human
  settlements that pushed northward over centuries.

- Central Valdris: The heartland. The dominant human kingdom's core
  territory. Cities, farmland, the academy. Most of the story's early
  action takes place here.

- Southern Valdris: Warmer, more arid. Trade routes and port cities.
  Historically contested — multiple minor kingdoms rose and fell here
  before the dominant kingdom absorbed them. Goblin slave populations
  are heaviest in the south.

- Eastern Valdris: The frontier. Demi-human territories begin here. The
  border is porous and contested. This is where the first inter-racial
  conflict of Saga 2 ignites.

**CONTINENT II — SERATH:** Introduced in Saga 5. A continent of open
plains, ancient ruins, and deep inland seas. Home to the largest
concentration of demi-human independent territories and the origin point
of the goblin liberation movement's most radical faction. The war in
Saga 5 reaches Serath's western shores.

- Western Serath: The most integrated zone — demi-human and human trade
  settlements exist here in uneasy proximity.

- Central Serath: The demi-human heartland. Commander Tavo's people
  originate here. Old and proud culture with no interest in human
  political systems.

- Eastern Serath: Largely unmapped by human cartographers. Ancient ruins
  that predate recorded history. Elven scholars believe this is where
  the first Phasite ever lived — though no proof exists.

**CONTINENT III — DRAVENMOOR:** The third continent. Barely referenced
until Saga 6. Cold, largely unexplored by human standards. The
Dragonites are believed to inhabit its interior — though no human
expedition that ventured deep into Dravenmoor has returned with reliable
testimony. The continent itself feels wrong to mana-sensitive
individuals — like it is saturated with something older and heavier than
standard magical energy.

# **SECTION II — THE KINGDOM OF VALDENMERE**

The dominant human kingdom of Valdris is called Valdenmere. It is the
political, cultural, and magical centre of the known world — at least
from a human perspective. Elves dispute this framing politely but
consistently.

## **Character & Feel**

Valdenmere feels like a city-state that grew too large for its own
philosophy. Its capital is grand — broad stone avenues, towers that
catch the morning light, monuments to battles won and kings remembered.
But walk two streets off the main thoroughfare and the grandeur gives
way to something more honest: cramped housing, market stalls that smell
of yesterday's fish, children playing in streets that haven't been
repaired since the last king's coronation.

Valdenmere is not medieval European — it is something older and more
layered. Its architecture draws on centuries of conquest and absorption
— you can see the bones of older kingdoms in the stonework if you know
what to look for. Its culture values magical achievement above almost
everything. A powerful mage from a commoner family has more social
mobility here than a wealthy merchant's son with no mana. This is both
its greatest virtue and the engine of its inequality.

## **Key Locations in Valdenmere**

**The Capital — Valdris Prime:** The seat of the king and the kingdom's
political heart. A walled city of roughly 400,000 people. Three tiers:
the Crown District (nobility and government), the Mage Quarter (ranked
mages, academy alumni, magical commerce), and the Common Ring (everyone
else). The gap between tiers is architectural — the Crown District's
streets are paved with pale stone imported from the north. The Common
Ring's streets are packed earth.

**The Academy — The Greyveil Academy of the Arcane Arts:** Located at
the northern edge of Valdris Prime, built into the base of a mountain
that was partially excavated to create its lower levels. State-funded
and state-influenced, but the principal has traditionally held
significant autonomy. It is the most prestigious institution in
Valdenmere and one of the most prestigious in Aethon. Students come from
across Valdris and occasionally from elven communities. Commoner
students are accepted — in theory.

**The Vane Village — Ashford:** Aldric's home village. A small
agricultural settlement roughly four days' travel from Valdris Prime.
Known for nothing in particular. Has a local mana assessor who visits
once a year on behalf of the kingdom. The monster attack in Saga 1 Arc 2
puts it briefly in the kingdom's official records.

Wyla — A trader and shopkeeper in Ashford. Sells root oil and other
goods. Has an ongoing account with Maren Vane. Broad, efficient, not a
woman of unnecessary words. First appears Chapter 1.

Vane Household: A modest working home on the interior of Ashford. Maren
Vane operates a herbalist workroom — the smallest room in the house,
herbs hanging from the ceiling beams, jars arranged on the lower shelf
in a specific non-random order, a shallow drying rack under the
south-facing window. The household's layout is simple and well-used. The
back fence faces an open field — Aldric and Daran's default position for
private conversation throughout their village years.

Travel time to capital: Ashford is approximately four days' travel from
Valdris Prime by road, assuming clear conditions. Established by Josse
Vane, Chapter 4.

## **The Political Structure of Valdenmere**

- The King sits at the apex. Currently a monarch of moderate ability and
  significant political pragmatism — not evil, not heroic, deeply
  invested in maintaining the balance of power that keeps him
  comfortable.

- The Noble Council advises the king. Fifteen seats, held by the oldest
  noble families. Deeply resistant to any change that threatens their
  position. The faction's corruption runs through at least four of these
  seats by Saga 3, confirmed active from Saga 2 onward.

**Noble Council — named seats, new canon (NEW_CANON.docx Section A):**

  - **House Halvon** — Lord Berrin Halvon (Crest Halvon's father).
    Traditionalist hawk; opposes elven/demi-human concessions on

  - **House Croft** — Lady Isolde Croft (Nessa Croft's mother).
    Pragmatic centrist; votes with the prevailing power.

  - **House Thane** — Lord Edmund Thane (new). Reformist minority voice;

- The Mage Assembly is a secondary political body — elected by ranked
  mages of B class and above. It has advisory power only, but its
  influence is significant because the kingdom's military depends

## **The Class System**

**Nobility:** Old families with land, titles, and hereditary seats.
Social power comes from bloodline first, magical rank second. A non-mage
noble outranks a B-class commoner mage in most social contexts — though
not in military ones.

**Ranked Mages:** The meritocratic tier. Social mobility exists here — a
D-rank commoner who rises to A rank will be treated as a peer by minor
nobility. B rank and above receive formal recognition from the crown.

**Commoners:** Farmers, craftspeople, merchants, labourers. The vast
majority of the population. Mana-sensitive commoners are identified
young and offered academy placement if their output is sufficient. Most
are not.

**Goblins:** Not a class. A caste. Documented separately in Section V.

Section: Kingdom Infrastructure / Monster Incident Protocol

Monster Incident Reporting

Following a confirmed monster incursion in a settlement, the local
district post files an incident report with the kingdom's administrative
record. The report places the settlement's name in kingdom records.
Acknowledgment from the kingdom typically takes two to three weeks,
sometimes longer. The filing does not guarantee a response or
remediation — it establishes a paper record only. Ashford's incident
report was filed the morning after the Greyback attack in Saga 1, Arc 2.
This is the first time Ashford appears in kingdom records.

# **SECTION III — THE GREYVEIL ACADEMY**

## **Overview**

The Greyveil Academy of the Arcane Arts is the dominant institution of
magical education in Valdenmere and, by extension, in most of the known
world. It is state-funded, politically influential, and theoretically
meritocratic — practical reality is more complicated. Founded 340 years
before the story begins by a coalition of A-rank mages who believed
magical education should be centralised and standardised rather than
passed through private family instruction.

## **Structure**

**Years of Study:** Six years standard. Students enter between 14–18.
Graduation requires achieving a minimum D rank by final assessment.

**Entry:** Three-stage exam: written theory, practical mana assessment,
and combat application. Assessed by a rotating panel of academy faculty.
The principal has historically had override authority on borderline
cases — which is how Aldric was admitted.

Preliminary Assessment: In advance of the formal three-stage academy
entrance exam, families may request a preliminary mana review from the
kingdom's travelling assessor. This review is informal — it carries no
official weight and does not substitute for the full exam — but it
establishes a baseline and allows applicants to understand where they
stand before the formal process begins. The assessor visits Ashford and
surrounding villages once a year; preliminary reviews are scheduled
through the same visit.

**Years 1–2:** Foundation years. Core theory, elemental basics, circuit
conditioning, physical training. All students together regardless of
rank. Social hierarchies form fast.

**Years 3–4:** Specialisation begins. Students track into their affinity
type. Elective courses include advanced combat magic, healing
applications, enchanting, and theoretical research.

**Years 5–6:** Advanced study. Students begin participating in
real-world assessments — kingdom-contracted work, supervised combat
deployment, research projects. The Grand Tournament occurs at the end of
Year 1 and Year 3.

Assessment Hall interior — confirmed layout:

Four distinct spaces used across the entrance exam process. Waiting
hall: long, high-ceilinged, stone, lamp-oil smell, bench seating.
Applicants self-sort by social background without instruction. Written
stage room: rows of desks, single proctor, exam distributed face-down
and turned on instruction. Practical assessment room: low-ceilinged, off
the main hall, single measurement instrument on a stand, one faculty
examiner. Combat yard: walled, open-sky, flagstoned, training construct
at centre, marked rope observation line, two faculty examiners at a
scoring table. Results delivered by lead examiner in the waiting hall,
read alphabetically by family name.

## **The Grand Tournament**

The end-of-year Grand Tournament is the academy's most public event. It
serves three functions: it assesses student progress in a high-pressure
environment, it provides the kingdom with a showcase of magical talent
for recruitment purposes, and it is spectacle — nobles attend, bets are
placed, and the winner gains significant social capital regardless of
their background. Commoner winners make nobles uncomfortable. This is
not incidental.

## **The Faculty**

- The Principal: Edrath Solm. His role, character, and death are
  documented in the Character Bible.

- Deputy Principal: A politically cautious B-rank mage who has spent
  twenty years not making enemies. After Solm's death he becomes acting
  principal — and immediately begins making compromises that Solm never
  would have.

- Combat Faculty: Three instructors, all former military A-rank mages.
  One of them is the compromised mentor of Saga 3 — a trusted figure
  whose faction allegiance is revealed mid-story.

- Theory Faculty: Academics rather than combat mages. Less politically
  involved, more likely to notice anomalies in students' mana patterns.
  One theory professor quietly documents Aldric's unusual mana signature
  in Saga 2 — and files it somewhere it will matter later.

## **The Academy's Political Position**

The academy is state-funded, which means the Noble Council has budget
oversight. In practice this means noble families have historically
influenced admissions, faculty appointments, and curriculum. The
principal's traditional autonomy has been the only check on this. With
Solm gone, that check disappears. By Saga 5 the academy has become
politically captured — a fact that several former students find
personally enraging.

# **SECTION IV — THE RACES OF AETHON**

> *Every race in this story is important. Their cultures, politics, and
> grievances are real and specific. Do not flatten any race into a
> single stereotype. Each has internal diversity, factions, and
> contradictions — just like humans.*

**HUMANS** *— Dominant but fracturing*

**Distribution:** Primarily Valdris, with significant populations on
Serath's western coast. The most politically organised race — and the
most internally divided.

**Magic Relationship:** The only race that formally ranks and classifies
its mages. The F-to-S system was invented by human kingdoms. Elves find
it reductive. Demi-humans have their own classifications.

**Internal Divisions:** Noble vs commoner. Mage vs non-mage. Valdenmere
vs the smaller kingdoms. Humans are their own worst enemies politically
— which the faction exploits deliberately.

**Cultural Feel:** Ambitious, hierarchical, and capable of extraordinary
generosity and extraordinary cruelty within the same social structure.
Their relationship with other races is defined by a discomfort they
rarely examine: they are not the oldest, not the most magically gifted,
and not the strongest — but they built the most powerful political
structures. They hold power through organisation, not superiority.

**Story Role:** The world's political centre of gravity for Sagas 1–5.
Their fracturing in Saga 4–5 creates the power vacuum the faction
exploits.

**ELVES** *— Ancient, integrated, and quietly furious*

**Distribution:** Integrated throughout Valdris — primarily in the
north, in major cities, and in the academy. They do not have a separate
territory on Valdris. Their ancestral homeland on Serath's eastern coast
is considered culturally sacred but no longer politically controlled.

**Social Position:** Elves are treated as socially superior to humans in
most formal contexts — their longer lifespan, greater average magical
ability, and older cultural history give them a status that is
acknowledged even by nobles who resent it. In practice this superiority
is performed rather than enforced. An elf cannot simply override a
noble's decision. But a noble who publicly disrespects an elf will face
social consequences.

**Lifespan:** 400–600 years. This creates a specific kind of patience
that humans find unnerving and elves find difficult to explain. An elf
who is 200 years old has watched three or four human generations rise
and fall. Their perspective on 'current events' is genuinely different.

**Magic:** Elves do not use the human rank system but their mages are
generally considered equivalent to B or A class. Their magic tends
toward precision and complexity rather than raw output. Elven healing
magic is the finest in Aethon.

**The Phasite Prophecy:** In elven historical records — not widely
shared with humans — there is a passage describing eleven individuals
who will appear when the world requires correction. Elyn Dawnveil is the
elven scholar who connects this prophecy to the living Phasites in Saga
6. The elven council knew. They chose not to share this information.
Their reasons are political and will be a source of conflict.

**Internal Factions:** Isolationists who believe elves should withdraw
from human politics entirely. Integrationists who believe elven
influence within human society is their best protection. Hawks who
believe the elven response to centuries of encroachment should be active
resistance. Elyn belongs to none of these factions, which is why the
council sent her — she is unaligned and therefore deniable.

**Elven leadership, new canon (NEW_CANON.docx Section A):** **Queen
Aelira Duskwane** rules the elven homeland — the authority Ambassador
Thess answers to. **Princess Sae Duskwane**, her daughter, is
established as a figure who will play a part later in the series — role
deliberately undefined beyond that. **Ambassador Ilvane Thess** —
Integrationist, leads the Silverwood Accord delegation at Wrenhollow;
the Saga 2 summit's collapse is a personal blow to a position she has
staked her career on, seeding her hardening toward Hawk sympathies
across Saga 2–3. **Consul Vaeth Ironroot** — Hawk-faction elder; publicly
breaks with Thess's Integrationist approach after the summit collapses.

**Story Role:** A third power that has been observing. Their entry into
the war in Saga 7 changes the balance — but their motives are never
simple.

**DEMI-HUMANS** *— Independent but economically trapped*

**What They Are:** Demi-humans are a broad category covering multiple
sub-races that share partial animal characteristics — lupine, ursine,
avian, and others. They are not a unified race in the biological sense;
the term is a human political convenience that groups very different
peoples under one label. Demi-humans themselves use their specific
sub-race names and find the blanket term reductive.

**Distribution:** Their own territories on eastern Valdris and central
Serath. These territories are formally independent — no human kingdom
claims sovereignty over them. In practice, demi-human territories are
economically dependent on human trade networks, human-controlled ports,
and human banking systems. The independence is real but the leverage is
not.

**Magic Relationship:** Demi-humans have their own magical traditions
that do not map cleanly onto the human rank system. Many demi-human
mages are assessed as C or B class by human instruments — but their
actual capabilities are different in kind, not just degree. Avian
demi-humans have wind and spatial magic traditions. Lupine demi-humans
tend toward enhancement magic and tracking.

**Commander Tavo's People:** The lupine demi-humans of central Serath. A
warrior culture with a strong oral tradition and a specific,
long-standing grievance: for 200 years, lupine demi-human soldiers have
served in Valdenmere's military and been systematically denied promotion
above a certain rank. Tavo is the first lupine officer to reach a senior
command position — and he got there by being so obviously the best
option that denying him became politically costly.

**Story Role:** The third power that formally declares its independence
in Saga 6. Their entry into the coalition in Saga 7 is on their terms —
they are not joining the human cause, they are fighting for what comes
after.

**Eastern Valdris leadership, new canon (NEW_CANON.docx Section A):**
**Kael Rowan** — avian demi-human elder-envoy, eastern Valdris territory
(community connected to Pell's and Cael's families, see Character
Bible). Represents the settlement whose land is reassigned in the Saga 2
sabotage; present at Wrenhollow without a formal seat, and the summit's
scapegoat (see Political Structure, above). **Warden Brask Emberclaw** —
current strongest leader of the broader eastern Valdris demi-human
region ("strongest leads the pack" — no formal government). Not directly
at Wrenhollow, but the authority Kael answers to informally.

**GOBLINS** *— The wound at the centre of the world*

**Physical Description:** Small — roughly 1.2 to 1.4 metres on average.
Green-grey skin ranging from pale olive to deep forest. Large eyes
adapted for low-light environments. Physically stronger per kilogram
than humans but shorter and lighter overall. Longer fingers, excellent
fine motor control. Historically this made them sought after for
precision craftwork — which became the justification for the institution
of goblin labour exploitation.

**Legal Status in Valdenmere:** Formally enslaved goblins: owned by
noble houses, registered as property in kingdom records, subject to
purchase and sale. Legally they have no rights, cannot own property,
cannot testify in court, and cannot leave their owner's land without
written permission. Indentured goblins: technically free, legally
recognised as persons, but bound by debt contracts that are structured
to be unpayable. They can move, own minor property, and theoretically
seek legal recourse — but the courts that serve them are staffed by
people who benefit from the system. In practice, indentured goblins have
the appearance of freedom and the substance of slavery.

**How They Got Here:** Three hundred years ago, Valdenmere fought a war
against a goblin nation that no longer exists. The war ended with a
treaty that was rewritten twice by human historians. The original treaty
gave the defeated goblin peoples protected status as a subject people.
The first rewrite reclassified them as indentured labourers. The second
rewrite, fifty years later, legalised formal ownership. By the time the
current story takes place, most humans in Valdenmere have no knowledge
of the original treaty. Elven scholars know. They have not shared this
information publicly. This will become relevant.

**Goblin Culture:** Oral tradition — goblins have maintained their
history in spoken form because written records were confiscated or
destroyed. They have a complex spiritual tradition centred on the
concept of the Ember — the idea that the self persists through suffering
if it refuses to be extinguished. The liberation movement's language
draws heavily on Ember theology.

**The Liberation Movement:** Small and underground in Sagas 2–5. Growing
and organised by Saga 6. Led openly by Saga 7. Sera becomes one of its
most significant voices — not because she is the most radical but
because she is the most strategic. She understands that the movement
needs allies outside goblin communities and she is willing to build
those alliances even when it costs her.

**Story Role:** The moral centre of the world-building thread. The
goblin situation is the clearest expression of the story's theme that
power structures justify their own perpetuation. The abolition in Saga 8
is not a clean ending — it is a beginning that will take generations to
fully realise. The story is honest about this.

**DRAGONITES** *— The apex. The myth. The warning.*

**What Is Known by Most Characters:** Almost nothing reliable.
Historical records describe encounters that may be exaggerated. The
academic consensus in Valdenmere is that Dragonites are extinct or
mythological. A minority of scholars — Varek Noss among them — believe
they exist and are simply choosing not to be found.

**What Is Actually True:** They exist. They inhabit the interior of
Dravenmoor. They are aware of everything that happens in Aethon at a
level that most races cannot comprehend. They have not intervened in
world events in over a thousand years — not because they cannot, but
because they have been waiting for something. Varek's transformation
using stolen Dragonite biological material is the first thing in a
thousand years that has motivated them to move.

**Physical Description:** Do not describe in detail until their
appearance in Saga 7–8. Keep them vast, ancient, and incomprehensible in
scale. A Dragonite in flight should make the sky feel small. Their magic
should feel like weather, not like casting.

**Magic:** Documented in the Power System Bible. Systemic. Total. Beyond
measurement.

**Their View of the Other Races:** Patient. They have watched human
kingdoms rise and fall. Elven centuries feel short to them. They do not
hate or love any race — they observe with the specific quality of
attention that very old things give to very young ones. Varek's
transformation represents something different: a violation. Something
was taken from their nature without permission. Their response to this
is not anger as humans understand it. It is correction.

**Story Role:** Mentioned as myth through Saga 5. Hinted as real from
Saga 6. One speaks a single line in Saga 8. The thread they leave open
is deliberate — their story continues beyond the novel's ending.

# **SECTION V — THE FACTION**

## **Name — The Unbound**

Varek Noss's faction calls itself The Unbound — referencing their core
ideology: that the current world is defined by chains, and they intend
to remove them. The name is known only internally until Saga 4, when
their first open strike forces them into public acknowledgment. Before
that they operate under various covers — research collectives, trade
guilds, philosophical societies.

## **Ideology**

The Unbound's stated ideology is liberation — from corrupt noble power,
from the suppression of non-human races, from the artificial limitations
placed on magical development. In broad strokes, they are not wrong
about the world's problems. Their methods — assassination, infiltration,
mass violence, and Varek's personal goal of forced transformation — are
where ideology becomes atrocity.

A significant number of Unbound members genuinely believe in the cause
and would be horrified by what Varek intends in its totality. This
internal tension matters — some of them are reachable. Kordas's shift in
Saga 7 is the most visible example.

## **Structure**

**The Core — Varek + 6, new canon:** Previously "The Five" (Varek + 4).
Expanded by author instruction to Varek plus six named lieutenants:
Dessa Rho, Kordas, Vampirica, Halden Corr, Reyus Kade, and Oren Vask.
Only Kade knows Varek's full plan — the others believe in the cause
without knowing its totality. None of the seven are revealed on-page as
Unbound members in Saga 2. Full profiles: Character Bible. Abilities:
Power System Bible, Section IX. Their identities are revealed
progressively from Saga 4 through Saga 6.

**Why the Phasites exist, new canon:** Varek's research revealed the 11
Phasites were created specifically to combat the Calamities — a
parallel, fixed-at-3 classification of world-threatening entities — should
any go rogue. See Power System Bible, Section X, and Lore & Glossary
Bible. This directly answers the series' planted mystery of what the
Phasites are "a response to," and reframes the Phasite Silence as a
period with no rising Calamity threat.

**Reyus Kade's infiltration:** Embedded as "Lord Cassian Veyra" on the
Noble Council since before Saga 2 — see Political Structure, above.

**The Informed:** Mid-level operatives who know they serve the Unbound
and broadly understand the goals. They are committed ideologically.

**The Unaware:** Outer layer infiltrators — the compromised mentor,
certain noble council members — who believe they are serving a reformist
political movement. Some discover the truth and stay. Some discover it
and become liabilities.

## **Their Timeline**

- Sagas 1–2: Establishing — planting assets, building the spy network,
  identifying the Phasites.

- Saga 3: Activating — first probing moves, testing academy defences,
  watching Aldric specifically.

- Saga 4: First strike — the academy assault, the principal's death. A
  calculated move designed to test the kingdom's response and eliminate
  Solm's protection of Aldric.

- Saga 5: Using the war — the inter-racial conflict is not their
  creation, but they engineer its escalation and use the chaos to secure
  three critical assets.

- Saga 6: Revealing — Varek steps forward. The full ideology is stated
  publicly for the first time. Recruitment increases. Defections also
  begin.

- Saga 7: The full assault — everything Varek has built comes to bear.
  The transformation happens. The kingdom falls temporarily.

- Saga 8: Defeat — not because they were wrong about the world, but
  because the world they wanted to rule chose not to be ruled.

# **SECTION VI — WORLD-BUILDING RULES FOR CLAUDE**

## **What Claude Can Do Without Permission**

- Add background detail to established locations — street names, market
  descriptions, weather, atmosphere.

- Create unnamed minor characters who serve clear functional roles in a
  single chapter.

- Describe the culture, food, clothing, and daily life of established
  races and locations.

- Reference historical events in passing that are consistent with
  established world history.

- Suggest new world details by flagging them clearly as suggestions
  before incorporating them.

## **What Claude Must Flag Before Writing**

- Any new named location that does not appear in this bible.

- Any new named character who will appear in more than one chapter.

- Any historical event presented as established fact that contradicts or
  significantly expands existing lore.

- Any new race, sub-race, or cultural group presented with significant
  detail.

- Any political development that changes the status of an established
  kingdom, faction, or institution.

## **Consistency Rules**

- Valdenmere's capital is Valdris Prime. The academy is Greyveil.
  Aldric's village is Ashford. Ironmark is the sword school's city;
  Forgehall is the institution itself. Do not rename these.

- The world is called Aethon. The three continents are Valdris, Serath,
  and Dravenmoor.

- Goblins have the Ember spiritual tradition. Elves have the Phasite
  prophecy. Demi-humans use their specific sub-race names.

- Dragonites are not described in physical detail until Saga 7–8. Keep
  them vast and incomprehensible before then.

- The faction is The Unbound. Their name is not public knowledge until
  Saga 4.
