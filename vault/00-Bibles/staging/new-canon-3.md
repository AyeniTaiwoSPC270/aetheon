# SECTION A — SAGA 2, ARC 1: THE NOBLE COUNCIL

World Bible reference: 15 seats total, held by the oldest noble families. At least 4 seats compromised by the Unbound by Saga 3 — confirmed active from Saga 2 onward.

**House Veyra — **"Lord Cassian Veyra." Southern river trade wealth. Chief negotiator for the Silverwood Accord summit. The real Veyra is dead — this seat is held by Reyus Kade in disguise (see Section C).

**House Halvon — **Lord Berrin Halvon (Crest Halvon's father). Traditionalist hawk; opposes elven/demi-human concessions on principle.

**House Croft — **Lady Isolde Croft (Nessa Croft's mother). Pragmatic centrist; votes with the prevailing power.

**House Thane — **Lord Edmund Thane (new). Reformist minority voice; genuinely invested in the treaty succeeding.

## Why the summit is happening

The Silverwood Accord renewal is a periodic, scheduled treaty cycle — not a sudden crisis. Over the year prior to Saga 2, Kade (as "Veyra") used his Council position to push a demi-human land reassignment clause into the renewal draft specifically, manufacturing the flashpoint he later detonates. No connection to the Ashford incident from Saga 1 — the timing is coincidental.

## The sabotage mechanism

During a negotiation recess, "Veyra" pays an outside agitator — who does not know who is really funding him — to stage a break-in near the treaty hall, timed to plant a weapon in the area assigned to Kael Rowan's envoy party. The "breach" is framed as an assassination attempt on Veyra himself. Security "traces" it to demi-human sympathizers before a proper investigation can occur.

- Kael Rowan and the demi-human settlement are publicly discredited.

- Ambassador Thess loses faith in Valdenmere itself — the breach happened on the host kingdom's watch.

- Veyra emerges looking like a victim and a hero.

## Key figures — the Crown, Paladins, elven and demi-human leadership

**The King — **Does not attend the summit; receives reports only. Publicly calm and non-committal after the collapse. Privately orders a quiet Council review, which proves politically toothless since Kade has partial visibility into how it's run.

**Ysen Arral (Paladin II) — **Oversees Wrenhollow summit security. Handles the physical incident competently; the records/investigation manipulation afterward is not a failure on her part.

**Queen Aelira Duskwane — **Rules the elven homeland; the authority Ambassador Thess answers to.

**Princess Sae Duskwane — **Her daughter. Established as a figure who will play a part later in the series — role deliberately undefined beyond that.

**Consul Vaeth Ironroot — **Hawk-faction elder. Publicly breaks with Thess's Integrationist approach after the summit collapses.

**Ambassador Ilvane Thess — **Integrationist. Leads the Silverwood Accord delegation. The collapse is a personal blow to a position she's staked her career on — seed her hardening toward Hawk sympathies across Saga 2-3.

**Kael Rowan — **Avian demi-human elder-envoy, eastern Valdris territory (community connected to Pell's and Cael's families). Represents the settlement whose land is reassigned. Present at Wrenhollow without a formal seat.

**Warden Brask Emberclaw — **Current strongest leader of the broader eastern Valdris demi-human region ("strongest leads the pack" — no formal government). Not directly at Wrenhollow, but the authority Kael answers to informally.

# SECTION B — THE UNBOUND: ORIGINS & STRUCTURE

## Already established (pre-session canon — unchanged)

- Name: The Unbound — references their ideology that the world is chained and they intend to remove those chains. Secret until Saga 4.

- Leader: Master Varek Noss, formerly one of the world's most respected Phasite researchers.

- His research revealed the 11 Phasites are the world's response to something — a correction, a preparation. Tied to the Phasite Silence (a ~200-year gap with no new Phasites, ending ~250 years before the story).

- Ideology: liberation from corrupt noble power, racial suppression, and magical hierarchy. Diagnosis largely correct; methods (assassination, infiltration, mass violence, forced transformation) are the atrocity.

- Three-layer goal: (1) overthrow the kingdoms' power structure, (2) force conditions for the Phasites to fulfil their purpose, (3) Varek's private goal — Dragonite transformation, placing himself beyond any power's reach.

- Structure: Core (leadership) → Informed (committed mid-level operatives) → Unaware (outer infiltrators who believe they serve a reformist movement).

- Saga-by-saga timeline: Establishing (S1-2) → Activating (S3) → First strike/academy assault (S4) → Using the war (S5) → Revealing (S6) → Full assault/transformation (S7) → Defeat (S8).

- Historical grievance invoked: The Obsidian Compact (350 years ago) settled the sword-vs-magic legitimacy question but left racial inequality untouched.

- Confirmed field asset: Drel Voss (Beast Resonance), directed the Greyback toward Ashford in Saga 1 under Unbound coercion — method never explained on-page, deliberately open thread.

## New this session — the true purpose of the Phasites

The Phasites were created specifically to combat the Calamities (see Section D) should any go rogue. This directly answers the series' planted mystery of what the Phasites are "a response to." The Phasite Silence now reads as a period with no rising Calamity threat — the current 11 appearing together suggests one is stirring again.

## New — the Core, expanded to 7 members

Previously "The Five" (Varek + 4) per the World Bible. Expanded by author instruction to Varek + 6 named lieutenants. None of these members or their roles are revealed on-page in Saga 2 — internal reference only.

## New — the Beast Resonance / Drel Voss thread

Proposed (pending confirmation): Drel Voss's coercion method ties to Varek's Dragonite-adjacent research — an augmentation technique layered onto Voss's genuine, rare Beast Resonance gift, rather than the gift itself being common. Beast Resonance stays exactly as rare as already established; the coercion method is a thread pointing at how far along Varek's forbidden research already was in Saga 1.

# SECTION C — THE UNBOUND CORE: FULL PROFILES

## MASTER VAREK NOSS — The Leader

**Age: **Late 50s

**Classification: **A-rank mage (pre-transformation), Conceptual affinity

**Ability — Latticework: **Perceives and maps the underlying structure of mana itself — how circuits, spells, and entire magical systems are architected. The same gift that let him first notice anomalous patterns in Phasite mana architecture decades ago.

**Why he is the strongest of the Core: **Not raw output — mastery and knowledge. Latticework lets him read how an opponent's power works in real time; decades of research across virtually every magical tradition (elemental, Ki, Phasite, fragments of Dragonite/Calamity-level power) mean there is functionally no fighting style he hasn't already studied a counter for.

**Vs. a Paladin: **Wins — not through superior raw output, but by identifying and dismantling the exact shape of the opponent's ability before the fight is decided by force.

**View of Ki users: **Genuine intellectual dismissiveness, not fear or hatred — regards Ki as pure accumulated discipline with no theoretical ceiling to study, nothing to "understand" the way mana architecture can be. Respects results but finds Ki users intellectually uninteresting. Dessa Rho is his one deliberate exception (see below).

**Personal goal: **Three-layered — see Section B.

## DESSA RHO — The Exception

**Age: **Mid-30s

**Classification: **Ki user — Obsidian rank (apex title, fewer than 10 confirmed in the world at any time)

**Signature technique — Ashfall: **A rapid two-blade combination strike layering Ki output in overlapping waves, designed to overwhelm a defender's ability to read where the real hit is coming from.

**What makes her different: **An abnormally massive Ki pool, closer in scale to a mage's channel than a typical Ki practitioner's bounded output. She applies Ki at a volume most Ki users can't physically sustain — this is specifically why Varek respects her despite his general dismissal of Ki users.

**Weapon — the Composite Sword: **One of only 7 legendary weapons in existence in Aethon. Named for the real smithing technique of layering/laminating multiple materials into one blade — here elevated to a legendary weapon capable of dispelling magic attacks outright, making her uniquely feared by mages independent of the Unbound entirely.

**Combat tier: **S-tier / Paladin-contending — the strongest pure combatant in the Core.

**Backstory: **Ex-military. Watched Ki practitioners denied command and treated as the military's second-class force despite being the most dangerous people in the room — ties directly to the existing Sword Units resentment thread.

**Personal goal: **Wants the mage-first hierarchy gone outright; privately wants a real fight against a Paladin on equal terms, for her own sake.

# SECTION E — LEGENDARY WEAPONS

Exactly 7 legendary weapons exist in Aethon. Each was wielded by one of the 7 Great Powers when they sealed Vherith the Hollow King (Section D) in the deep past. When the Great Powers vanished, the weapons scattered across the world instead of disappearing with them, and have been choosing new wielders across the ages since — never inherited or won by force in the ordinary sense; each weapon chooses its own bearer by criteria specific to itself.

**The Ki drawback — applies to all 7: **Regardless of a weapon's outward effect, all 7 legendary weapons draw on Ki, not mana, to function. Meaningful use requires an abnormally large Ki reserve. This is why even a Ki-elite Paladin (Orin Vael) does not carry his legendary weapon as a primary, everyday sidearm — sustaining it draws on the same Ki pool he needs for baseline reflexes and physical conditioning. These weapons are drawn only when a fight has escalated to a level that justifies the cost.

**Two-tier power structure — applies to all 7: **Each weapon has an everyday battle-use capability (the version safe/sustainable to use somewhat regularly) and a rarely-invoked true capability (full output, at real personal cost) — the true capability is closer to what the weapon actually did during the sealing of Vherith, and should not be treated as a routine option.

## Mournblade — Orin Vael (Paladin III)

**Battle use: **Any wound it deals resists magical healing and regeneration, forcing even a powerful mage's injury to heal at ordinary human speed.

**True capability: **At full commitment, can sever a being's connection to its own regenerative force entirely. During the sealing, this is the weapon that ensured nothing Vherith did could undo the damage being inflicted on him.

**Chooses: **Someone who carries grief without performing it.

## The Composite Sword — Dessa Rho

**Battle use: **Dispels magic attacks outright on contact. Makes her one of the most feared warriors in the country independent of the Unbound.

**True capability: **At full output, can unravel a magical construct, curse, or transformation at its foundation, even mid-cast. During the sealing, this is the blade that began unmaking pieces of Vherith's own nature.

**Chooses: **Raw, exceptional strength that has been underestimated or denied its due.

**Name origin: **Named for the real smithing technique of layering/laminating multiple materials into a single blade.

## Hollowfang — lost

**Battle use: **Strikes drain a target's mana channel directly into the wielder, bypassing circuits entirely.

**True capability: **A full strike can hollow out a target's entire magical reservoir in one blow. During the sealing, this is the weapon that stripped Vherith of enough raw power for the other six to contain what was left.

**Status: **Lost — last confirmed sighting generations ago. Open hook for a future discovery.

## Duskmere — Grevan Tusk, later Daran

**Battle use: **Cuts through any Ethereal Crystal ward or magical barrier as though it weren't there.

**True capability: **Capable of cutting a boundary into reality itself. Likely the blade that carved the prison Vherith is sealed inside, not merely a tool that broke his defenses.

**Chooses: **Not virtue — absolute, total will. A commitment sharp enough to cut through anything, including whatever should have stopped its bearer.

**Original holder: **Grevan Tusk — Daran's mentor at Ironmark. His decades as "the most dangerous person in any room without magic" across three wars are explained by this weapon.
