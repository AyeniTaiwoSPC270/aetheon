## Section A — Vampirica, Expanded

- **Age:** Found as a child (roughly 6–8 years old) 28 years before Saga 1, making her chronologically around 34–36 by the time of the story. Her curse's regenerative nature slowed her physical aging dramatically once she reached maturity — she has looked the same age (early-to-mid twenties) for well over a decade. Most people around her have aged normally while she hasn't; she rarely mentions it.
- **Does she accept "Calamity"?** She knows intellectually what she is — Varek told her, given what his research uncovered — but resists the label emotionally. "Calamity" implies inherent world-ending monstrosity, which she finds reductive: it erases the specific person she is in favor of a category. She would rather be feared for what she's actually done than for a designation she never chose.
- **Relationship with Varek:** Genuine love, but not uncomplicated. She believes he loves her — and he does — but she's never fully able to separate that love from what she represents to his larger ambitions (his Dragonite transformation goal, the Unbound's cause). It's the one relationship where even his devotion is tangled up with obsession, and she knows it, even if neither says so directly.
- **Her own goals:** Not ideology, not the Unbound's cause specifically — she wants the freedom to exist openly, without being hidden, protected, or sequestered for her own safety. She wants to be seen as herself, not as Varek's ward or a designation. This is exactly what makes her first meeting with Daran in Saga 4 land so hard — he doesn't yet know what she is, which means, for once, he's just looking at her.

## Section C — The 7 Great Powers, Named

The coalition that sealed Vherith in the First Age — one champion each from Dragonite, elf, demi-human, goblin, human, demon, and angel kind. All ceased to exist as a result of the sealing and the creation of the 11 Phasites; only the 7 Legendary Weapons remain as their legacy.

| Power | Race | Character note |
|---|---|---|
| **Auroth** | Dragonite | The eldest and most powerful of the seven; spoke rarely, acted decisively. The primary architect of Vherith's actual sealing method. |
| **Elarion** | Elf | The group's strategist and record-keeper even then — the reason any true account of the sealing survives at all, passed down through elven oral history. |
| **Draveth Stonefist** | Demi-human | The coalition's physical anchor, first to strike and last to fall back. Demi-human warrior tradition still invokes his name in oaths of honor. |
| **Skarn Emberkin** | Goblin | The origin of the goblin Ember tradition itself (see Section D). His sacrifice in the sealing is literally what the tradition has honored ever since — Ember Hollow's name and the Ember-Bound Chain are both distant echoes of him specifically. |
| **Edrin Halloway** | Human | The one exceptional individual from the youngest race; earned his seat through raw refusal to yield rather than any inherited standing. |
| **Kaszrath, the Broken Horn** | Demon | One of Vherith's own kind, opposing him from the start — the reason Aethon's demon/angel dynamic was never a clean good/evil split. |
| **Auralei** | Angel | Stood beside Kaszrath despite their races' war. The angel realm itself came to be called **Aurelia** in her legacy specifically. |

## Section D — The Ember Tradition

A goblin spiritual practice rooted directly in Skarn Emberkin's sacrifice during Vherith's sealing. Central belief: **the ember never dies, even when the fire is stamped out** — that something of a person's spirit endures unextinguished no matter how completely they're crushed, exiled, or enslaved.

Practically, this manifests as keeping a small hearthfire or coal perpetually alight within goblin households and communities — even in slavery, even in secret, a literal flame kept going as a quiet act of defiance and memory. Skarn's sacrifice is passed down through oral recitation, generation to generation, functioning as both scripture and history at once.

The tradition carries a living prophecy: that the Ember-Bound Chain will one day "reignite" and play a role in real goblin liberation — giving the weapon's planned Saga 6–7 recovery arc genuine spiritual weight rather than just being a plot device. Finding the Chain isn't just recovering a powerful artifact; for goblins who know the tradition, it's the literal fulfillment of a promise made since the First Age.

## Section E — The Monster Classification System

Six formal tiers. Regular military and mage corps handle Tiers 1–4. **Sovereign-tier (5) threats and above are specifically what the three Paladins exist to answer** — this is why there are only three of them; apex-threat response is a narrow, rare skillset the kingdom can't afford to spread thin.

- **Tier 1 — Feral.** Baseline dangerous wildlife, heightened but natural. Established example: the Greyback (Ch. 6). No magical origin required.
- **Tier 2 — Marked.** Creatures altered by prolonged exposure to wild or unstable mana zones — visibly changed (unnatural coloring, extra limbs, glowing features), meaningfully more dangerous than Feral, but still recognizably natural in origin.
- **Tier 3 — Warped.** Deeper corruption — creatures exposed to genuinely dark magic, unstable experimental research, or ancient corrupted sites. Less recognizable as their original species; behavior often turns aberrant, not just physically stronger.
- **Tier 4 — Branded.** Deliberately created or altered, not naturally occurring — the result of intentional magical experimentation or breeding. Marked by a creator, not by chance.
- **Tier 5 — Sovereign.** Apex-tier, singular or exceedingly rare — creatures capable of threatening a city or an army outright. Effectively boss-monster scale within the natural (non-Calamity) hierarchy.
- **Tier 6 — unnamed, officially denied.** The kingdom does not acknowledge this tier exists. Reserved for entities whose confirmation would destabilize public trust entirely — creatures touched by the Hollow, remnants of Dragonite activity gone wrong, or anything that brushes against Calamity-adjacent truths the crown has every reason to keep buried.

### The Monster Arc — threaded across the sagas

- **Saga 1:** Tier 1 (Feral) — already happened, the Greyback.
- **Saga 2:** Tier 2 (Marked) appears only as background/political noise — rising sighting reports along the frontier, cited by both sides in the land dispute, discussed in dialogue rather than encountered on-page. Saga 2 is locked as a zero-combat saga, so this stays atmospheric, not an active encounter.
- **Saga 3:** The first actual on-page monster encounter — Tier 2 (Marked) and Tier 3 (Warped) both surface here, with Warped plausibly tied to the compromised mentor's secret activity.
- **Saga 4:** Tier 4 (Branded) revealed as deliberate Unbound experimentation — part of what makes "First Blood" more than just a political assassination.
- **Saga 5:** Tier 5 (Sovereign) — a genuine city-threatening apex monster amid the war, forcing Paladin-level response.
- **Saga 6–7:** Tier 6 hints surface as the world order collapses and the Unbound fully reveals itself.
- **Saga 8:** the final war plausibly involves something Tier 6-adjacent or beyond.
