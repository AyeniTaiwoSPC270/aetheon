*Aethon — The Known World, Fifth Age*

# Overview

This document consolidates every geographic location established in this session's world-building into a single reference: the physical map of Aethon, every named continent, sea, region, kingdom, and settlement, and how they all connect by road and sea. It should be read alongside NEW_CANON_HISTORY_OF_AETHON.md, which covers the history and cosmology behind this geography — the two documents describe the same world from different angles: one through time, this one through space.

Aethon has three known continents — Valdris, Serath, and Dravenmoor — set within a single ocean. Its named waters are the Undertide Sea (between Valdris and Serath), the Emerald Deep (west of Northern Valdris), and the Veiled Sea (west of Southern Valdris). Aethon itself is one world among a large, sprawling multiverse of realms, most unexplored and unnamed. The only two realms named so far — the Hollow (the demon realm) and Aurelia (the angel realm) — lie entirely beyond Aethon and appear on no map of it; see the closing section of this document.

# Valdris

The largest and most politically complex of the three continents, and the primary setting for Sagas 1–4. Divided into four regions — Northern, Central, Eastern, and Southern Valdris — each with a distinct culture, population, and role in the story.

## Northern Valdris — Elven Territory

Dense old-growth forest rising into a cold mountain range that forms a natural wall against the rest of Valdris. Elves have held this land since long before Valdenmere existed, and unlike almost anywhere else on the continent, human settlement here is minimal — an arrangement most humans simply accept as how the north has always been. The forest canopy is old enough that daylight barely reaches the ground in places.

**Thaelwood — **The larger and older of the two named elven settlements, built up into and around the trees rather than cleared from them — walkways and dwellings integrated into the forest canopy. Considered the cultural heart of Northern Valdris.

**Sil-Aera — **A smaller settlement closer to the mountain line, known as a place of study rather than daily life — where much of the elven scholarly and record-keeping tradition is actually practiced, fitting given elves' role as the world's long-lived historians.

**Mournhollow Pass — **The single reliable crossing through the mountains separating Northern from Central Valdris. Narrow, cold, and easily defended — historically a chokepoint that has kept Northern Valdris difficult to invade even during periods of tension with Valdenmere.

## Central Valdris — The Heartland

The heartland of the kingdom of Valdenmere — farmland, cities, and the political and magical center of the known world from a human perspective. Most of the story's early action takes place here.

**Valdris Prime — **The capital of Valdenmere. Walled, roughly 400,000 people, divided into three tiers — the Crown District, the Mage Quarter, and the Common Ring. Every major road in Central Valdris radiates outward from here, reflecting its role as the true political and infrastructural center of the kingdom.

**Greyveil — **Full name the Greyveil Academy of the Arcane Arts. Built into the base of a mountain at the northern edge of Valdris Prime, partially excavated to create its lower levels. State-funded, though the principal traditionally holds significant autonomy.

**Ashford — **Aldric's home village. A small agricultural settlement roughly four days' travel from Valdris Prime.

**Wrenhollow — **Site of the Saga 2 diplomatic summit. A settlement significant enough to host inter-racial diplomatic proceedings, positioned within easy reach of the capital.

**Halveth — **A market town along the main road network — one of the more prosperous minor settlements in Central Valdris, serving as a trade stopover between the capital and the region's farmland.

**Brackenford — **A river town, sitting where the region's central waterway is shallow enough to ford — the origin of its name. A natural stopover for travel between Central and Southern Valdris.

**Oakmere — **A farming town and one of the primary agricultural suppliers for Valdris Prime itself, close enough to the capital for produce to travel fresh.

## Eastern Valdris — The Frontier

Where Central Valdris's farmland gives way to rougher, more contested land bordering demi-human territory. The border here is porous and has been the site of escalating tension since the Fourth Age's demographic encroachment; this is where Saga 2's inter-racial conflict ignites.

**Ironmark — **A mid-sized city on Valdenmere's eastern border, home to Forgehall — the sword school where Daran trains. State/military-run, brutal survival-of-the-fittest culture.

**Duskgate — **A border fort-town, more garrison than settlement — Valdenmere's most visible military presence on the eastern frontier.

**Thornwick — **A frontier trade post, one of the few places in Eastern Valdris where human and demi-human traders regularly do business face to face, if warily.

**Kelmoor — **A genuinely mixed settlement — human and demi-human residents living, working, and, uneasily, governing side by side. The closest thing Eastern Valdris has to a model of peaceful integration, which makes the political tension surrounding it in Saga 2 land harder.

## Southern Valdris — The Three Kingdoms

Historically the most contested part of the continent — multiple minor kingdoms rose and fell here before Valdenmere's Third Age wars absorbed most of them. Three genuine sovereign kingdoms survive today, each independent for a different reason.

**Corvane (capital: Rendhall) — **Old, proud, and militarily formidable. Corvane survived Valdenmere's expansion by simply being too costly to conquer — the two have fought to stalemate more than once, and relations today are wary respect rather than open hostility.

**Kessari (capital: Kessport) — **A mercantile power built around its coastal trade routes. Kessari survives through economic leverage — Valdenmere needs Kessari's trade more than it needs Kessari's land, and both kingdoms know it.

**Thalyss (capital: Highhold) — **An insular highland kingdom nestled defensively against the southern mountains. Conquering Thalyss was never worth the cost the terrain would demand, and the kingdom has used that isolation to stay culturally distinct from its neighbors.

**Ember Hollow — **Not a kingdom — a grim, walled settlement where much of Valdenmere's goblin slave population is concentrated, its name a bitter echo of the Ember spiritual tradition the goblins carry with them even here. The heaviest concentration of goblin slavery in the kingdom is most visible and most concentrated at Ember Hollow.

**Southmarch — **A border town where Corvane, Kessari, and Valdenmere's own southern holdings meet — a natural administrative and trade point given how many jurisdictions converge nearby.

# Serath

Introduced in Saga 5. A continent of open plains, ancient ruins, and deep inland seas. Home to the largest concentration of demi-human independent territories.

## Western Serath — The Trade Zone

The most integrated part of the continent — demi-human and human trade settlements exist here in uneasy proximity, made more complicated by Verai's colonial presence.

**Corsevain — **Capital of Verai's foothold on Serath — a genuine colonial settlement, not a native Serathi city, giving the kingdom of Verai commercial and political reach beyond Valdris entirely.

**Saltmere — **A trade town on Western Serath's coast, serving the shipping traffic that moves between Corsevain and the wider region.

## Central Serath — The Demi-Human Heartland

Commander Tavo's people originate here. An old and proud culture with no real interest in human political systems.

**Kethrayn — **The main gathering-hold of Central Serath's demi-human population — not a walled city in the human sense, but a genuine center of culture, governance, and daily life for the region's warrior-clan society.

## Eastern Serath — The Unmapped Ruins

Largely unmapped by human cartographers. Ancient ruins predating recorded history.

**Aelith****'****s Reach — **The ruin site elven scholars believe may be where the very first Phasite ever lived, though no proof exists. The road network genuinely ends before reaching it — anyone travelling there is off any charted path.

# Dravenmoor

The third continent. Cold, largely unexplored, believed Dragonite territory. No settlement of any people survives here, and no expedition that has ventured past the frost line has returned with reliable testimony.

**The Silent Peaks — **The one named landmark on the entire continent — a dramatic mountain range where Dragonite sightings are said to concentrate, as much myth as geography at this point.

**The Frost Line — **Not a settlement but a boundary — the informal edge past which no human survey has ever successfully mapped the interior.

# The Seas

**The Undertide Sea — **The waters separating Valdris and Serath — the primary sea route between the two continents, and the one most travelled by trade and, eventually, war.

**The Emerald Deep — **The waters west of Northern Valdris, named for their color where they meet the elven coastline.

**The Veiled Sea — **The waters west of Southern Valdris, named for the mists that gather along that coast — fitting, given the region's historically contested and secretive politics.

# Roads & Routes — How It All Connects

Central Valdris's road network radiates outward from Valdris Prime like spokes from a hub — the most direct physical expression of the capital's political centrality. Major roads run from Valdris Prime to Greyveil, to Ashford, to Wrenhollow, and onward toward Eastern Valdris (through Ironmark) and Southern Valdris (through Ember Hollow and into the three kingdoms). Minor and trade roads branch off these major arteries to connect smaller settlements — Halveth, Brackenford, Oakmere, Duskgate, Thornwick, and Kelmoor.

Southern Valdris's three kingdoms are connected to the central network but remain distinctly bordered — the roads crossing into Corvane, Kessari, and Thalyss are travelled, but treated politically as crossing into foreign territory the moment they pass the sovereign border.

Northern Valdris connects to the rest of the continent through exactly one reliable route: Mournhollow Pass. This is not incidental — it is the reason Northern Valdris has remained so separate culturally and politically even while technically part of the same landmass as Valdenmere.

Between continents, travel is entirely by sea. The Undertide Sea crossing between Valdris and Serath is the only reliable route between the two, making Western Serath's ports — Corsevain and Saltmere — the effective gateway to the entire continent for anyone arriving from Valdris. Central Serath (Kethrayn) is reached by inland travel from the western coast. Eastern Serath is different in kind: the road network genuinely gives out before reaching Aelith's Reach, and travel beyond that point is trailblazing, not travel on any charted route.

Dravenmoor has no roads at all, by design — there has never been a successful expedition into its interior to build one.

# Beyond Aethon

The Hollow (the demon realm) and Aurelia (the angel realm) are not part of Aethon's physical geography and appear on no map of the world. They exist as separate realms entirely, part of a much larger, largely unexplored multiverse that Aethon's own creator, Ordessa, did not make and does not govern. This wider cosmology remains a separate thread from the physical geography documented here — see NEW_CANON_HISTORY_OF_AETHON.md, Section 1, for the founding myth that connects them.
