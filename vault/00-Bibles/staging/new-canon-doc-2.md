# SECTION A — FORGEHALL

The renamed sword school — formerly only referred to generically. Ironmark remains the city name; Forgehall is the institution itself, its name drawn directly from the Dual Path religion's two sacred forces: the Forge (sword/physical) and the Current (magic/knowledge). Greyveil teaches the Current; Forgehall teaches the Forge.

**Governance: **A formal branch of Valdenmere's armed forces — state/military-controlled, not an independent guild. Owned by the kingdom.

**Access: **Humans only. No demi-human or goblin admission, consistent with the kingdom's broader treatment of those groups elsewhere.

**Culture: **Brutal, survival-of-the-fittest. High washout rates, no coddling — the sharpest possible contrast to Greyveil's formal, curriculum-driven academy culture.

**Admission: **Open to anyone regardless of class — commoner or noble, same principle as Greyveil. Entry requires clearing BOTH a brutal physical/combat trial testing raw potential AND a formal aptitude assessment for Ki potential — Forgehall's equivalent of the academy's mana-resonance instruments.

**Age ****&**** course length: **No fixed entry age. Students arrive at very different points in life depending on background — this makes Daran's arrival at ~15 unremarkable in-world, even though it reads as a late start against the academy's fixed entry age.

**Women at Forgehall: **Technically equal footing, culturally rare — a woman who makes it through is notable specifically because it's rare, not because the door is formally closed.

**Career path after graduating: **No single track. A real mix of the Sword Units (military), bodyguard work, freelance mercenary contracts, and a smaller number who stay on as instructors. Higher Blade Titles open doors lower ones don't.

**Physical character: **Functional military bones — barracks, drilling yards, severity — broken up by a small number of genuinely sacred spaces, most significantly a dedicated shrine/vault where Duskmere is formally kept and honored.

**Relationship to Greyveil: **Formal and respectful, grounded in the 350-year-old Obsidian Compact — occasional joint ceremonies or exchanges between the two institutions.

**Dueling culture: **Informal only. Fights happen constantly, but there is no official ranking ladder — status is earned through reputation, not a formal bracket.

**The Duskmere vault: **A specific shrine/vault space honoring the blade, tied to Grevan Tusk's role as its keeper before Daran takes it by killing him (see Section C). This makes that killing a violation of something institutional and sacred, not just personal.

## New named faculty

**Instructor Kaelin Vosk — Weapons ****&**** Conditioning: **Runs the punishing physical fundamentals every new student passes through before reaching Grevan's tier. Unsentimental about washouts; embodies the school's brutal reputation day-to-day.

**Master-at-Arms Yorin Bracken — Tactics ****&**** Formation Combat: **Older, former Sword Unit commander. Teaches how Ki-users function inside kingdom battle formations, not just one-on-one dueling — gives Forgehall's military-branch identity a face beyond Grevan.

# SECTION B — DESSA RHO: "THE SWORD WIDOW"

*Revises/extends her entry in NEW_CANON.docx Section C — same character, expanded backstory and public identity.*

**Public title: **The Sword Widow. Known capital-wide, even to NPCs and the Paladins themselves — though her current whereabouts are unknown to anyone in power.

**Former rank: **Paladin III — the seat Orin Vael currently holds. She held it before him.

**What happened: **She commanded a Sword Unit alongside her closest friend and fellow Ki practitioner, Corran Vess. On a mission, mage command (Noble Council-sanctioned, not battlefield judgment) ordered the unit to hold a strategic position rather than break formation to retrieve Corran from a collapsing engagement — a political calculation weighing the position's value against the cost of retrieval. Corran died following that order. Everyone above her called it the correct call.

**Her core wound: **"What's the point of power if it can't protect someone you love." She didn't leave because she lost a fight — she left because she won, and the cost was ruled acceptable by people who never had to bury anyone themselves.

**Timeline: **Roughly 15-18 years before the story begins — long enough to be a genuine legend by the time Daran encounters her in Saga 3.

**Why she approaches Daran: **Old Sword Unit contacts bring word of an Iron-rank commoner student outperforming an established rival at an institution that claims equality but doesn't practice it. She sees a version of her own former self in him — someone the hierarchy will eventually deny, the way it denied her. Her offer is genuine on her own terms, even though it also happens to serve the Unbound's recruitment purposes. She half-wants him to prove her right about the world's unfairness, and half-wants him to prove it can still be fair without her interference.

**Relevance to Orin Vael: **Her prior hold on Paladin III gives Orin's own status as a rare Ki-user Paladin real precedent — Paladin III quietly becomes "the seat a sword can earn."

# SECTION C — DARAN VALE: FULL ARC, SAGA BY SAGA

## Saga 1 — established, now textured

Fails the Greyveil exam, watches Aldric leave, arrives at Forgehall. Clears both the physical trial and the Ki aptitude assessment — the first time in his life something measures him as good at something.

## Saga 2 — established, now specific

- Enters Instructor Kaelin Vosk's brutal fundamentals track.

- Matched against his Saga 3 rival (see below) during this phase and loses badly — badly enough a normal instructor would stop the match. He keeps getting back up past the point of tactical sense.

- Grevan stops the match personally, not to end it but to intervene — recognizing the exact quality that built his own reputation decades ago: someone who doesn't know how to stop, and isn't performing that refusal for an audience. No conversation, no ceremony — Grevan simply begins correcting his form the next day, as if already decided. Daran does not ask or beg for this.

- Reaches Steel rank by end of saga.

## Saga 3 (Iron rank)

- Forgehall reputation builds around a named rival — someone genuinely his equal at this stage, so his eventual dominance is earned, not handed to him.

- Grevan's mentorship deepens into the established truth-telling role: the only person willing to tell Daran his hatred of Aldric is making him better and destroying him at the same time.

- The Sword Widow's approach: Dessa Rho seeks him out in person, tests him in a real bout, then offers him a shortcut to power.

- His refusal — the saga's central character beat and the first on-page proof of his established "refuses to serve the faction" trait. He refuses out of pride, not nobility: being handed power would cheapen what he's trying to prove to himself.

- Iron rank achieved, closing the saga.

## Saga 4 (Silver rank)

- News of Aldric reaches him indirectly — the academy assault, Principal Solm's death, Aldric's growing name. This is where his established "unreadable reaction" to the principal's death actually lives, now with context underneath it.

- The Vampirica encounter — her true first appearance in the series. Deliberately ordinary and unremarkable in setting, which is what makes it unsettling. She says or does something that lodges in him without explanation. Neither names what she is. She is simply gone afterward, unresolved. Her Unbound/Calamity status is not known to Daran or the reader at this point — the scene's real weight is retroactive.

- Silver rank achieved, closing the saga.

## Saga 5 (Gold rank)

- Reaches Gold rank.

- The draw with Aldric happens via open battlefield chaos during the inter-racial/inter-kingdom war — neither seeks the other out deliberately. War throws them together.

- Private admission (established): Aldric became real to him.

- His line to Aldric, LOCKED: "Don't die before I beat you." In character — competitive, possessive of Aldric as his rival alone, refusing to let the war decide something only he's allowed to decide. Dark humor in the margins, as intended for his character.

## Saga 6

*Core sequence already locked in NEW_CANON.docx Section F — summarized here for arc continuity, with new consequences below.*

- Desperate for power to close the gap with Aldric, seeks out Grevan, who holds Duskmere.

- Grevan refuses to hand it over, recognizing what Daran is becoming.

- Daran kills Grevan — the only person who ever told him the truth about himself, and the keeper of Forgehall's sacred vault — to take the weapon.

- Duskmere chooses him anyway — not despite the killing, but recognizing the totality of will it demonstrated.

- This act is the direct engine of his established Saga 6 hesitation upon seeing what the Unbound truly is.

- Grevan's established Saga 7 "significant moment" becomes a memory, not a live scene — his resurfacing words are what tip Daran toward the coalition.

**NEW — fugitive status, LOCKED: **Daran becomes formally wanted by Valdenmere after Grevan's death and Duskmere's theft — a real fugitive, not just an ambiguous wild card. This shapes his Saga 6-7 isolation directly: he can't safely surface anywhere the kingdom has reach.

## Saga 7

- Reaches Obsidian rank (established).

- Reappears at Aldric's gathering, sits down, says "I'm not doing this for you." Stays. Underneath the line sits Grevan's resurfacing, unspoken words — the truth Daran killed a man to avoid hearing.

**NEW — the Corvin Aske confrontation, LOCKED: **Corvin Aske (Section C-2 below), now a Sword Unit officer, is present at the gathering with standing orders to arrest Daran on sight. He stands down — not out of friendship, but recognition: years since their Saga 3 rivalry have made him the kind of officer who follows real judgment over a technicality. Not forgiveness. Recognition. This makes Daran's return both emotionally loaded (Grevan) and legally loaded (a fugitive walking back into the one institution that would have him hanged, and staying anyway).

## Saga 8

- Fights as the coalition's greatest swordsman.

- Saves Aldric's life.

- Takes his hand as an equal.

- Per author instruction, Grevan's death remains a permanent, unabsolved scar through all of this, including the series' ending.

**Role: **Daran's Saga 3 rival at Forgehall — noble-born, genuinely skilled, disciplined and well-trained since childhood. What separates them isn't talent — Daran doesn't know how to stop, and Corvin has never had to find out if he does.

**Later arc: **Resurfaces as a Sword Unit officer by Saga 7 — see the confrontation above. Gives a human face to the "other track" of Forgehall's system: someone who did everything right by its rules and still ended up in Daran's shadow.

# SECTION D — SERIES TIMELINE

Previously unestablished. Locked this session as a lightweight, low-maintenance anchor rather than a full calendar system.

**Aldric****'****s starting age: **15, at the start of Saga 1 — matches Daran's identical starting point at the academy-entry exam.

**Total series span: **Approximately 10-12 years across all 8 sagas. Chosen specifically because it makes several already-locked beats work: Daran's Blade Title curve (Steel to Obsidian) needs real years to feel earned; Aldric becoming king by Saga 8 needs him to read as an adult; Princess Sae Duskwane needs enough elapsed time to grow into her flagged later role.

**Calendar anchor: **Saga 1 opens in early summer; the academy year begins in early autumn, standard academic-year logic. Tracked as "Year [X] of the Fifth Age" — Saga 1 begins at Year 12 of the Fifth Age. This gives a simple, maintainable way to timestamp events (Saga 1 = Year 12, Saga 2 = Year 12-13, etc.) without inventing a full zodiac-style calendar.
