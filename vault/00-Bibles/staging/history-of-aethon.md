## SECTION 1 — THE FOUNDING MYTH (First Age)

In the unrecorded First Age, the Dragonites' own essence — blood, breath, being — given form, became magic itself. This is the literal origin of all magic in Aethon.

In this same era, **Vherith, the Hollow King**, a great demon, rose as a world-ending threat.

**The 7 Great Powers** — a multi-species council, one champion each from: Dragonite, elf, demi-human, goblin, human, demon, and angel — fought and **sealed** Vherith. Not killed him. The sealing drained them near the edge of their own existence.

Rather than fade immediately, they used what remained of themselves to create an ongoing safeguard against future threats of Vherith's kind: **the 11 Phasites** — a number and nature deliberately distinct from the 7 Weapons/Powers themselves.

That final act of creation finished what the sealing began. **The 7 Great Powers ceased to exist**, leaving only the **7 Legendary Weapons** behind as their legacy — scattered, waiting to choose new wielders across the ages that followed.

**Notes on the human seat:** Humans, though the youngest and least-established people at the time, still held one of the 7 seats — one exceptional individual, not a civilizational achievement. An early thematic echo of the series' own throughline (an unestablished individual proving exceptional against the odds).

**Notes on the demon and angel seats:** Vherith was opposed by a coalition that included one of his own kind (a demon) standing beside an angel. This means Aethon's demon/angel dynamic is NOT a simple good/evil split — to be explored further in a future cosmology/realms session.

**Status of the seal:** Fully stable. Not a live plot thread.

**Public knowledge:** This myth is publicly known — it survives as the foundation of the **Dual Path religion**:
- **The Forge** tradition venerates the 7 Great Powers, who forged Vherith's seal through Ki/martial sacrifice.
- **The Current** tradition venerates the Dragonites, whose essence flows through the world as magic.
- **Walkers** (existing canon) serve both traditions jointly.
- This reframes the **Obsidian Compact** (Third Age, sword/magic co-legitimacy) as a political formalization of a principle the religion had already held sacred for millennia.

**Why Dragonites are distant today:** With Vherith sealed and the Phasites created, the surviving (lesser) Dragonites' purpose was fulfilled, and the greatest among them were already spent. They withdrew into deliberate distance — choosing to watch rather than interfere, believing the world must now handle its own fate.

## SECTION 2 — FIRST AGE SOCIETIES (pre-kingdom)

- **Elves:** Long-lived observers and record-keepers. Watched and remembered more than they acted, even in the First Age. This explains why elven scholars alone know all five Ages in full detail — and foreshadows their Third Age complicity (the same trait that makes them the world's memory is the trait that makes them bystanders when action was needed).
- **Demi-humans:** Warrior/clan-based societies, organized around strength, honor, and territory from the very start.
- **Goblins:** Small, scattered clans. No unity yet — the Grath Confederacy is a much later achievement, generations away.
- **Humans:** Arrived last. The youngest race, least established, settling among peoples already established — despite one individual holding a seat among the 7 Great Powers.

## SECTION 3 — SECOND AGE: THE AGE OF ORDERING

Driven by ambition — a hunger for power and territory the older races weren't pursuing the same way — humans found their edge when the scholar **Drevos** codified the mana channel/circuit system, turning magic from an instinctive, tribally-taught gift into a scalable, organizable resource. This is the direct enabling technology behind human kingdoms rising faster and harder than any other people.

The **Grath Confederacy** (a real goblin peer nation) is established in southern Valdris. First human-elf treaties are signed.

**Demi-human territory is formally recognized** in eastern Valdris — but only after early human expansion attempts were fought to a military standstill by demi-human warrior-clans. Recognition was a **concession born of respect for their strength**, not generosity.

Human expansion, fueled by that same ambition, pushes into Grath Confederacy territory. **Humans are the clear aggressor** in the resulting first human-goblin war — though later human record mythologizes it as "mutual" or "disputed." This conflict is the direct template for the Burning to come.

## SECTION 4 — THIRD AGE: THE AGE OF CONFLICT

Human kingdoms turn their ambition on each other — the great wars. The pattern from the first conflict repeats, catastrophically.

**The Burning (Southern Pacification):** The Grath Confederacy is destroyed via a **coordinated magical scorched-earth campaign** — human kingdoms, fielding mage armies at a scale only possible because of Drevos's systematized magic, deliberately burned Grath Confederacy lands and cities as policy. The very tool that let humans organize becomes the instrument of the atrocity. The original peace treaty gave goblins protected status; it has since been rewritten twice.

**Elven withdrawal:** The elves, witnessing the brutality of the Burning, withdraw from human politics out of **moral horror** — and this withdrawal is genuine complicity, not neutrality. They could have intervened and chose not to. Their isolationist faction still defends the choice as correct; others do not. This deepens the Third Age elven throughline and directly informs Elyn Dawnveil and the Saga 5 elf POV arc.

The age closes with the **Obsidian Compact** — sword and magic traditions formally declared co-equal, understood now as a political echo of the much older Dual Path religious framework.

## SECTION 5 — FOURTH AGE: THE AGE OF INSTITUTIONS

**Greyveil Academy** is founded (340 years before the story) by 7 A-rank mages, led by one clear driving figure among them, on a personal, apolitical belief: raw talent was being wasted without structure.

The F-to-S mage rank system is formally standardized. Goblin indentured servitude is legally codified into slavery — the institutional hardening of what the Burning started. Demi-human eastern land, once won through demonstrated strength, begins eroding again — this time through **pure demographic pressure**: slow, impersonal population growth pushing human settlers outward. This is the direct root of Saga 2's border conflict.

### Varek Noss — full origin

At the age's end, Varek Noss is born. Before any organization exists, he finds and **raises Vampirica** — recognizing what she is from his own research — the first true bond of his life, predating any ideology.

Later, employed and funded by Valdenmere's own institutions, Varek's Phasite research uncovers the deep truth: the Calamities, the 7 Great Powers, the real reason the Phasites exist. The discovery comes at a personal cost — his own methods expose a way to **forcibly override a Phasite's ability against their will**. The crown/academy seizes this discovery and uses it to capture and weaponize the Phasite he was studying — **Toren Larke** — as a state asset. Varek's faith in the institutions he served breaks at the exact moment he learns something enormous and true.

This double wound — love for someone the world would hunt for existing, and guilt/betrayal over what his own research enabled — becomes the true foundation of the Unbound. Not ideology first. The people first.

**The Core of 7 comes together** through a mix of Varek's direct recruitment (people like Kordas, isolated and feared his whole life, given a place to belong for the first time) and organic growth through the network (early members like Halden Corr, Varek's former research peer, helping find others).

## SECTION 6 — NEW PHASITE: TOREN LARKE (Phasite 8)

**Ability: Fatethread** — probability/fate manipulation.

- **Concept category:** probability/fate (distinct from force, identity, causality-replay, and negation — the four other defined Phasites).
- **Everyday use:** subtly tilting small outcomes — a blade missing by an inch, a coin landing right, a guard glancing away at the wrong moment.
- **True scale:** tilting the outcome of an entire battle, a succession crisis, a harvest — kingdom-shaping probability manipulation.
- **Cost gating true scale:** mental/will erosion — forcing fate at scale costs Toren his own sanity.
- **Current status (Fifth Age, present):** Still alive. Still a weaponized state asset of Valdenmere, his ability seized and directed by the crown against his own will. **This is a live plot thread**, available for use from Saga 4 onward.

## SECTION 7 — PHASITE SYSTEM STANDARDS (new hard rules for all 11)

**Rule 1 — Conceptual Standard:** A Phasite ability must be conceptually unprecedented. It cannot be a stronger, rarer, or combined version of any existing spell school (fire, wind, water, light, etc.) or Ki technique. Each of the 11 manipulates a fundamental concept ordinary magic and Ki cannot touch — examples: force, identity, causality, negation, probability, memory, the life-death boundary, space, time, scale, entropy, fate. **No two Phasites may share a conceptual family.**

**Rule 2 — Scale Standard:** Every Phasite ability must have a theoretical true-scale expression capable of kingdom-level or greater impact — either through raw physical scale (mirroring the 7 Legendary Weapons' "everyday use / true capability" model) or through strategic/positional leverage. Most Phasites operate at an everyday, controlled level; true scale is rare, costly, or dangerous to invoke.

**Rule 3 — Identification:** Standard F–S assessment instruments cannot properly classify a true Phasite — the reading doesn't fit the scale, producing an anomalous "double-check" result (as already written in Ch. 3 with Aldric) rather than a clean rank. This flags a *candidate* only — it does not confirm Phasite status alone. **True confirmation requires the ability itself to manifest** as an act with no precedent in recorded magical or Ki history. This is why some Phasites go years unconfirmed, or don't know what they are (consistent with Saga 6: "some are hiding, some don't know what they are").
