# **SECTION I — THE MANA FRAMEWORK**

Magic in this world operates through a biological system present in every mage from birth. It has two core components — the Mana Channel and the Mana Circuits. Together they determine what a mage can do, how much they can do, and what happens when they push too far.

## **The Mana Channel**

The Mana Channel is an internal reservoir located at the core of a mage's body — roughly the chest and abdomen. It stores raw mana. Think of it as a tank. Its size is determined at birth and cannot be fundamentally enlarged through training alone, though it can be conditioned to fill more efficiently and recover faster.

- A larger channel means more mana available before exhaustion.

- Channel size is one of the primary factors measured in a mage's class ranking.

- A completely depleted channel causes unconsciousness and, in extreme cases, permanent damage.

- Mana regenerates naturally through rest, food, and certain herbs. Rate varies by individual.

## **Mana Circuits**

Mana Circuits are the network of pathways running throughout a mage's body — from their channel outward through their limbs, spine, and extremities. They allow mana to flow from the channel to where it is needed for casting. Think of them as veins, but for magical energy.

- Circuit density determines how fast and how complexly a mage can cast.

- More circuits allow multi-layered spells, faster output, and finer control.

- Circuits can be damaged through overuse, trauma, or external attack.

- CRITICAL: If a mage's circuits are severed — through a specific cutting technique or certain magical attacks — they lose access to their magic. This loss may be temporary or permanent depending on severity.

- Circuit severing is considered one of the most brutal techniques in combat. Its use carries social and legal weight in most kingdoms.

## **Mana Exhaustion — Stages**

**Stage 1 — Strain: **Mana below 50%. Mild fatigue. Casting begins to feel effortful. No visible symptoms.

**Stage 2 — Depletion: **Mana below 20%. Noticeable physical weakness. Spells begin to destabilise. Hands may tremble.

**Stage 3 — Critical: **Mana below 5%. Severe pain in the channel and circuits. Spells may misfire or collapse. Collapse risk.

**Stage 4 — Burnout: **Channel fully empty. Unconsciousness. Potential circuit damage. Recovery time: days to weeks.

**Stage 5 — Rupture: **Forced casting past burnout. Channel and circuits physically rupture. Permanent damage likely. Potentially fatal.

## **The Mana Sense**

Trained mages develop a passive ability called Mana Sense — the ability to feel the presence and rough output level of mana in their environment. Higher ranked mages have more refined Mana Sense. S-class mages can detect mana signatures at significant distances and identify individuals by their mana pattern. Phasites have an instinctive Mana Sense from birth that surpasses even S-class mages.

# **SECTION II — THE MAGE RANK SYSTEM**

All magic users in this world are formally classified into seven ranks — F through S. Rank is determined through a combination of four factors measured at official assessment centres run by the kingdoms. Ranks are not permanent — a mage can be reassessed and promoted. Demotion is theoretically possible but extremely rare.

## **The Four Assessment Criteria**

- Mana Channel Capacity — raw size of the internal reservoir, measured in standardised mana units.

- Circuit Density & Quality — number, distribution, and condition of mana circuits.

- Output Force — maximum mana release in a single burst, measured against standardised targets.

- Skill & Technique — practical combat or application assessment. A mage who scores high on raw power but cannot control it will be ranked lower. Precision, creativity, and adaptability are all evaluated.

*No single criterion determines rank. A mage with a massive channel but poor circuits and no technique may rank lower than a mage with a smaller channel and exceptional control. The system rewards the complete mage.*

## **The Rank Table**

| **Rank** | **Title** | **Description** | **Channel Size** | **Circuit Density** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F** | **Latent** | Mana channel detected but output below functional threshold. Cannot cast reliably. Most commonly identified in children. | Minimal | Sparse |
| **E** | **Initiate** | Basic casting possible. Single-element spells only. Used for minor support roles. Most commoner mages plateau here. | Small | Basic |
| **D** | **Practitioner** | Reliable single-element casting. Can hold combat roles. Academy entry standard minimum. Most graduates reach D or C. | Moderate | Developed |
| **C** | **Adept** | Multi-element capable. Competent in combat. Standard working mage rank. Most academy graduates target this. | Good | Dense |
| **B** | **Specialist** | High output and refined technique. Capable of complex compound spells. Elite military and academy teaching rank. | Large | Very Dense |

If assessed, they may register oddly or not at all on standard instruments. Varek Noss's research was the first to formally document this.*

## **Rank in Society**

- F and E ranked individuals are not considered mages in formal contexts — they are 'mana-sensitive'.

- D rank is the minimum for academy graduation and professional mage certification.

- B rank and above receive significant social privileges in most kingdoms — stipends, noble recognition, legal protections.

- A rank mages are treated as national assets. Some kingdoms require them to register and serve.

- S rank mages are effectively political entities. They are courted, feared, and occasionally assassinated.

# **SECTION III — KI ****&**** THE SWORD USER SYSTEM**

Not all combat practitioners use mana. Sword users — and certain martial specialists — operate on an entirely different energy system called Ki. Ki and mana are distinct forces. They do not interact cleanly, which is part of what makes a skilled sword user uniquely dangerous to mages.

## **What Is Ki**

Ki is the body's own refined life force — drawn from physical conditioning, breath control, and the perfection of technique over years of training. Unlike mana, Ki is not stored in a channel. It is generated continuously by the body and accumulated through disciplined practice. A Ki user who stops training loses potency. A mage's mana channel is fixed at birth. A Ki user's ceiling is defined entirely by their dedication.

- Ki users have minimal to no mana. Daran's mana channel was assessed as sub-threshold at the academy exam — this is standard for naturally Ki-inclined individuals.

- Ki amplifies physical abilities — speed, strength, reflexes, and blade sharpness — beyond what an unenhanced body can achieve.

- Advanced Ki users can coat their blade in concentrated Ki, allowing it to cut through magical barriers and interfere with mana circuits directly.

- Ki cannot cast spells. It has no elemental affinity. It is force, precision, and will made physical.

## **Ki Ranks — The Blade Titles**

Sword users do not use the F-to-S mage classification. They have their own recognition system — not numerical ranks but titles earned through combat reputation, formal duels, and peer acknowledgment. The titles from lowest to highest are:

**Steel: **Entry level — a competent sword user who has demonstrated reliable Ki control.

**Iron: **Skilled practitioner. Can trade blows with C-rank mages. Respected in military contexts.

**Silver: **Elite sword user. Ki output is refined enough to coat the blade. Dangerous to B-rank mages.

**Gold: **Master level. Rare. Can threaten A-rank mages in direct combat. National-level reputation.

**Obsidian: **The apex title. Fewer than 10 confirmed at any time in history. Their blade Ki can sever mana circuits. Daran reaches this by Saga 7.

## **Mana vs Ki — Combat Interactions**

- Ki-coated blades can physically disrupt mana circuits on contact — making skilled sword users uniquely terrifying to mages.

- Mana-based barriers are partially effective against Ki attacks but degrade faster than against magic. Ki cuts through magical defences differently to how spells do.

- A sword user cannot be detected by Mana Sense — they produce no mana signature. This makes them invisible to a mage's most reliable early-warning system.

- At high levels, concentrated Ki strikes can temporarily suppress a mage's circuit flow in a localised area — stunning their ability to cast from that limb.

- Aldric is the only practitioner who operates in both systems simultaneously — his Phasite force manipulation is mana-based while his swordsmanship is Ki-enhanced. This combination has never been recorded before him.

# **SECTION IV — MAGIC TYPES ****&**** INTERACTIONS**

Regular mages — non-Phasites — express magic through elemental or conceptual affinities. These affinities are not chosen. They emerge naturally as a mage's circuits develop, usually in childhood. A mage cannot change their affinity but can learn to use it in increasingly sophisticated ways.

## **Primary Magic Categories**

**Elemental: **Fire, water, earth, wind, lightning. The most common affinity types. Widely studied, well-documented, and the basis of most combat magic taught at academies.

**Physical Enhancement: **Mana channelled into the body — strength, speed, durability. Not as flashy as elemental magic but exceptionally dangerous in close combat. Rynn's fire magic has an enhancement component.

**Precision / Technical: **Mana expressed through fine control rather than raw output. Solen's affinity. Allows complex spell construction, mana surgery, and barrier work of exceptional quality.

**Sensory: **Mana used for detection, tracking, and information gathering. Mana Sense is a basic version of this. Full sensory mages are rare and invaluable for intelligence work.

**Conceptual: **Rare. Magic that operates on abstract principles rather than physical elements — time perception, spatial warping, probability. These mages often confuse assessors and are poorly understood.

**Phasite: **Categorically different from all above. See Section V.

## **Type Interactions — How Magic Counters Magic**

Magic type interactions exist on three levels:

- HARD COUNTERS: Certain types have direct physical advantages. Water suppresses fire. Earth absorbs lightning. Wind disperses fog and smoke-based magic. These counters are consistent and taught at academies.

- SOFT COUNTERS: Some interactions depend on skill level and technique. A skilled fire mage can overcome a water mage of lower rank by sheer output. Type disadvantage can be overcome with sufficient mastery.

- TYPE BLINDSPOTS: Certain magic types simply do not interact cleanly. Precision magic and raw elemental magic operate on different frequencies — a precision mage cannot easily counter elemental brute force, but can outmanoeuvre it. Conceptual magic frequently bypasses elemental interactions entirely.

*Aldric's force manipulation does not fit neatly into any counter system. Force affects the physical world directly — it can redirect fire, crush water constructs, and disrupt earth formations. But it has no elemental affinity to counter. This is part of what makes Phasites strategically terrifying.*

## **What Makes a Phasite**

- A Phasite is a mage whose magical ability is entirely unique — the first of its kind ever recorded.

- Their ability did not exist in any documented form before them. It cannot be learned, copied, or inherited.

- Phasites register anomalously on standard mana assessment instruments — some read as impossibly high, some read as broken equipment.

- All Phasites have mana channels and circuits, but their architecture is different from standard mages — more complex, with circuit patterns that assessors have never seen before.

- Phasites are not more powerful than S-class mages in raw output terms. They are categorically different — their abilities operate on principles that standard magic theory cannot fully explain.

- Varek Noss's research established that the 11 Phasites are not random anomalies. They are the world's response to something. What that something is becomes the central mystery of Sagas 6–8.

- **Conceptual Standard: **A Phasite ability must be conceptually unprecedented — never a stronger or rarer version of an existing spell school or Ki technique. No two Phasites share a conceptual family.

- **Scale Standard: **Every Phasite ability has a theoretical true-scale expression capable of kingdom-level or greater impact. Most operate at an everyday, controlled level; true scale is rare, costly, or dangerous to invoke.

- **Scattered & Multi-Race Standing Rule: **Phasites and Legendary Weapon wielders are not confined to Valdenmere or even Valdris — scattered across the world, and not human-exclusive; any race may hold a Phasite ability or wield a Legendary Weapon, EXCEPT goblins, explicitly excluded from both.

- **»» Identification Standard (added 2026-09-26): **Standard F–S assessment instruments cannot properly classify a true Phasite — the reading doesn't fit the scale, producing an anomalous "double-check" result (as on-page with Aldric, Chapter 3) rather than a clean rank. # **SECTION VII — DRAGONITE MAGIC ****&**** VAREK'S TRANSFORMATION**

*Dragonites do not appear until late in the series. They are treated as myth until Saga 6. This section documents what is known — and what Varek Noss discovered — for consistency purposes only.*

## **Dragonite Magic — What Is Known**

- Dragonites are the apex race. Their magic does not operate through mana channels or circuits as humans understand them.

- Dragonite magic is systemic — it runs through their entire body simultaneously, with no separation between storage and expression. They do not cast. They are.

- Their output level is categorically beyond the S-class ceiling. Standard mana measurement instruments cannot register Dragonite signatures accurately.

- They have no known elemental affinity — they express whatever the situation requires. This has never been satisfactorily explained by human magical theory.

- Physical contact with a Dragonite's active magic is described in historical records as experiencing 'the weight of something that has always existed.'

## **Varek's Half-Dragonite Transformation**

**How: **Decades of research into Dragonite physiology. Varek identified a specific biological marker in Dragonite anatomy — a secondary circuit architecture woven through muscle and bone rather than following the standard human pathway. He spent years developing a technique to graft this architecture onto a human body.

**What It Does: **The transformation gives Varek access to a partial version of systemic Dragonite magic — his mana is no longer stored in a channel but distributed through his entire body simultaneously. His output increases by an order of magnitude. His physical durability becomes inhuman.

**What It Costs: **The transformation is irreversible. It is also ongoing — his body continues to change after the initial transformation. By Saga 8 he is no longer fully human in any biological sense. The psychological cost is implied to be significant but he never acknowledges it.

**Its Weakness: **Because his magic is now systemic — everywhere at once — it cannot be focused with the precision a channel-based mage achieves. He is overwhelmingly powerful but less surgically precise than a skilled S-class mage. Aldric's force manipulation, which works on physical forces rather than mana directly, interacts with him differently than any spell-based attack would.

**The Dragonites' Response: **The actual Dragonites register the transformation as a violation. Something was taken that was not given. Their response — ancient, vast, and deliberate — is one of the threads left open at the series' end.

**SECTION VIII — MONSTER CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM**

There are exactly six formal tiers in the monster classification system used by hunters, soldiers, and scholars across Valdenmere. The system runs parallel to the mage rank system but is entirely separate from it. Below the six formal tiers sits an unofficial, unacknowledged seventh category that the kingdom does not formally recognise.

Writing rule: Characters reference monster tiers naturally — a soldier saying 'that's a Feral, we can handle it' versus 'Branded sighting, pull back and send word' tells the reader everything without explanation.

TIER 1 — FERAL

Natural predators. Large, dangerous to unarmed civilians, killable by a competent soldier or low-ranked mage. No magical properties. Pack wolves, giant boars, territorial river creatures, Greybacks. Animal in intelligence and behaviour. The most common tier encountered by ordinary settlements.

TIER 2 — MARKED

Animals or creatures with minor mana exposure. Faster, tougher, occasionally with a single anomalous trait — glowing eyes, unnatural endurance, hide that resists basic spells. Still animal in intelligence. Dangerous to small military units. A Marked creature near a village warrants a kingdom response.

TIER 3 — WARPED

Significant magical corruption. No longer recognisable as their original species in most cases. Behaviour becomes erratic and occasionally tactical — not intelligent, but wrong in ways that feel deliberate. First tier where civilian engagement should never be attempted. A Warped sighting triggers mandatory military response.

TIER 4 — BRANDED

Origin unclear. These creatures did not evolve — they appeared. Physically powerful beyond what biology should allow. Some display rudimentary problem-solving. Kingdom-level response required. Rare enough that a Branded sighting is a significant event that reaches the Noble Council's attention.

TIER 5 — SOVEREIGN

The apex of natural monstrosity. Ancient. Territorial over vast areas. A Sovereign-class creature appearing near a city is a national emergency requiring Paladin-level response. There are perhaps a dozen confirmed living Sovereigns in the world. Their territories are mapped and avoided by treaty in some cases.

TIER 6 — [UNNAMED]

Not formally acknowledged by the kingdom. Not in any official classification document. These are monsters with genuine intelligence — reasoning, memory, possibly language. The world does not know this tier exists. A handful of researchers are aware. Varek Noss is among them. The discovery that some monsters think is a shock reserved for later sagas. Do not reference this tier explicitly until the author instructs.

*NOTE (2026-09-26): "Beast," the Saga 5 invasion's Tier 6 leader, is now locked canon — full profile in NEW_CANON_ACT1_SAGA4_AND_SAGA5.md, PART 5. It deliberately does not use the "Sovereign" name to avoid collision with the Tier 5 classification term above.*

THE GREYBACK

Classification: Feral — Tier 1

A large predatory creature native to the northern forests of Valdris. Bear-mass, low to the ground, broad-shouldered, built for endurance over speed. Its hide has taken enough scar tissue over a lifetime of territorial combat that blades do not bite cleanly. Not magical. Not intelligent. Driven down from the forests when prey is scarce or when something disturbs its territory. The creature that attacked Ashford in Arc 2 was a Greyback — directed toward the settlement deliberately by Drel Voss under Unbound coercion. A Greyback is dangerous to any undefended settlement. It is killable by a competent soldier or low-ranked mage. It killed no one in Ashford. It put Josse Vane on the ground.

THE ETHEREAL CRYSTAL

A ward crystal used by settlements, estates, and institutions to repel monster approach. Operates by projecting a mana frequency that registers as threat or wrongness to creature instincts — Feral and Marked tier creatures will not approach an active crystal's radius. Higher tier creatures are less affected. The crystal requires regular recharging by a mage — the cost scales with crystal quality. Cheap crystals exist and are common in wealthier settlements. High-quality crystals cover larger radii and hold their charge longer. Ashford has never had one — the village relied on its distance from the northern forests and could not sustain the upkeep cost. After the Greyback attack this changes.

Writing note: Ethereal Crystals are background world detail — the equivalent of a lock on a door. Their absence in poor settlements is a class detail, not a plot convenience.

BEAST RESONANCE

Classification: Rare mage affinity — Conceptual category

The ability to project mana outward as a frequency that interfaces directly with a creature's nervous system. At low output: calming animals, reading emotional states, communicating in impressions rather than language. At high output: overriding a creature's instincts entirely — directing it, suppressing fear, pushing it toward a target. Works on any non-sapient creature. Does not work on beings with full intelligence. The line between those two categories is something practitioners think about more than they admit.

Formally assessed as C-class — the ability reads poorly on standard instruments because of its structural unusualness. In practical terms a skilled Beast Resonance mage is significantly more dangerous than a C-rank in specific contexts. In a straight mage duel they are not exceptional. In a forest with multiple Marked-class creatures at their direction they are something else entirely.

Currently only one confirmed practitioner: Drel Voss. Whether others exist is unknown.
