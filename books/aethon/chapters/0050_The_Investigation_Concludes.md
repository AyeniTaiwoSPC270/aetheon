# Chapter 50: The Investigation Concludes

### Saga 1, Arc 7

---

— Aldric Vane —

"I'm just saying," Rynn said, "that if you'd told me in the first week that I'd be *nostalgic* for the day Crest called you a pamphlet, I'd have had you looked at."

"You did have me looked at. You threw a punch on my behalf without asking my opinion first."

"That's different. That's supportive."

"It cost you two weeks of lamp brackets."

"Character building," Rynn said, with the specific, aggressive cheer of a man determined to enjoy the last ordinary corridor of his morning before Mana Theory ruined it. "I've got excellent character now. Ask anyone. Ask Solen."

"He has excellent character," Solen said, not looking up from the folded page he was squaring the corners of, a habit Aldric had stopped finding strange sometime in the last two years and started, instead, finding load-bearing — Solen composed meant Solen thinking, and Solen thinking usually meant something worth hearing arrived within the next thirty seconds. "It's mostly composed of volume."

"See, that's support with a knife in it. That's your whole personality."

"It's efficient," Aldric said.

"Don't you start."

The corridor outside the east stair had its usual mid-morning texture — students moving in loose, unhurried clusters toward whatever rotation claimed them next, someone's satchel strap failing spectacularly near the noticeboard, the particular institutional hum of a term winding down without anyone having formally announced that it was. Aldric had walked this stretch of corridor perhaps four hundred times since his own first, disoriented morning in it, and had long since stopped mapping it consciously — which was, he understood now, its own small victory, one he hadn't noticed happening until it had already happened, the way most of the good things at Greyveil seemed to arrive.

He nearly missed the voices for exactly that reason. He had stopped listening for trouble in this corridor months ago, on the reasonable theory that a hallway this well-lit and this full of first-years arguing about lamp brackets was not, on balance, where trouble tended to live.

It was Bavel's voice that caught him first — not the words, just the register, pitched lower and flatter than his ordinary classroom clip, the specific tone of a man who has decided a conversation isn't for the corridor and is having it in the corridor anyway because there wasn't a better room available on short notice. Aldric slowed without deciding to. Rynn, mid-sentence about something involving a goat and a wager he'd apparently lost to Torval, didn't notice. Solen did, and went quiet in the particular way that meant he'd clocked it too.

Bavel was three doors down, half-turned into an alcove with a records clerk Aldric didn't recognize — an older man, ledger under one arm, the specific weary posture of someone who'd been fetching files since before dawn and expected to keep fetching them past dusk. Their voices didn't carry whole sentences, only fragments, the kind a corridor gives up freely and takes back just as fast.

"—capture operation, outside Valdris Prime—" the clerk said, and something in Aldric's chest did the small, involuntary thing it always did at a phrase like that, an old reflex from a life he mostly didn't think about anymore, sharpening without his permission.

"—confirmed?" Bavel, flat.

"—Crown's calling it concluded. Officially. As of this morning."

"And the—"

The rest dissolved into the general corridor noise — a laugh two doors up, someone's boot heel catching a loose flagstone, Rynn finally noticing Aldric had stopped walking and turning back with a question half-formed on his face. By the time the corridor settled again, Bavel and the clerk had drifted further into the alcove, and whatever came next stayed there with them.

"You've got the face," Solen said, quietly, arriving at Aldric's shoulder.

"I don't have a face."

"You have several. That was the arithmetic one." Solen's eyes flicked once toward the alcove, cataloguing rather than staring, and came back. "What did you catch?"

"Capture operation. Outside Valdris Prime. The Crown's calling it concluded."

"Concluded what?"

"I don't know. That's rather the trouble with a fragment."

Rynn had caught up properly now, radiating the specific confusion of a man who'd been mid-anecdote and found the audience gone. "Are we eavesdropping on Bavel? Is that a thing we do now? I feel like that should've come with a briefing."

"We weren't eavesdropping," Aldric said. "We were standing in a corridor Bavel chose to have a conversation in."

"That's the same thing with better posture."

"It's considerably better posture."

Solen hadn't moved his eyes from the alcove, though Bavel and the clerk had already gone, the way faculty conversations at Greyveil tended to — present, then abruptly not, leaving nothing behind but the shape of where they'd stood. "A capture operation is a specific kind of thing to conclude," he said, in the tone he used for problems he hadn't finished solving yet. "Not a battle. Not a skirmish. Something someone was hunting, and finally caught."

"Could be anything. Bandits. Smugglers. Somebody's escaped bull."

"Bavel doesn't lower his voice for an escaped bull."

That was true, and Aldric turned it over for exactly as long as it took to register that turning it over further wasn't going to produce anything but more turning. He had learned, across two years at Greyveil, a fairly reliable rule about faculty conversations conducted in half-sentences and lowered voices: they were rarely about him specifically, and when they were, someone eventually told him, at whatever pace and in whatever tone they judged he could bear. This had the texture of the first kind — large, official, entirely outside the boundaries of a first-year's ordinary concerns, the sort of thing the kingdom handled the way it handled weather, at a scale that simply didn't require his opinion.

"It's probably nothing to do with us," he said, and started walking again, because Mana Theory did not wait politely for a corridor's worth of half-heard gossip, and because — if he was honest, which he tried to be at least with himself, on principle — filing it as *probably nothing* was considerably easier than the alternative, which was turning over exactly why the phrase *capture operation* had made something in his chest sit up before his mind had caught up to why.

"That's very confident for a man who caught four words of a much longer sentence," Solen said, falling into step beside him.

"I'm extremely confident. It's a personality trait I'm cultivating."

"Cultivating it badly."

"I'm aware."

Rynn, apparently deciding the moment for gravity had passed without anyone officially declaring it, resumed the goat anecdote from approximately where he'd abandoned it, with the specific enthusiasm of a man who'd been robbed of an audience and intended to collect the debt in full. Aldric let it wash over him, half-listening, the other half of his attention still faintly, stubbornly caught on a fragment of sentence that had nothing to do with him and every reason, by any sensible accounting, to be forgotten by the time they reached the lecture room.

Maret's classroom had its usual pre-session quiet, benches filling in the particular unhurried order two years of habit had carved into the room — Solen and Rynn peeling off toward their customary seats, Nessa Croft already settled with a diagram half-copied, Pell folding himself into the back row with the loose-limbed patience of someone who'd long since stopped needing to prove anything to anyone in this room. Aldric took his own seat, pulled out the previous week's notes, and found — with a small, private irritation at himself — that *capture operation, outside Valdris Prime* had settled somewhere at the back of his thinking and did not seem inclined to leave.

He told himself, as Maret entered without greeting and began chalking a new diagram onto the board, that this was simply the shape of an idle mind looking for something to worry at, the same way it had once worried at an assessor's double-check in an Ashford guild hall over a matter that had, in the end, turned out to be exactly as significant as it felt at the time and not one particle less.

He filed it anyway, the way he filed everything he didn't have room to examine properly, and told himself he'd know if it mattered.

Maret worked the diagram for the better part of the hour — a return-path failure two branches deep, the kind of problem that used to take Aldric a full minute to see and now took him closer to ten seconds, which he counted, privately, as one of the term's few uncomplicated victories. He answered when called on, watched Rynn's thread flare and steady under Maret's flat corrections, and managed, for most of an hour, to think about circuits instead of corridors.

It came back the moment the bell released them.

Nessa Croft was gathering her things two benches over with the brisk efficiency she brought to everything, and Aldric found himself angling toward her before he'd fully decided to, on the theory — reasonable, he told himself — that a girl whose mother heard things before the Council's own runners did was a faster route to an answer than waiting for Bavel to volunteer one.

"Can I ask you something political?"

"You can ask." Nessa didn't look up from her satchel. "Whether I answer depends entirely on how political."

"Capture operation. Outside Valdris Prime. Concluded as of this morning. Mean anything to you?"

That got her attention, briefly and precisely — a flicker of something crossing her face too fast to name before the practiced neutrality closed back over it, the same neutrality she wore into every conversation that might eventually be repeated to her mother. "Where did you hear that?"

"A corridor. Secondhand. Possibly thirdhand by the time it reached me."

"Then I'd leave it at secondhand." She shouldered her bag, and for a moment Aldric thought that was the whole answer he was going to get. Then, quieter, with none of her usual bluntness: "My mother's runner came at dawn instead of the usual hour. She hasn't told me why. She tells me most things eventually, when she's decided which parts are safe to have said out loud." A pause, weighed rather than hesitant. "This might not be one of those."

"That's not an answer."

"No," Nessa agreed. "It's the shape of one. I'm told that's occasionally useful." And she was gone before he could ask her to be less economical about it, off toward whatever her own morning required, leaving him with exactly as many facts as he'd started with and a new, considerably less comfortable sense of their size.

"That went well," Solen observed, arriving at his shoulder with the specific flat cheer he reserved for other people's failed information-gathering.

"She told me nothing."

"She told you her mother's runner came at dawn instead of the usual hour, which is not nothing, it's simply nothing *phrased* as nothing. You should be more suspicious of people who are good at that. I'd know."

"You're insufferable."

"I'm informative. There's an overlap, but they're not the same word." Solen fell into step beside him as the corridor refilled, unhurried, cataloguing. "For what it's worth, I don't think it's nothing either. I just don't have anything to put in the gap yet."

"Neither do I."

"That makes two of us, which is roughly the least satisfying number of people to share an unanswered question with. Three, at least, you can argue about it properly."

Rynn caught them up outside the lecture hall, mid-complaint about something Maret had said regarding return paths, and the corridor swallowed the last of the morning's strangeness the way it swallowed everything eventually — into banter, into the next class, into the ordinary machinery of a term still determined to finish itself on schedule whether or not anyone inside it had quite caught up.

Aldric let it go. He had learned, across two years at Greyveil, a fairly reliable rule about mornings like this one: most of what unsettled him for an hour turned out, by evening, to have unsettled him for no better reason than an idle mind with too little to do and too much practice noticing things. He told himself that now, walking toward Elemental Foundations with Rynn's voice rising and falling beside him and Solen's silence doing its own quieter work on the other side.

He had been wrong about that particular reassurance before. He had simply never yet had cause, in the moment itself, to notice.
