# Chapter 14: Solen Was Already Watching

### Saga 1, Arc 4

---
— Aldric Vane —

By noon, the story of the punch had a chair in it.

Nobody had picked up a chair. Aldric had been standing four feet away.

"—and then Rynn, *Wren*, something, hit Halvon so hard he flew across the room—" said a boy by the stairwell.

"He didn't fly," said the girl next to him.

"He *staggered* across the room," the boy insisted.

"He took one step," Aldric said, to no one, and kept walking.

By the third retelling, there was a second punch. By the fourth, Aldric was in it.

"—the Ashford one. Stepped right between them and told them the office would want a list of who was standing where. Like he'd read the procedure," said a girl leaning against the wall.

"Had he?" asked the boy beside her.

"Knows it by heart, apparently."

Aldric had read the curfew card twice. He supposed that counted as a related field.

Crest came down the corridor in the middle of the fourth retelling.

The lip had been treated, and treated well; only a slight fullness gave it away. Milo trailed a step further back than yesterday. Seya walked on Crest's other side, studying the ceiling, the walls, the window, everything except Aldric.

Crest didn't look at him either.

Yesterday, he'd chosen his target. Today, he was avoiding one.

Aldric didn't enjoy that as much as he'd expected to.

"Vane."

The junior administrator from the common space stood at the end of the corridor with the face of a man on his fourth errand of the morning. "The office wants your account. Now."

"I expected that."

"Did you."

"I did suggest it."

The administrator did not laugh. He walked. Aldric followed, and a few steps on the man said, without turning, "There's a conduct hearing on the table for your friend."

Aldric's stride didn't change. Everything else did. "My what?"

"Striking a noble student. Suspension is on the table. It depends on the witnesses."

"How many have you got?"

"Two. You'd make three." A beat. "It depends what you say."

*Friend.* Aldric had known Rynn for about ten words and one punch. He decided this was not the moment to correct the terminology.

"East stair. Third door. There's a bench. Sit." The administrator peeled off, and Aldric took the stairs faster than he'd have admitted.

---

The corridor was narrow and quiet: one window at the far end, one bench on the left, doors on the right. The flagstones were worn into a trough down the middle, by everyone who had ever waited here to be told something.

He counted paces out of habit. *Seven, eight—*

"Nine," said a voice. "Ten, if you take the last one carefully."

Aldric stopped.

There was a boy on the bench.

Aldric had looked at that bench when he turned the corner. He'd put it in the catalogue as *bench, empty.*

The catalogue did not get things wrong.

The boy was *neat*, and that was nearly all there was to him. Average height. Sandy hair cut exactly. A collar in some minor house's colour. Everything sat so precisely in place that the eye had nothing to catch on and slid right off. He looked like someone who had been arranged not to be remembered.

It was working. That bothered Aldric more than he'd have liked.

"You looked straight at me from the corner," the boy said.

"I looked at the bench."

"That's what I said."

He unfolded the sheet in his hands, squared one corner against his knee, and folded it again. Then, as if it hadn't gone right, he did it a second time.

"Solen."

That was the whole name. In a building where nobles led with their houses the way other people led with *hello*, it was either an oversight or a decision.

Aldric didn't think this boy oversaw things.

"Aldric."

"Vane. Ashford. Four days south, population two hundred, depending on the season." Flat, like reading a figure back. "I was by the gate yesterday. The south window, today."

Aldric went back through the common space in his head. The south window was in it. Light. A chair. The courtyard beyond the glass.

A hole in the catalogue, the shape of a person.

"You timed it," Solen said, as if returning something Aldric had dropped. "Eleven seconds from the end of Halvon's remark to the strike. Roughly ninety from the strike to the administrator. Your comment took up most of them."

"You *counted*?"

"It's a habit. Not a talent." Corner, square. "Halvon built the second remark from the same frame as the first, with the padding off. The first failed quietly. He could survive that. The second failed loudly."

It was accurate. It was more accurate than anything Aldric had worked out himself, and he hated how much he wanted to say so.

"Before Rynn moved, you shifted your weight," Solen went on. "Left foot, an inch forward. You were about to speak."

"I was about to breathe."

"You were about to say something. I'd like to know what."

"It's on the record. What I said instead."

"That isn't what I asked."

"I know."

Solen looked at him. Grey eyes, level. They didn't want anything. They just read, patiently, like a finger going down a page.

Aldric had spent his whole life being the one who read the room. Nobody had ever read him back.

He held still. He gave the page nothing to hold.

Solen nodded slightly, as if that, too, were data. "I gave my statement an hour ago. They offered to let me leave. I said I'd wait for the third account."

"Why?"

"To see whether you'd tell it straight or tell it kind."

"Which did you expect?"

"Neither. That's why I stayed."

Aldric glanced at the door. Glanced back. "Why were you watching? Before the punch."

Solen considered it like a fair question.

"I haven't finished working that out."

The door opened.

"Vane."

---

The office was small and mostly ledger. Aldric gave his account the way he'd have mapped it: who stood where, who said what, the split lip, the four feet between them. Crest's remark, word for word, because he remembered it word for word. He added no adjectives.

Somewhere behind the closed back door, Rynn was waiting to find out what those words were worth.

When Aldric finished, the stylus had stopped moving.

"You remember it exactly."

"I remember most things."

"It's very close to the previous account. Almost word for word."

*Previous.* Aldric thought of a folded page. A corner squared twice.

"Then it's a coincidence of good memory," he said.

The administrator glanced at the ledger. "Two consistent accounts, given separately. A strike against a noble goes to a conduct hearing as a matter of course. On these facts, this one won't." He looked up. "Written reprimand, on record, with the circumstances noted beside it. You and the earlier witness are the reason."

Aldric let out a breath he hadn't known he was holding. Rynn was staying.

"And Halvon?"

"His remarks, as reported, don't amount to a breach. They're commentary."

"On my bloodline."

"Commentary."

He said it the way you set down something you hadn't chosen to carry. Aldric filed it. Once, this chapter. Under *Greyveil*.

"Thank you," he said, which was the correct thing to say, and left.

---

Solen was still on the bench.

Aldric sat beside him, an arm's length of wood between them. Neither spoke. Solen squared his page again. It was, by now, the most exact piece of paper in the academy.

The back door opened, and Rynn came out like weather.

He stopped. The flush was gone, replaced by something worse, a stricken red. He held a slip of paper in front of him as if it might go off.

"I'M SORRY."

The corridor absorbed this.

"You could lower it," Aldric said.

"I *AM*." Rynn checked. The volume hadn't changed. He tried again, visibly straining. "I'm sorry. I meant to hit him, I'll be honest, but I didn't mean for you to have to stand in the middle of it—" He ran out of sentence and looked at the slip. "Written reprimand. On file. Two weeks of evening conduct duty. No suspension." He read it again. "That's not as bad as it sounded in there."

"Less bad than it was going to be."

"Why?"

"Someone gave a very accurate statement."

Rynn looked at Aldric. At the boy on the bench. At Aldric again. "Who's this?"

"A witness," Solen said.

"A *what*?"

"Yours, mostly. It was accurate." Level, no heat. "You struck him with the flat of your knuckles. That's why he's standing and you're on report. A closed fist and we'd all be having a different conversation." He glanced at the slip. "Rynn, I believe."

"How does everyone know my name?"

"It was on the report."

"I'd do it again," Rynn said, with some dignity.

"That," Solen said, "is the problem."

Rynn turned to Aldric, with the desperate look of a man handed a stranger he doesn't know how to hold. "Is he always like this?"

"I met him nine minutes ago."

"Eleven," Solen said.

"Eleven."

Something twitched at the corner of Solen's mouth. It was gone before Aldric could swear to it. The page needed squaring again.

"I didn't do it for you," Solen told Rynn, almost gently, as though the point mattered more than the courtesy. "I want that understood. Accuracy was all I had to give, so I gave that."

Rynn frowned at this for a long moment, working it through with his whole face.

"That's the weirdest way anyone's ever helped me," he said. "Thanks."

"You needn't."

"Doing it anyway."

Solen inclined his head a quarter inch. It seemed to be all the acknowledgement he had.

Somewhere below, the next bell rang. Rynn remembered, suddenly and completely, that he had somewhere to be. He said "*Right*," and "Later," and "Thanks again," in that order, far too loudly, and took the east stair two at a time.

---

Aldric stood.

"Later, Vane," Solen said, quietly.

Aldric turned to answer.

The bench was empty.

He hadn't seen Solen rise. Hadn't heard him go. Light lay across the flagstones, and the trough in the stone held nothing but the wear of everyone who had ever waited there.

He knew the shape of an empty space. He'd learned it on a bench in an assessment hall, a few days ago.

This one was a different shape. This one had a word left in it.

Aldric read rooms for a living. It was how he'd survived Ashford, and Valdris Prime, and one very long morning.

Somebody had just read him back.

And he hadn't felt it happen.
