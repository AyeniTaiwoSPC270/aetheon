# Chapter 46: The Attack

### Saga 1, Arc 6

---
— Aldric Vane —


The post-tournament gathering was the first entirely unstructured hour Aldric had spent in four days, and he was still deciding what to do with that freedom when the first scream told him the decision had been made for him.

It came from the direction of the refreshment tables — not a scream of fear exactly, more confusion, the sound of a crowd that had just noticed something wrong and hadn't yet worked out what — and Aldric was moving before he'd finished processing it, the old Ashford instinct arriving faster than thought, the same one that had once put his hand between Josse and a Greyback without asking his permission first.

"Stay with Rynn," he said to Solen, already three steps away.

"I was about to say the same thing to you."

The courtyard beyond the refreshment tables had gone wrong in a way that took Aldric a full second to actually parse, because it didn't look like an attack at first. It looked like staff — the tournament's own catering and grounds crew, familiar green-and-grey uniforms Aldric had walked past a dozen times that day without a second glance — moving with a coordination that no ordinary staff moved with, fanning out through the thinning crowd in a pattern that had nothing to do with clearing tables.

*Disguised,* he understood, all at once, the way he understood most things that mattered. *They walked in wearing the one uniform nobody in a crowd this size would ever think to question.*

Someone near the tables shouted a name he didn't catch. A table went over, glassware shattering, and the scream from a moment ago multiplied into several, the crowd's confused stillness cracking all at once into something closer to panic — except panic, Aldric noticed even as he moved through it, was exactly wrong for what was actually happening. The five figures in staff uniforms weren't attacking the crowd at all. They were moving through it, past it, around it, with the specific economy of people who'd already decided the crowd wasn't the point.

They were moving toward the faculty dais.

Toward Principal Solm.

— Edrath Solm —


Edrath had spent twenty-two years at Greyveil learning to read a room the way other men read a book, and the moment the first table went over, he understood two things simultaneously, with the particular calm that had gotten him through every crisis this office had ever handed him.

The first was that this was not chaos. Chaos didn't move in formation. Chaos didn't wear five identical, correctly tailored uniforms and cut through a panicking crowd without once colliding with anyone in it.

The second was that they were coming for him, specifically, and that the reason why was not one he could share with a single person in this courtyard without making the situation considerably worse for everyone in it.

He had, at most, perhaps four seconds before the nearest of them closed the distance across the dais, and he spent every one of them doing the only thing his position actually equipped him to do — not fighting, not yet, but buying time for the people who could. "Faculty to the dais," he said, pitched to carry without shouting, the voice he used for assemblies and evacuations and every other moment that required calm to spread faster than fear. "Students, clear the courtyard. Now. Not toward the gates — toward the east cloister. Move."

It was, he thought with the small, detached part of his mind that never entirely stopped calculating even in a crisis, a remarkably good crowd for this. Two terms of curfew drills and conduct codes had apparently taught this year's students to actually listen to an instruction shouted with real authority behind it, and the courtyard began, messily but genuinely, to empty in the direction he'd pointed.

The five in staff uniforms did not chase the crowd. They had never been interested in the crowd. They came directly for the dais, and for him, with a discipline that told him more about who had sent them than anything he'd learn from their faces.

*Not the faction proper. Not their pattern. This is someone smaller, working alone, spending resources they don't have their masters' permission to spend.*

He did not have time to wonder who, specifically. He barely had time to raise his own guard before the first of them was on him.

— Aldric Vane —


Aldric reached the edge of the dais at the exact moment Solm's guard went up — a controlled, precise ward that Aldric recognized, distantly, as considerably more refined than anything he'd been taught in two years of Combat Application — and watched, for one suspended second, an A-rank mage meet a disguised attacker's opening strike and hold it, cleanly, without visible effort.

It wasn't enough. There were five of them, and Solm, for all his rank, was one man standing between them and whatever they'd actually come here to do.

Aldric didn't think about what came next. He'd learned, across two years and three uncontrolled bursts, that thinking about it was usually the thing that made it worse. He simply moved, the way he'd moved at Ashford, the way he'd moved in the final an hour ago, placing himself in the gap between the second attacker's line and Solm's exposed flank without any plan beyond the plain, animal certainty that the gap needed to not be a gap anymore.

The attacker who found him there hesitated — a fraction of a second, no more, the specific pause of someone whose plan had not accounted for a first-year student choosing, for no tactical reason at all, to be somewhere he had no business being. Aldric used the hesitation the only way he knew how. He didn't reach for the thing in his chest. He didn't need to, not yet. He threw a plain Mana Bolt, aimed low, at the attacker's leading foot rather than any target that made sense, and bought himself exactly as much time as Torval's own instincts had once bought Rynn — not a win, just a moment, just enough of one.

It was enough. Bavel arrived from somewhere on Aldric's left with an expression that had none of his usual dry economy left in it at all, and put the staggered attacker down with a spell Aldric didn't recognize and didn't have time to admire. Two more faculty members Aldric didn't know by name converged from the opposite side. The fight, such as it had been, was over in less time than it had taken Aldric to fully understand it was happening — five disguised attackers reduced, in the space of perhaps thirty seconds, to three fleeing figures and two others already restrained, faculty robes and staff uniforms tangled together in a scene that would take considerably longer to make sense of than it had taken to resolve.

Solm was unhurt. Aldric confirmed this the only way that mattered to him in the moment — a glance, direct, the Principal's eyes finding his across the wreckage of the dais and holding there for a beat too long to be simple acknowledgment.

"You shouldn't have been here," Solm said. Not unkindly. Almost gently, in fact, in a voice that had none of the authority it had carried thirty seconds earlier.

"I wasn't planning to be. It happened faster than planning."

"That's usually how it happens." Solm's eyes moved, briefly, toward the treeline beyond the courtyard wall — the same treeline, Aldric realized with a cold, settling certainty, that he'd caught himself watching an hour earlier during the ceremony — and something crossed his face that he didn't let stay there long enough for anyone to read properly. "Go and find your friends. Make sure they're safe. That matters more right now than anything you could ask me."

"I wasn't going to ask you anything."

"You were about to. I've had considerable practice noticing that particular look." Solm's voice, for just a moment, carried something almost warm underneath its exhaustion. "Later, Vane. Not tonight."

— Edrath Solm —


He watched the boy go, weaving back through the wreckage toward the east cloister where the rest of the students had gathered, and allowed himself, for exactly as long as it took Aldric to pass out of sight, the luxury of feeling something other than calm.

*He put himself between me and an attacker with no reason to and no idea what either of us actually was.*

It was, Edrath thought, watching the last of the fleeing figures disappear over the outer wall before faculty pursuit could close the distance, either the bravest thing he'd witnessed from a first-year in twenty-two years at this institution, or the single most alarming, and he suspected, standing amid the broken glass and overturned tables of what had been, an hour ago, a celebration, that it was probably both at once.

Bavel reached him a moment later, breathing hard, already scanning the two restrained attackers with the flat, assessing look he gave everything. "They're not talking. Won't be surprised if they can't — this has the shape of people who were told very little, on purpose, by whoever actually sent them."

"I know."

"You know who sent them."

"I have a very strong suspicion, and no proof at all, and until that changes, I intend to say nothing to anyone, including you." Edrath allowed himself a breath, the first genuinely unguarded one since the first table had gone over. "Get the two of them somewhere secure. Quietly. I don't want this becoming the story the entire kingdom takes home from what should have been a celebration."

"And the boy?"

Edrath didn't need to ask which boy Bavel meant. There was, tonight, only ever going to be one boy either of them meant.

"The boy," he said, "put himself in front of me without knowing why anyone would want to hurt me at all. I don't think that's a coincidence I can afford to keep filing away and not examining. Not anymore." He looked once more toward the treeline, empty now, betraying nothing of what had briefly moved along it. "Something out there already knows more about him than we've told him. Tonight is the first time it's come close enough to prove it."

He did not know, standing amid the wreckage with the last of the evening's noise settling into a very different kind of quiet, whether that was the end of something or only the very beginning of it. He suspected, with the same cold certainty that had gotten him through every crisis this office had ever handed him, that it was almost certainly the second.
