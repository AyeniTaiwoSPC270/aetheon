### Arc 1: The Village Years (Chapters 1–5)
**Review:** Aldric Vane, age 17, lives in Ashford with adoptive parents Maren (healer) and Josse (carpenter). He is ordinary by village standards but carries an unidentified mana pressure that manifests sporadically. His closest friend Daran is ambitious, detail-oriented, and obsessed with academy entrance. A traveling mage visits the harvest market, triggering Aldric's awareness that power exists beyond the village. The arc culminates in Aldric receiving an acceptance letter to the formal academy examination in Valdris Prime. The tone is intimate, domestic, and grounded in the texture of small-town life. Key hook: the pressure sensation Aldric experiences is not explained—it simply is, and he files it away rather than investigating it.

### Arc 2: The Incident (Chapters 6–7)
**Review:** En route to Valdris Prime, Aldric and Daran's cart breaks down. While waiting, Aldric experiences a breakthrough: the pressure sensation manifests as visible mana discharge, a shimmer of light between his fingers. Daran witnesses this and says, "It didn't look like you." The moment is not celebrated—it is unsettling, and Aldric immediately compartmentalizes it. They continue to the city. The arc establishes that Aldric's power is not under his control and that witnesses to his advancement will react with unease rather than congratulation. Key hook: the nature of Aldric's mana (why it manifests differently, why it frightened Daran) remains unexplained.

### Arc 3: The Academy Application (Chapters 8–13)
**Review:** Aldric and Daran arrive in Valdris Prime and take the formal academy examination. The exam has three components: written theory, practical mana assessment, and combat application. Aldric passes all three and is accepted to Greyveil Academy. Daran fails the practical component—his mana output is below threshold—and is rejected. This is the first major divergence in their paths. Aldric departs for Greyveil Academy via convoy; Daran's fate after rejection is not shown. At Greyveil, Aldric is assigned to a dormitory, meets his first-year cohort, and begins Combat Application training under instructor Bavel. In the common space, a noble student named Crest Halvon makes a contemptuous comment about commoner students who are "self-taught" from "pamphlets and guesswork." Rynn, a commoner student with high mana output, physically confronts Crest. Aldric intervenes with a cryptic statement ("You're not the problem") that defuses the situation. Rynn is taken to the administrative office; Aldric and Rynn exchange a brief conversation that suggests the beginning of an alliance. Key hooks: (1) Daran's rejection and disappearance; (2) the nature of Aldric's mana and why it manifests differently; (3) the social tension between noble and commoner students; (4) Aldric's emerging role as a stabilizing force; (5) the identity and significance of Crest Halvon's hostility.

### Arc 4: Foundation Year Begins (Chapters 14+)
**Continuation path:** The narrative now enters the institutional phase. Aldric must navigate Greyveil Academy's module system, which includes Combat Application, Mana Theory, Circuit Cultivation, and Practical Integration. The first-year cohort is stratified by social class and pre-academy training. Aldric's advantage is not raw power output but adaptability and the ability to read situations—skills developed in Ashford through observation rather than instruction. 

**Immediate plot drivers:**
1. **Rynn's administrative consequence** and whether Aldric's intervention creates debt or alliance
2. **Crest Halvon's escalation**—the first move failed; the second move will be more calculated
3. **Seya's observation**—she noted that Crest's move did not land as intended; her next action will depend on whether she sees Aldric as a threat or an asset
4. **The pressure sensation**—Aldric has not experienced it since arriving at Greyveil; the academy environment may suppress it, trigger it, or transform it
5. **Daran's absence**—no word from Daran; Aldric will eventually need to confront whether Daran survived rejection or disappeared into the city
6. **Module progression**—the written theory component will require Aldric to learn the official framework for mana cultivation; his self-taught understanding may conflict with institutional doctrine
7. **The elf student**—introduced in Chapter 11, she has not yet been named or integrated into the cohort; her outsider status mirrors Aldric's but with a different vector (species rather than class)

**Pacing structure for Arc 4 (Chapters 14–50, estimated):**
- Chapters 14–16: Settlement and module integration. Aldric attends his first Mana Theory lecture, learns the official tier system, and begins to understand that his pressure sensation may correspond to a documented stage of cultivation. Rynn returns from administrative office with a reprimand but no expulsion. First conversation between Aldric and Rynn establishes mutual respect. Seya makes a deliberate choice to interact with Aldric, testing his response.
- Chapters 17–22: First module assessments. Combat Application produces a ranking; Aldric places in the middle tier, neither exceptional nor struggling. Mana Theory written exam reveals gaps in his knowledge but also unexpected insights. Practical Integration (hands-on circuit work) is where Aldric begins to outpace expectations—his intuitive understanding of mana flow exceeds students with higher output but less adaptability.
- Chapters 23–28: Social dynamics crystallize. Crest's third move is more subtle—not direct contempt but structural exclusion. He begins to dominate group project assignments, ensuring commoner students are separated or marginalized. Aldric, Rynn, and Seya naturally form a study group; the elf student (name: Kael) joins without asking, and they accept her without comment. This group becomes the counterweight to Crest's cluster.
- Chapters 29–35: First breakthrough trigger. During a Practical Integration session, Aldric's pressure sensation returns—stronger, more controlled. He manages a minor circuit opening that the instructor (different from Bavel) recognizes as Stage 1 cultivation. The achievement is noted but not celebrated; it places him in a new category. Rynn, who has higher output but less control, has not yet achieved Stage 1. This creates the first real tension in their alliance—Aldric is advancing faster in the metric that matters.
- Chapters 36–42: Daran reappears. A letter arrives, or Aldric encounters him in the city during a supervised outing. Daran has not disappeared—he has found work, possibly with the guild system or as an independent practitioner. The reunion is complicated: Daran is proud and defensive about his rejection, Aldric is careful not to emphasize his acceptance, but the gap between them is now structural. Daran may offer information about the city or the broader power structure that Aldric needs.
- Chapters 43–50: Mid-foundation assessment. The first-year cohort takes a comprehensive exam covering all modules. Aldric's results are strong but not exceptional in any single area—he is a generalist in a system that rewards specialization. This is both his weakness and his eventual strength. Crest's results are exceptional in theory and combat but weaker in practical circuit work, suggesting his noble training has emphasized certain paths over others. The assessment results trigger the next phase of social reorganization and the introduction of second-year mentorship pairings.

**Rhythm principles for Arc 4:**
- **Training montages** (weeks of module attendance) alternate with **detailed breakthrough scenes** (the moment Aldric achieves Stage 1 cultivation, the moment he realizes his intuitive advantage over trained students).
- **Internal struggle escalates with power**: Aldric's Stage 1 achievement brings pride, which brings temptation to leverage it, which brings the risk of making enemies. His advantage is not strength but adaptability; using it requires restraint.
- **Peer rivalry with Crest maintains tension**: Crest is not a villain—he is a noble student operating within a system that has always favored him. His hostility toward Aldric is not personal but structural. As Aldric advances, Crest's contempt becomes more dangerous because it becomes more targeted.
- **New threat types emerge**: The academy is not a place where physical strength determines hierarchy. Political maneuvering, information control, and the ability to read social systems matter more. Aldric's village skills (observation, patience, reading people) become his primary assets.
- **No power loss, only complications**: Aldric's Stage 1 achievement is permanent. But it creates new problems—higher expectations, the attention of instructors, the jealousy of peers who are still baseline.
