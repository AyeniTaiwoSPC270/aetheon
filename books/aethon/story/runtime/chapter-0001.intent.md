### Body
## Current task
Aldric arrives in Valdris Prime for the first time and walks into Greyveil Academy's registration hall, where the intake examiner's public reading of his mana score forces him to stand exposed before a room full of noble applicants who immediately mark him as nothing.

## What the reader is waiting for right now
1. The reader has no prior expectation — this is the opening; they are scanning for a reason to stay, a protagonist worth following, a world with weight.
2. This chapter does not pay anything off — it opens the central wound: a boy with the wrong face (white hair, red eyes, commoner papers) in the wrong room, whose mana reads strange and low, and who does not flinch. The gap between what the room sees and what the reader suspects is the hook that carries them into chapter 2.

## To pay off / to keep buried
- Pay off: nothing — this is the opening; no prior hooks exist to resolve
- Keep buried: the true nature of Aldric's mana anomaly (the shimmer, the silver shift) — suppress until the Arc 4 breakthrough; this chapter shows only the surface strangeness, no explanation
- Keep buried: Aldric's noble bloodline — no hint this chapter; the room reads him as a commoner and so does the reader, for now

## What the slow / transitional beats carry
- [Opening scene, Aldric on foot entering Valdris Prime's Common Ring] → establishes the world's class geography in one sensory sweep — the smell of the Common Ring vs. the clean stone of the Crown District — without naming it as exposition; plants the city as a character
- [Registration hall interior, Aldric in the queue] → introduces the noble-commoner hierarchy through observed behavior (who gets waved to the front, who gets made to wait), not through narration; Aldric watches, calibrates, says nothing
- [The examiner's reading] → the chapter's pressure point; Aldric's mana reads low but strange — the examiner pauses, consults the instrument twice, says nothing useful; the room notices; Crest Halvon's name is spoken once, casually, as the boy who gets the highest reading and makes sure everyone hears it; Aldric is admitted because the instrument does not say "fail," only "anomalous"

## Three-question check on the key choice
- Protagonist's most important choice this chapter:
  - When the examiner pauses and the room goes quiet and someone laughs, Aldric does not perform dignity or suppress anger — he simply waits, eyes forward, until the examiner writes "admitted" on the form.
  - Why this choice? Aldric's suppression-dominant register means he does not spend energy on rooms that haven't earned it; he is not performing calm, he genuinely does not need the room's approval yet.
  - Does it match current interest? Yes — getting through the door is the only goal; anything else costs him the entry.
  - Does it match their persona? Yes — dry, still, internally watchful; the humor surfaces only in the margin (one beat, one line of internal observation about the instrument's hesitation), never at the center of the tension.
- Antagonist / supporting cast's most important choice this chapter:
  - Crest Halvon (named, not yet expanded) makes a single public remark after Aldric's anomalous reading — loud enough to carry, casual enough to be deniable — that frames Aldric as a curiosity or a mistake.
  - Why this choice? Crest has nothing to lose and social capital to gain by establishing the room's hierarchy on day one; Aldric is the easiest target.
  - Does it match current interest? Yes — Crest is performing dominance for an audience of noble peers; the remark costs him nothing and buys him position.
  - Does it match their persona? Yes — contemptuous, not cruel; he is not a villain yet, just a boy who has never been told the room doesn't belong to him.

## Required end-of-chapter change
- Information change: Aldric is inside the academy — the door that could have been closed is open, but the room has already decided what he is
- Relationship change: Crest Halvon has registered Aldric as beneath notice; Aldric has registered Crest as a data point, not a threat — asymmetric and unstable
- Emotional gap (hook): Aldric, alone in the assigned dormitory corridor at chapter's end, looks at the admission form — "anomalous" is the examiner's only written word — and the reader does not know whether that word is a sentence or a key
