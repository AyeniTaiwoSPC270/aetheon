## Chapter goal
Close the village arc — the letter arrives, Ashford ends

## Current task
The formal acceptance letter arrives at the Vane household, forcing the last ordinary day in Ashford to become the first day of departure — Aldric must say a real goodbye to the village, to Daran, and to the version of himself that still belongs here.

## What the reader is waiting for right now
1. The reader expects the acceptance letter and Daran's parallel result to arrive soon, and anticipates the first crack in the "we'll both get in" promise — the wound is primed.
2. This chapter delivers a partial payoff: Aldric's letter arrives and is confirmed; Daran's result is either absent or its content is withheld from Aldric, widening the gap — the reader sees the fracture forming but the rupture is deferred to Ch. 6–7.

## To pay off / to keep buried
- Pay off: SP-03 (Aldric's domestic life in Ashford) — full close; the village as home is rendered complete and then relinquished; this arc ends here
- Pay off: H-04 (Maren's unqualified certainty) — partial; when the letter arrives Maren's reaction is disproportionately unsurprised, exactly as seeded; she does not explain herself, but the gap between her calm and what the moment should feel like is now undeniable
- Keep buried: H-03 (what Josse knows) — suppress until Arc 1 close or Arc 2; Josse is present and warm but gives nothing away this chapter
- Keep buried: H-05 (the assessor's note) — not touched; institutional consequences belong to the academy arc
- Keep buried: H-02 (the broken promise) — Daran's result is not confirmed on the page; the reader knows something is wrong but the word "rejected" is not spoken this chapter; rupture deferred to Ch. 6–7

## What the slow / transitional beats carry
[Opening — morning before letter] → establishes the texture of an ordinary Ashford day that Aldric is now watching from a slight remove; sensory catalogue (the specific smell of Maren's workroom, the sound of Josse's boots on the stone step, the quality of light through the kitchen window) functions as foreshadow of loss and as the reader's last full immersion in the domestic world before it closes

[Letter delivery — midpoint] → the physical object (sealed, kingdom-channel courier, heavier than expected) lands as the chapter's pivot; Maren's reaction is the beat that pays off H-04 — she reads the seal before Aldric does, her hands do not tremble, she sets it on the table rather than handing it to him; that small sequencing is the tell

[Aldric reads alone] → brief private moment; fifth pressure event occurs — this one is not involuntary and not frightening; it is almost anticipatory, a warmth rather than a weight; this advances H-01 without resolving it and plants the contrast between the anomaly-as-threat framing and the anomaly-as-response framing

[Daran scene — late afternoon] → Aldric goes to find Daran; Daran is not at home or is present but the conversation does not surface his result; what the reader gets instead is the two boys together in the eastern field one last time — the pamphlet is mentioned, the afternoon light is the same as Ch. 1, the parallel is deliberate; Daran says something that will read differently on reread once his rejection is known; the promise "we'll both get in" is not repeated but its ghost is in the scene

[Final night — Vane household] → Josse and Maren at the table; no large speeches; Josse gives Aldric one concrete, practical thing (not a revelation, not an explanation — a tool, an object, a habit); Maren does not cry; the household breathing sound from Ch. 3's close is mirrored here but now Aldric is the one awake listening to it as something he is about to leave; closes SP-03

## Three-question check on the key choice
- Protagonist's most important choice this chapter:
  - Aldric chooses to go find Daran after the letter arrives rather than staying in the house with his parents — he moves toward the friendship before the fracture becomes visible
  - Why this choice? The letter confirms his future; the instinct is to share it with the person the future was supposed to include; suppression-dominant register means he will not perform the emotion at home
  - Does it match current interest? Yes — Aldric's interest is in preserving parity with Daran for as long as it holds; going to Daran is the last act of that preservation
  - Does it match their persona? Yes — dry, action-over-declaration; he does not announce the letter, he just goes; the gesture is the feeling

- Antagonist / supporting cast's most important choice this chapter:
  - Maren's choice: she does not explain her unsurprised reaction to the letter; she absorbs the moment quietly and lets Aldric have it without making it about her knowledge
  - Why this choice? Her certainty (H-04) is not performed certainty — it is the certainty of someone who already knew; explaining it would collapse the hook prematurely
  - Does it match current interest? Yes — her interest is in Aldric's departure being clean, not complicated by whatever she knows
  - Does it match their persona? Yes — Maren's established register is warm competence and deliberate restraint; she treated the neighbour's wrist injury with the same economy of motion

## Required end-of-chapter change
- Information change: Aldric's acceptance is now confirmed and concrete — he is no longer a boy who might go to the academy; he is a boy who leaves in days
- Relationship change: the Aldric-Daran scene closes their village-era dynamic without either boy naming what is shifting; the friendship is intact but the reader knows it will not survive the next chapter intact
- Physical change: SP-03 formally closes — the domestic world of Ashford is rendered and relinquished; the village ceases to be the story's home ground
