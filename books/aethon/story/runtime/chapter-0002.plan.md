## Chapter goal
Demonstrate Aldric's edge through one concrete, witnessed event

## Current task
Aldric, during an unstructured moment in Ashford (the day after the field session), attempts the pamphlet hand-position again in private — and this time the pressure does not flicker and die but holds, visibly, long enough for one other person to see it before Aldric shuts it down.

## What the reader is waiting for right now
1. The reader expects the mana flickers from Chapter 1 to remain hidden a little longer — a slow-burn mystery of Aldric practicing in secret, nothing yet exposed.
2. This chapter cracks that expectation open partway: the pressure holds this time, one witness catches the tail end of it, and Aldric's instinct is to deny and suppress — widening the gap rather than paying off, because the witness is not yet a threat or an ally the reader can fully read.

## To pay off / to keep buried
- Pay off: H-01 (mana pressure sensation) → partial only — the sensation becomes sustained and leaves a visible physical trace (pale shimmer on Aldric's hands, brief), but its nature and origin remain unexplained; no character names what they saw with any precision
- Keep buried: H-02 (Daran's "we'll both get in" promise) → suppress entirely this chapter; Daran does not appear; the promise sits undisturbed so its eventual fracture lands harder; revisit no earlier than Ch. 6–7

## What the slow / transitional beats carry
- [Opening, Aldric alone before dawn] → establishes the private ritual quality of his practice; seeds the sensory detail of the specific location (a back room, the cold floor, the smell of the house in early morning) that will be echoed when he later trains at the academy; functions as a contrast anchor
- [Hand-position attempt, pressure builds and holds] → the demonstration beat; this is where H-01 advances from "flicker" to "sustained shimmer"; the physical sensation is rendered in five-sense detail — weight in the sternum spreading outward to the fingertips, a faint hum Aldric feels in his back teeth, the pale white-silver light visible on his knuckles in the dim room
- [Witness enters, Aldric shuts it down] → the hook-generating beat; the witness (one named character, no expansion beyond a name and a single reaction line) sees enough to register something happened; Aldric's suppression is immediate and practiced-feeling, which is itself strange given he has almost no practice; the witness's reaction is unreadable — not alarm, not recognition, something quieter
- [Closing, Aldric alone again] → Aldric does not process what happened aloud or internally in full; he files it under "not yet"; the chapter ends on a sensory detail — the shimmer is gone but his hands are still warm — which is the hook forward

## Three-question check on the key choice
- Protagonist's most important choice this chapter:
  - **Choice:** Aldric shuts the pressure down the instant the witness appears, then says nothing about what they saw.
  - **Why this choice?** The two Chapter 1 flickers were both concealed from Daran by reflex; concealment is already his established pattern. He does not yet have language for what is happening, and he does not trust that language would help him even if he did.
  - **Does it match current interest?** Yes — he is not yet at the academy; drawing attention to an unexplained anomaly in Ashford serves no purpose and risks adult intervention that could complicate the application.
  - **Does it match their persona?** Yes — suppression-dominant register, dry deflection rather than confrontation; he would rather the moment not have happened than explain it.
- Antagonist / supporting cast's most important choice this chapter:
  - **Choice:** The witness says nothing beyond a single neutral line and leaves the room.
  - **Why this choice?** The witness does not have enough context to name what they saw, and their instinct is not to press Aldric on things he clearly does not want pressed.
  - **Does it match current interest?** Yes — pressing Aldric would create friction; the witness's interest is in the household's ordinary rhythm continuing.
  - **Does it match their persona?** Yes — the witness is a domestic figure (Maren or Josse Vane; writer chooses which fits the scene's light better), whose core tag is quiet attentiveness rather than interrogation; they see more than they say, always.

## Required end-of-chapter change
- Information change: one other person in the world now has a partial, unverified image of Aldric's mana manifesting — they have not named it, but they have seen it
- Physical change: Aldric's hands retain warmth after the shimmer closes, a new physical marker that was not present in Chapter 1's flickers — the pressure is developing, not static
