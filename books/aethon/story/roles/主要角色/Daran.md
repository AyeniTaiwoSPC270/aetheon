## Core_Tags
Obsessive, Ki-path specialist, driven by a broken promise rather than malice, emotionally legible only in retrospect — Daran is the series' most dangerous character because he is comprehensible; his choices make sense from inside his logic, which makes them harder to dismiss.

## Contrast_Detail
He trains until his hands bleed and then wraps them himself, neatly, without complaint — but he still remembers Aldric's mother's name and asks after her in the one letter he sends in Volume 2, which Aldric does not answer.

## Back_Story
Grew up alongside Aldric in Ashford. Sub-threshold mana channel — his body can generate mana but cannot sustain the pathways required for formal magic. The Greyveil rejection was not a surprise to anyone but him, because he had built an identity around the idea that Aldric's path was also his path. The broken promise — specific, named between them, never repeated to the reader until Volume 5 — is the engine. He goes to Ironmark because it is the only door still open, and he goes alone, which is the detail that matters.

## Protagonist_Arc
Starts as the childhood friend who got left behind. Ends as the person who came back — not redeemed, not villainous, but present, which is its own kind of answer. The arc's cost is that the friendship cannot be restored to what it was; what exists in Volume 8 is something different, harder, and more honest. His Obsidian rank by Saga 7 is the quantifiable marker of what pure Ki-path obsession produces when it has a personal wound as fuel.

## Current_State
At Chapter 14, Daran has already left Valdris Prime. He is somewhere on the road to Ironmark. He has not said goodbye properly. He is angrier than he has ever been and more disciplined than he has ever been, simultaneously, which is the condition Ironmark will refine into something the series will have to reckon with.

## Relationship_Network
Aldric: the person the entire arc is in conversation with, even when they are not in the same scene. Grevan Tusk: a shared mentor whose teachings Daran absorbed differently than Aldric did — Tusk taught them the same techniques, and they learned different things.

## Inner_Driver
He wants to prove that the path he was forced onto is not lesser — that Ki without mana is not a consolation prize but a different kind of ceiling, and that he can reach it. He is willing to pay any cost. He is not willing to admit that what he actually wants is for Aldric to have waited.

## Growth_Arc
Ironmark to Obsidian rank across Volumes 1-6; the wild-card turn in Volume 6; the return in Volume 7 as something that defies the series' established categories of ally and enemy.
