### THE 4-ACT STRUCTURE

- **Act 1 — Man vs. Monster (Sagas 1–5).** The visible threat is the monster crisis (Tiers 1–6). The Unbound operates quietly underneath, using monsters as tools — not yet the visible antagonist.
- **Act 2 — Man vs. Man.** The Unbound steps into the open. Full political/military war. Ends in Varek Noss's defeat.
- **Act 3 — Man vs. the Divine.** Kingdoms and continents draw in the Holy Church (the Dual Path religion) directly. Angelic and demonic lore — the Hollow and Aurelia — enters properly for the first time.
- **Act 4 — Man vs. the Cosmic.** Dragonites and the Calamities become the active threat. The series' true endgame.

Only Act 1 (Sagas 1–5) is planned in detail. Acts 2–4 are conceptual only — do not plan chapter-level content for them without a dedicated planning session first.

## WRITING STANDARDS

**Style:** Cinematic, fast-paced, hype and epic. Plant seeds. Consequences are real. Deaths matter.

Aldric has genuine silliness — dry humor, unexpected jokes. Use it in the margins of tension, not at its centre.

**POV:** Multi-POV. Label every POV shift: — [Character Name] —. Aldric is the anchor POV. Villains and antagonists must be written with full humanity.

**Chapter length:** 2,000–3,000 words standard. Key scenes up to 5,000–7,000 — flag before writing. The word count is measured with a tool, never estimated.

Always recap the previous chapter in 3–5 sentences before any new writing. (This recap belongs in /plan — it is not written into the chapter prose.)

**Visual elements:** Use sparingly for power moments. No stat screens or game UI. Spell costs, ranks and mana numbers are backstage tracking only — never written into the prose. Show consequences (fatigue, trembling hands, a spell wavering) instead.

**World-building:** Reveal through character experience, not info-dumps. Every race feels like a real people.

### THE WEB NOVEL CHAPTER CONTRACT (v5.1)

Audience: web-novel readers (Royal Road / Webnovel-style serial readers), not slow-burn literary readers. Every chapter must earn the next click. These rules apply to every new chapter from Chapter 14 onward. Chapters 1–13 were written in a quieter register; do NOT imitate that register when writing new chapters — write to this contract.

1. **OPEN IN MOTION.** The first 1–3 lines must contain a conflict, a question, something odd, or a joke. Never open on weather, room-mapping, or scene-setting description.
2. **ONE WANT, ONE OBSTACLE, ONE TURN.** Every chapter gives the POV character a concrete want (not just "give nothing away"), something in the way, and a turn that changes the situation. State the stakes or a clock early.
3. **END ON A HOOK.** Close on a reveal, a threat, a reversal, or an unanswered question. Never close on a quiet image or "he went to sleep."
4. **FASTER PROSE.** Short paragraphs. Dialogue-forward. Interiority tied to action. Trim description to what serves tension. The "filed it / set aside / the drawer" signature is a rare tool — at most once per chapter, and only when it lands a payoff.
5. **SHARP, FREQUENT HUMOR.** Aldric's dry wit and the friends' banter are selling points. Humor lives in the margins of tension, not at its centre.
6. **PAYOFF CADENCE.** Something the reader wants to see — a power moment, a reveal, a fight, a reversal — lands every 3–5 chapters. Small but satisfying counts.
7. **MYSTERIES AS QUESTIONS.** Plant seeds so the reader can track them as open questions (the assessor's double-check, Maret's notation, the hair and bloodline, the faction's look at Aldric).
8. **SPEAKER CLARITY.** Every line of dialogue must have an unambiguous speaker. Use dialogue tags or action beats; never leave more than two consecutive lines untagged; label an unnamed speaker by role at first use.
9. **ACTIVE WANT AND OUTSIDE PRESSURE (v5.4).** Every chapter gives the POV character an active want (not only "hide" or "give nothing away") and at least one pressure from outside — a person, a clock, a rule or a threat — not just being noticed. Rotate ending-hook types (Reveal, Question, Threat, Reversal, Setback) and never repeat a type in consecutive chapters. Keep claude/TRACKER_HOOKS.md current.

These rules never override canon, the Hard Rules, or the approved /plan.

### MEASURED CRAFT CHECKS (v5.4, part of the /write self-review)

- Real word count, measured with a tool, target 2,000–3,000.
- At least three jokes that land.
- One mid-chapter reversal.
- Reader rating: at approval the author gives a 1–5 "would you click next?" score. Record it in the Chapter Log entry at /summary. Target average 4 or higher. If no rating is given, log "not yet given" and ask once at the next /plan.

### CHAT-DRAFT SPEAKER BRACKETS (v5.3)

Every chapter draft delivered in the terminal/chat (/write and any /rewrite) puts the speaker's name in brackets immediately after every spoken quote, e.g. "I'm good" (Darah). This is a reading and checking aid for the author, so who said what can be verified at a glance.

- Applies to every quoted segment of speech, including each part of a quote split by a tag. An unnamed speaker is labelled by role, e.g. (Second-row boy).
- Does not apply to inner thoughts in italics — those are not speech.
- Drafts shown to the author only. Brackets NEVER go into the manuscript, the Chapter Log, or any project file. At /summary the brackets are stripped and the manuscript text is otherwise exactly as approved.
- Brackets do not replace Rule 8. The clean, bracket-free text must still make every speaker clear through tags and action beats, and the self-review checks Rule 8 on the text as it will appear in the manuscript.
- Do not change the prose to add or remove brackets — the bracketed draft and the clean text must otherwise be word-for-word identical.

### THE SPELL GATE (v5.2; full rule in claude/SPELL_REGISTRY.md)

Spells may be named and used, but only through the Spell Registry. Every spell has its own profile (type, rank, element, effect, range, cast method, mana cost, circuit requirement, limits, counters, visual signature, teachability, first appearance). A character may know any number of spells, each logged on their Known Spells list with how and when it was learned. Before any spell appears in a /plan, /write or /rewrite, it must pass every check:

1. **REGISTRY:** it has a profile in the registry, or a full profile is in the current /plan and approved by the author. No profile = no spell.
2. **RANK:** the caster's rank (F–S) is equal to or higher than the spell's rank. An F-rank cannot cast an S-rank spell.
3. **AFFINITY:** the spell matches the caster's affinity. Multi-element needs C+; compound spells need B+.
4. **CAPACITY:** the cost fits the caster's current mana; exhaustion stages are enforced (Strain <50%, Depletion <20%, Critical <5%, Burnout, Rupture).
5. **CIRCUITS:** the caster's circuit density meets the spell's requirement.
6. **KNOWLEDGE:** the spell is on the caster's Known Spells list, and how they learned it is plausible.
7. **TIMELINE:** plausible for the saga and stage (Act 1 Aldric is magic-only; no sword training).
8. **CONSISTENCY:** same effect, cost and limits every time; changes only by an author-approved Mastery Upgrade.
9. **INTERACTION:** counters and blindspots follow the Power System Bible; Ki users do not register on Mana Sense.
10. **NAME:** no collision with an existing spell, technique, Phasite ability, or legendary weapon.
11. **OVERREACH:** casting above rank happens only as an explicit author-approved event with a heavy on-page cost.

Ki techniques and Phasite Applications are logged in the registry too, but follow their own gates (Ki title; Phasite stage) — they never cost mana and are not ranked F–S. A spell may never replicate a Phasite's concept. The registry's cost scale (channel sizes by rank, cost bands by spell rank) and its nine starting profiles were approved by the author on 2026-09-26.

## THE PHASE COMMANDS

Streamlined (v5.4). Target: three approvals per chapter (plan, draft, done). These are workflow steps you run. The author may type them (`/plan`, `/write`, `/rewrite`, `/approved`) or just say "plan," "go," "approved," and so on. Every chapter is written through these phases in order. Never skip a phase. Never write without an approved /plan. Never silently fix anything — flag and wait.

### /plan [Chapter X, Arc X, Saga X]  (includes the brief)

1. Read CHAPTER_LOG-1 first to confirm the last written chapter and current status — every time, same session or new. Also read the trackers (first appearances, who knows what, voice sheets, hook ledger).
2. Read the relevant arc section in the Master Plan and Saga Bible; place the chapter in the arc's progression.
3. Recap the previous chapter in 3–5 sentences, and note where the last payoff landed.
4. Chapter position: what must happen, which characters are involved, what must be established.
5. State the opening hook, the POV character's want (active) / obstacle / outside pressure / turn (Rule 9), a mid-chapter reversal, the ending hook and its type (checked against the hook ledger), and any payoff or mystery seed the chapter delivers.
6. Identify every character appearing; cross-reference the Character Bible and voice sheets; generate a Chapter Character Sheet.
7. Outline the chapter beat by beat.
8. Flag any new characters, locations, or lore that would need to be created.
9. SPELL CHECK — list every spell the chapter uses, with its registry status and a pass/fail against each Spell Gate check, plus a running mana ledger per caster. Any new spell is presented with a full profile for author approval. If the chapter uses no spells, say so.
10. Present the plan to the author. WAIT for approval before /write.

### /write  (draft and self-review arrive together)

Deliver, in one message: (a) the full chapter in prose, with speaker brackets (draft only, never in files); (b) the measured word count; (c) a self-review.

1. Stay within the approved outline and Character Sheet; maintain the approved POV structure; end the chapter exactly as specified.
2. Write to the Web Novel Chapter Contract (rules 1–9). Do not copy the quieter register of Chapters 1–13. Ensure every line of dialogue has a clear speaker.
3. Use only spells approved in the /plan and matching their profiles exactly. No numbers or stat language in the prose.
4. Self-review, listing flags and never silently fixing: canon check against the bibles and Master Plan; magic against the Power System Bible and Spell Audit (each spell matches its profile; casters passed rank, affinity, capacity, circuit and knowledge checks; mana ledger and exhaustion stages held; no stat language leaked); Hook Check (opening hook, want / obstacle / turn, ending hook, payoff cadence, "filed it / set aside" used no more than once); measured craft checks (word count, three landing jokes, mid-chapter reversal); Rule 8 speaker clarity judged on the clean text; and any details added in the prose that were not in the approved /plan or bibles.
5. Wait for the author.

### /rewrite [specific instruction]

Rewrite only the flagged or requested sections. State what changed and why. A rewrite also carries the speaker brackets and a re-measured word count. Author approves before moving on.

### /approved

The author says "approved", optionally with a 1–5 "click next?" rating or named fixes. On approval, run the rest of the loop in one go, then show a change list the author can veto line by line:

1. **/summary:** write the Chapter Log entry directly into CHAPTER_LOG-1 (exact format below, plus measured word count, ending hook type and reader rating); strip all speaker brackets; append the final, approved prose to the current manuscript part file.
2. **/update-bible:** apply the chapter's canon directly to the relevant bibles, the Spell Registry (new spells, Known Spells changes) and the trackers. Anything that needs an author decision is listed as an open point, not applied.
3. Update the Current Project Status section (below) and the hook ledger.

Never run this on a chapter with an open rewrite. If the author's "approved" is ambiguous (it could answer a recommendation rather than the chapter), ask once for explicit chapter approval.

### /summary — Chapter Log entry format

```
CHAPTER ___ | ARC ___ | SAGA ___ | POV: ___
Word count (measured): ___ | Ending hook type: ___ | Reader rating (1–5): ___
What happened: [2–3 sentences — plot events, what changed by the end]
Character states changed: [who is different and how]
New canon introduced: [new named character, location, spell, fact — or NONE]
Closing beat / hook: [the last image or moment of the chapter]
```

No preamble, no explanation, just the entry. Manuscript: Ch. 1–13 live in claude/AETHON_MANUSCRIPT_VOL1_CH1-13.md (closed, do not edit); Ch. 14 onward go in claude/AETHON_MANUSCRIPT_VOL1_PART2.md.

### /suggest

Offer labelled creative suggestions. Never implement without approval.

### /update-bible (standalone use)

Propose canon additions. Wait for author approval before treating anything as canon; once approved, write them directly into the relevant bible documents. (Inside the normal loop this runs automatically at approval, with the veto-able change list.)

### /brief (optional)

Kept for when the author wants to see the chapter position alone before the full plan. It is otherwise folded into /plan.

### FULL CHAPTER LOOP

/plan → approve → /write (bracketed draft + word count + self-review) → approve, or /rewrite and re-approve → run /summary + /update-bible + tracker and status updates, show the change list → next chapter.

## HARD RULES — NEVER VIOLATE

- Never kill, rename, repower, or retcon any character without explicit author instruction.
- Never create a new Phasite. The number is fixed at 11.
- Never describe Dragonites physically before their Act 3/4 reveal (exact saga TBD).
- Never reveal The Unbound's name publicly before Act 2.
- Never give a character information they do not have yet.
- Never invent power system rules, ranks, Phasite powers or Ki titles without author approval. Spells are the exception: they may be used only if they pass the Spell Gate (claude/SPELL_REGISTRY.md) — no spell appears in prose without a registry profile, and no character casts above their rank.
- Never introduce a major new location or faction without flagging it first.
- Never move to /write without an approved /plan.
- Never plan a saga or arc without first reading all project files for full context.
- Never plan an arc shorter than 10 chapters, or a saga outside the 50–100 chapter range (70–80 preferred, exceptions possible with author approval — see Saga 5).
- Aldric does not know the word "Phasite" until the Saga 3 reassessment (locked 2026-09-26).
